# Where to stay

Seven adults: three couples and Tatiana on her own. Fernanda will be 29–32 weeks pregnant: real beds, a lift or ground floor, a private bath, a short flat walk or taxi. Places to stay checked 2026-10-23; parts of town researched 2026-10-21.

Book only on a place's own site or a big booking site, and never pay through a link someone sends you: fake 'confirm your booking' messages went out in autumn 2026 after a ryokan booking system (TEMAIRAZU) was hacked.

## Ground rules

- **Decide the New Year anchor first**: The three New Year nights (31 Dec–2 Jan) are the only scarce resource; everything else is still open. Most small ryokan load rooms six months out and their New Year plans sell out within weeks of posting in September. City hotels, apartment hotels and machiya do not have that cliff.
- **New Year surcharge**: Ryokan: +50–100% on the normal per-person rate for 31 Dec–2 Jan, usually as a mandatory New Year plan with toshikoshi soba on the 31st and an osechi breakfast on 1 Jan (verified at Hakone Ginyu: +¥14,300 per person). City hotels: +30–80% on 31 Dec. Apartment hotels: roughly 1.5–2× the base rate.
- **Seven of us: four rooms, never one**: Ryokan price per person. For three couples and Pedro's sister, ask for 3 rooms × 2 plus a single, or 2 rooms × 3 plus one room for a couple, the same plan and the same dinner sitting. Houses need three bedrooms and a sofa bed or a fourth room. One room for seven adults including a pregnant woman and two older parents is not advisable.
- **Beds, lifts, ground floors**: Hotels always have lifts. Modern large ryokan do; small historic ones and machiya have steep stairs and bedrooms upstairs. Ask for a Japanese-Western room (和洋室): beds plus a tatami area. In a machiya, put her on the ground floor and check the bedrooms are heated.
- **Tell them she is pregnant**: Say one guest is pregnant (妊娠中) and two are seniors: rooms near the lift, cooked courses instead of sashimi, low-sodium if needed, in-room dining if offered. Ryokan handle this routinely with three days' notice.
- **Refundable first**: Hold city nights on official sites or Booking.com with free cancellation. Book the New Year ryokan on the strictest-but-still-refundable plan you can find (Ikyu and Rakuten show cancellation terms). Machiya brands charge in full at booking and refund to about 21–22 days out. Never hold more than two options per night.
- **The right channel**: Hotels: official site or Booking.com. Ryokan: Ikyu (English, shows New Year plans), Rakuten Travel (about 10% cheaper than Booking), Jalan (Japanese only, most listings). A ryokan that shows sold out online often still has rooms by email for 2 rooms × 3. Machiya: the brand's own site.
- **Send the suitcases ahead**: Yamato takkyubin hotel-to-hotel is about ¥2,000–2,800 per case, next day; allow an extra day from 28 Dec to 4 Jan. Travel the onsen nights with a daypack.

## Kinds of place

### Hotel

**Label:** Hotel

**Blurb:** Three rooms, or an apartment hotel with connecting units. A front desk for luggage forwarding and taxis.

### Rental

**Label:** Whole house

**Blurb:** A machiya or apartment for six: a living room for the evenings, a kitchen and a washing machine. Usually beats three hotel rooms for three nights or more.

### Ryokan

**Label:** Ryokan

**Blurb:** Dinner and breakfast included, a bath in the room. Priced per person. The nights where the stay itself is the point.

## Tokyo · 20–24 Dec · 4 nights · hotel

### Which part of town

**Our pick:** Nihombashi / Kyobashi (Yaesu side of Tokyo Station). The Ginza subway line runs right through Nihombashi and Kyobashi to Asakusa, Ueno, Ginza, Omotesando and Shibuya with no changes, so every day on the plan is one train or a short flat walk. The Marunouchi lights are a 10-minute walk, and on 24 Dec the Hokuriku shinkansen leaves from Tokyo Station, 5–12 minutes away on foot. It is also calmer and a little cheaper than Ginza itself.

#### Nihombashi / Kyobashi (Yaesu side of Tokyo Station) (our pick)

**What it's like:** Old business and department-store district east of Tokyo Station. Flat, wide pavements, quiet after 9 pm, lots of modern mid-range hotels.

##### Good for us

- Flat walk to Tokyo Station for the 24 Dec train
- Ginza line straight to Asakusa, Ueno and Shibuya
- Big modern hotels with lifts and real beds
- Department-store food halls for easy dinners

##### Watch out

- Fewer late-night restaurants than Ginza
- Some subway exits are stairs only, so look for the lift exits

**To the plans:** Marunouchi lights 10 min walk; Ginza 10–15 min walk or 1 stop; Tsukiji outer market 10 min by taxi; Asakusa 15–20 min and Ueno about 10 min on the Ginza line; Omotesando/Shibuya about 20–25 min on the Ginza line; Tokyo Station 5–12 min walk; Haneda about 30 min by van.

**Station:** Nihombashi or Kyobashi (Ginza line), 2–5 min; Tokyo Station Yaesu exit 5–12 min

**Price:** middle

#### Ginza / Higashi-Ginza

**What it's like:** Tokyo's smart shopping street, busy by day and lively for dinner. Flat grid, very safe, lots of choice for meals.

##### Good for us

- Tsukiji outer market about 7–10 min on foot; Hama-rikyu about 15 min
- St. Luke's International Hospital (obstetrics, 24-hour emergency) is about 5–10 min by taxi
- Ginza line direct to Asakusa and Shibuya

##### Watch out

- Rooms cost more than in Nihombashi
- Tokyo Station is 15–20 min on foot, so take a taxi on 24 Dec

**To the plans:** Tsukiji 7–10 min walk; Marunouchi lights 10–15 min walk; Asakusa about 17 min and Shibuya about 16 min on the Ginza line; Tokyo Station 5–10 min by taxi; Haneda 25–35 min by van.

**Station:** Ginza or Higashi-Ginza, 2–5 min

**Price:** higher

#### Ueno

**What it's like:** Busy, down-to-earth area around Ueno Park and its museums, next to the Ameyoko street market.

##### Good for us

- Museums and Ueno Park on the doorstep for 22 Dec
- Asakusa is 5 min on the Ginza line
- The Hokuriku shinkansen to Nagano stops at Ueno

##### Watch out

- Far from Tsukiji, Ginza and the Marunouchi lights
- Ueno Station is huge and has lots of stairs; JR Ueno and Keisei Ueno are different stations
- Furthest of the three from Haneda (about 40–50 min by van)

**To the plans:** Museums 5–10 min walk; Asakusa 5 min by train; Ginza about 12 min and Shibuya about 30 min on the Ginza line; Tsukiji 25–30 min; Haneda 40–50 min by van.

**Station:** Ueno (JR, Ginza and Hibiya lines), 3–10 min

**Price:** lower

**When you book:**

- On 23 Dec the hotel front desk can send the big cases to Kyoto. Ask them to set the delivery for 30 Dec, and make sure someone at the Kyoto place can receive them. A self-check-in house may not be able to.
- Ask for rooms on a high floor near the lift, and check that the hotel has twin or double beds for every couple plus a single for Tatiana. Tokyo rooms are small, so look at the square metres.
- Check which subway exit has a lift (Kyobashi and Nihombashi both have some). Plan the Tsukiji and Haneda trips by taxi or van rather than by subway transfers.

## Yudanaka, Shibu Onsen and the snow monkeys · 24–27 Dec · 3 nights · ryokan

### Which part of town

**Our pick:** Shibu Onsen (lantern lane). Only guests of Shibu inns get the key to the nine public baths, and the lantern lane is right outside the door, so staying here is what makes the plan work. Shibu is just 5 minutes by taxi from Yudanaka station and close to the monkey-park trailhead. Choose a modern inn with a lift, Western beds and rooms with their own bath. Some of the oldest wooden inns have steep stairs.

#### Shibu Onsen (lantern lane) (our pick)

**What it's like:** A small, old hot-spring village of wooden inns along one narrow, gently sloping lane, where guests stroll in yukata between the nine little bathhouses.

##### Good for us

- Inn guests get the master key to all nine baths
- The lantern lane at night is right on the doorstep
- Closest real village to the monkey park (5–10 min by car to the trailhead)
- Small enough to walk around

##### Watch out

- The lane slopes and can be icy, so wear proper shoes rather than geta
- Many inns are old and full of stairs. Some, like Kanaguya, are beautiful but awkward for Fernanda
- Fewer rooms with beds, so ask for them specifically
- Walking from Yudanaka station takes about 30 min, so take a taxi (about ¥1,200) or the inn's shuttle

**To the plans:** Yudanaka station 5 min by taxi or bus; monkey park trailhead 5–10 min by car, then about 30 min on the forest path; Obuse 25–40 min by Nagaden from Yudanaka; Shiga Kogen buses from Yudanaka station.

**Station:** Yudanaka (Nagaden), 5 min by taxi / about 30 min on foot

**Price:** middle

#### Yudanaka Onsen (around the station)

**What it's like:** The bigger, more practical spa town where the Nagaden line ends. It's a bit dated, without Shibu's charm, but has the most choice.

##### Good for us

- Some inns are a 2–7 min walk from the station
- The most inns with Western beds; Hakura, for example, is set up for guests with limited mobility
- Buses to the monkey park and Shiga Kogen start here
- Shops and a few restaurants

##### Watch out

- No key to Shibu's nine baths (only the big Oyu bath is open to visitors, by day)
- Shibu's lantern lane is 15–25 min away on foot
- Less special for a Christmas Eve ryokan night

**To the plans:** Station 2–10 min walk; Shibu 5 min by taxi; monkey park about 20 min by bus or taxi plus the 30-min trail; Obuse 25–40 min by train; Shiga Kogen by bus from the station.

**Station:** Yudanaka (Nagaden), 2–10 min walk

**Price:** lower

#### Kanbayashi Onsen (near the monkey park)

**What it's like:** A handful of inns in the forest at the start of the monkey-park trail. Very quiet, with no real village.

##### Good for us

- Shortest trip to the monkeys, so you can go early before the crowds
- Some upmarket inns (Senjukaku, for example) run a shuttle from Yudanaka station
- The Shiga Kogen bus stops here

##### Watch out

- Very few inns, and they sell out first
- No shops or evening life, and dark at night
- No key to Shibu's nine baths
- The furthest from the hospital in Nakano

**To the plans:** Monkey park 5 min to the trailhead plus a 30-min forest path; Shibu 5 min by car; Yudanaka station about 10 min by bus or taxi.

**Station:** Yudanaka (Nagaden), about 10 min by bus or taxi

**Price:** higher

**When you book:**

- Ask each inn for: a room with Western beds, a lift (or a ground-floor room), and a private bath in the room or a private family bath you can book. Ask for an English menu for the Christmas Eve dinner. The best small inns fill up first for winter.
- The monkey trail is a 30-minute forest walk, and it is often icy in December. Rent boot spikes at the trailhead. Fernanda should decide with her doctor. Winter cars can't reach the park.
- Onsen are no longer officially off-limits in pregnancy (Japan dropped that warning in 2014), but avoid very hot water and long soaks. Shibu's public baths can be very hot, so the private bath is the safer choice.
- The nearest hospital with obstetrics is Hokushin General Hospital in Nakano, about 15 min by taxi. Nagano Red Cross Hospital in Nagano city is a regional maternity centre, about 45–50 min away.

## Takayama

### Which part of town

**Our pick:** Between the station and the old town (Kokubunji-dori / Hanakawa). The strip between the station's east exit and the Miyagawa river (around Kokubunji-dori and Hanakawa-cho) is flat, 5–10 minutes from both the trains and the old town. Big hotels there have lifts and real beds. You get the old town by morning without a long walk with bags, which matters when the streets are icy in December and January.

#### Between the station and the old town (Kokubunji-dori / Hanakawa) (our pick)

**What it's like:** An ordinary, flat town-centre grid with mid-range hotels, some with their own onsen baths.

##### Good for us

- Flat 5–10 min to the station and to Sanmachi
- Mid-range hotels with lifts, Western beds and private bath options
- Takayama Red Cross Hospital (the region's main emergency hospital, with obstetrics) is close by

##### Watch out

- Less atmosphere than staying in the old town
- Pavements can be icy in winter

**To the plans:** Station 5–10 min walk; Sanmachi and the morning markets 5–10 min; Takayama Jinya 10 min.

**Station:** Takayama (JR) and the Nohi bus centre, 5–10 min

**Price:** middle

#### Takayama Station (east exit)

**What it's like:** Chain and business hotels beside the station and bus centre. Practical, with not much charm.

##### Good for us

- Trains and buses (to Shirakawa-go and Kanazawa) right outside
- Cheapest choice of modern rooms
- Shortest walk on arrival and departure

##### Watch out

- Old town is 10–15 min on foot; in heavy snow that walk is no fun
- Few ryokan

**To the plans:** Sanmachi 10–15 min walk; station 0–5 min.

**Station:** Takayama (JR), 0–5 min

**Price:** lower

#### Old town (Sanmachi-suji)

**What it's like:** The dark-wood merchant streets with sake breweries, magical early in the morning before the tour groups.

- The New Year period is the exception to cheap winter rates, so book early. Snow lies on the ground from late December.
- Takayama Red Cross Hospital in Tenmancho is the main hospital for the whole Hida region.

## Hakone

### Which part of town

**Our pick:** Hakone-Yumoto. Hakone-Yumoto is only 15 minutes from Odawara, where the shinkansen from Kyoto stops, and it is the closest part of Hakone to Odawara's maternity hospital. Many ryokan here offer beds and in-room baths, and taxis wait at the station. The Romancecar goes direct to Shinjuku when you leave. The higher villages (Miyanoshita, Gora) are steep, and Sengokuhara can only be reached by bus.

#### Hakone-Yumoto (our pick)

**What it's like:** The busy gateway hot-spring town along two rivers, full of ryokan, souvenir shops and places to eat.

##### Good for us

- Shortest trip from Odawara (15 min) with cabin bags
- The most ryokan to choose from, at every price
- Taxi rank at the station, restaurants and shops
- Odawara Municipal Hospital (regional maternity centre) is about 20–25 min by taxi
- Direct Romancecar to Shinjuku

##### Watch out

- Busy with day-trippers
- Some inns are across the river or up steep driveways, so pick one with a shuttle or take a taxi
- Every sightseeing trip starts with the slow mountain train uphill

**To the plans:** Odawara 15 min by train; Gora and the Open-Air Museum 35–40 min by mountain train; Lake Ashi 40 min by bus; Shinjuku about 85–90 min by Romancecar.

**Station:** Hakone-Yumoto (Odakyu / Hakone Tozan), 0–15 min walk or short taxi

**Price:** middle

#### Miyanoshita

**What it's like:** A small, old-fashioned resort on the slope around the historic Fujiya Hotel, quieter than Yumoto.

##### Good for us

- Central for the loop through Gora and Lake Ashi
- Classic hotels with Western beds

##### Watch out

- Steep main street and station on a hillside
- Few restaurants
- Mountain train from Yumoto takes about 25 min, plus a change at Yumoto

**To the plans:** Yumoto 25 min by train; Gora 10–15 min; Lake Ashi 25 min by bus.

**Station:** Miyanoshita (Hakone Tozan), 5–10 min uphill walk

**Price:** higher

#### Gora

**What it's like:** The hub of the Hakone loop at the top of the mountain railway, with many upmarket ryokan with private open-air baths.

##### Good for us

- Open-Air Museum and the cable car to Owakudani start here
- Lots of rooms with a private onsen bath

##### Watch out

- Very steep streets around the station
- 45–60 min from Odawara, with crowded switchback trains
- Further from the hospital
- Owakudani's volcanic gas: pregnant women are advised not to go into the valley

**To the plans:** Open-Air Museum 5–10 min; cable car at the station; Yumoto 35–40 min; Odawara 50–60 min.

**Station:** Gora (Hakone Tozan), 2–10 min, often uphill

**Price:** higher

#### Sengokuhara

**What it's like:** A wide, flat highland valley with museums and resort hotels.

##### Good for us

- Flat walks around the hotels
- Spacious resort rooms with beds

##### Watch out

- No train: 30–40 min by bus from Yumoto, longer from Odawara
- Really needs a car or taxis
- Furthest from a hospital

**To the plans:** Yumoto 30–40 min by bus; Lake Ashi (Togendai) 10–15 min by bus; Gora 15–20 min by bus.

**Station:** No station; Hakone-Yumoto is 30–40 min by bus

**Price:** higher

**When you book:**

- Ask the ryokan for Western beds and an in-room bath. Hakone has many rooms with a private open-air bath, and they sell out early in January.
- Pregnant women are advised not to enter the Owakudani valley because of volcanic gas. Fernanda could ride the ropeway over it, or skip it and do Lake Ashi.
- Hakone's luggage service takes bags from Hakone-Yumoto station to partner inns in the morning, so you don't have to carry them up the mountain.
