## The route in words

**Style:** classic

**Theme:** Snow country first, then New Year in Kyoto

**Tagline:** Five moves, three days apart, Kyoto for New Year.

**Feel:** An easy start in Tokyo while the jet lag wears off and the first-timers get the classic days. On Christmas Eve the shinkansen takes us to Nagano: Zenko-ji on the way, then three nights at a hot-spring inn in Yudanaka. Shibu Onsen's lantern lane after dinner, the snow monkeys the next morning, and a day that splits: skiing or snow at Shiga Kogen for whoever wants it, Obuse's Hokusai paintings for Fernanda and the parents. Then three nights in Kanazawa: Kenroku-en with its snow ropes and the 21st Century Museum, a day out to Shirakawa-go's thatched village while the museums are shut, and the market, the teahouses and crab in its last week. On 30 December a two-hour train takes us to Kyoto for six nights in Higashiyama: the Okera fire and the temple bells on New Year's Eve, a slow New Year's Day, then museums and easy days out to Nara, Uji or Himeji. Then Lake Kawaguchi, with Fuji straight across the water and two dawns to catch it clear, and two last Tokyo days for the museums that were shut in December.

### Activities

- Tokyo: Tsukiji and the Hama-rikyu garden, Asakusa and Senso-ji, the Western Art museum in Ueno, Meiji Jingu and the Nezu Museum, Skytree at dusk, the Marunouchi lights
- Nagano: Zenko-ji on the way in, a Christmas Eve ryokan dinner, Shibu Onsen's lantern lane, the snow monkeys, a ski or snow day at Shiga Kogen, Obuse and the Hokusai museum
- Kanazawa: Kenroku-en with the yukitsuri snow ropes, the 21st Century Museum, Omicho market, Higashi Chaya and a gold-leaf workshop, the D.T. Suzuki Museum, kobako crab
- Shirakawa-go: the thatched village in snow, as a day trip from Kanazawa by van or bus
- Kyoto: Higashiyama and Kiyomizu-dera, Nishiki market, the Okera fire and the temple bells on New Year's Eve, Ohara or Nanzen-ji on New Year's Day, the National Museum and Kitano Tenmangu on 2 Jan
- Days out from Kyoto: Uji's Byodo-in and tea street, Nara's Great Buddha and deer, or Himeji Castle
- Kawaguchiko: Fuji straight across the lake, the Panoramic Ropeway, the Itchiku Kubota kimono museum, hoto noodles, Chureito Pagoda at dawn for whoever wants the climb
- Tokyo again: the Musée d'Orsay show, the Tokyo National Museum open late on Fri 8 Jan, a single act of kabuki, last shopping and a farewell dinner

**Why:** It keeps every place to the nights it can fill and the moves far apart: five moves, at least three days apart, with cabin bags while the big cases wait in Kyoto. The third nights in Nagano and Kanazawa each carry a named day out, the ski or Obuse day and Shirakawa-go, and the only long stay is Kyoto, where day trips and museums fill the days after New Year. New Year's Eve is the real event in Kyoto, close to two big hospitals, and four places are new to Pedro and Fernanda. Days can split: João and Carolina can do two big things a day while Fernanda and the parents do one.

**Suits:** Fernanda at 29–32 weeks (beds, private baths, three nights between moves, light afternoons after long moves, every night from 29 Dec to 3 Jan near a big hospital), Carlos and Nadir on a first visit, and João, Carolina and Tatiana, who get the classic sights plus snow, monkeys, a ski day and a Kyoto New Year.

**Trade:** Gives up Takayama's old town and morning market (the 'with Takayama' route keeps them), a quiet ryokan New Year in Gero, Okuhida's snow baths and Hiroshima. Kyoto costs the most at New Year, and Kyoto to the lake is about 4 h 15, the one move over four hours (Hakone instead is about 3 h).

**New Year's Eve:** New Year's Eve in Higashiyama. Kiyomizu-dera and the old lanes early, before the crowds, then a rest at the house. Anyone can ring a temple bell in daylight: in 2025 Myoman-ji let visitors ring 15:00–17:00 and Zuiko-ji 14:00–17:30 (times for 2026 to check). Dinner in, with toshikoshi soba, the noodles that close the year. From about 19:30 the Okera fire burns at Yasaka Shrine: we go early, carry the flame home on a twist of rope, and are back by 21:00, so Fernanda can be in bed by then if she likes. For the midnight bells, Kodai-ji gives numbered tickets from 22:00 to the first 108 groups, and Eikando and Chishaku-in also let people ring (2025 pattern, to check). Buses and the subway do not run all night, so we walk home through Higashiyama.

### Stay notes

**Tokyo:** Arrival plus three full days. Jet lag and the first-timers need an easy start; Mon 21 Dec is a museum closing day, so it becomes the market-and-gardens day. One night less loses a whole side of the city.

**Yudanaka:** Zenko-ji on the way in and the lantern lane on Christmas Eve, the snow monkeys the next morning with a slow afternoon, then a split day: skiing or snow at Shiga Kogen for whoever wants it, Obuse for Fernanda and the parents. The third night is for that day.

**Kanazawa:** Kenroku-en and the 21st Century Museum on arrival (it closes from 29 Dec), Shirakawa-go as a day trip on Monday 28 Dec, when the museums are shut, then the market, the teahouses and the samurai lanes on the 29th. The third night is for Shirakawa-go.

**Kyoto:** Arrival on 30 Dec, New Year's Eve, a slow New Year's Day on purpose, then three days of museums and day trips: Nara, Uji, Ohara or Himeji. Kyoto is a hub, so every day has a plan. One night less drops a day out.

**Kawaguchiko:** One full day for the ropeway and the lake, and two dawns for a clear Fuji. The move from Kyoto is about 4 h 15, the longest of the trip, so day one is only the shore and the hotel.

**Tokyo:2:** The museums that were closed in December, a single act of kabuki, shopping, and a night close to Haneda before the Saturday flight. One night is possible but leaves no slack if a train or the weather slips.

### Story

- We land in Tokyo on 20 December, while the Christmas lights are still up. Four easy days: markets, old gardens, museums, good food, and time for the jet lag to pass.
- On Christmas Eve a fast train takes us into the snowy hills of Nagano. We stop at Zenko-ji, an old temple, and sleep three nights at a hot-spring inn. Wild monkeys sit in a steaming pool in the snow, Shibu's old lane glows with lanterns, and on one day some of us ski while the others see Hokusai's paintings in Obuse.
- Then Kanazawa, on the Sea of Japan: a great garden, teahouses and winter crab. One day we drive into the mountains to Shirakawa-go, a village of thatched farmhouses in the snow, and come back for dinner. On 30 December a two-hour train takes us down to Kyoto.
- Kyoto is our New Year. We carry the sacred fire home from Yasaka Shrine, hear the temple bells ring in the year, and walk back through the old lanes. Then calm days for museums, Nara's deer and the tea town of Uji. Last comes Lake Kawaguchi, with Fuji straight across the water, two more days in Tokyo, and home.

**Intro:** Japan has 47 prefectures and hundreds of towns worth a week. We looked at the whole country for these exact 21 days: what is open, what is closed, which trains sell out, where the snow is, what is sensible at 29–32 weeks of pregnancy, and which places can sleep seven. Twenty-six regions made the long list. The New Year calendar and the pregnancy ruled most of them out before taste came into it. ### Facts

- **New Year closes most of Japan for a week**
  - Text: Between 29 Dec and 3 Jan museums, castles, markets and most independent restaurants close. Shrines, temples, onsen towns and department-store food halls (from 2 Jan) stay open. Trains are fullest on 27–30 Dec leaving Tokyo and 2–4 Jan coming back; JR East puts the peaks on 30 Dec and 3 Jan. The two calm travel days are 31 Dec and 1 Jan. And 9 Jan starts a three-day weekend, so the last night should be close to the airport. That gives any sensible trip the same shape: Tokyo first, the small towns before the crowds leave the cities on 29–30 Dec, New Year somewhere big enough to fill the closed days, and the last night within reach of Haneda. 31 Dec and 1 Jan are calm days to travel. In our route the still place is Kyoto: we arrive on 30 Dec on reserved seats, on a train running against the main flow (to check), and stay until 5 Jan.

### Route

**Intro:** Put those three together and the route almost draws itself. Here is why each stop is where it is, in this order.

#### Steps

- **Tokyo first, 4 nights**: Region: tokyo · The flights land here. The first days absorb the jet lag and give the first-timers the classic city while the Christmas lights are up. Monday the 21st is the museums' closing day, so that becomes the market-and-gardens day. On the 23rd the big cases go ahead to Kyoto, and we travel the next week with cabin bags.
- **Yudanaka and Shibu Onsen, 3 nights**: Region: yudanaka · About 80 minutes on the Hokuriku shinkansen to Nagano, a stop at Zenko-ji, then 45 minutes on the local express. Christmas Eve is a ryokan dinner and Shibu's lantern lane; the next morning, the snow monkeys. The third day splits: skiing or snow at Shiga Kogen for whoever wants it, Obuse's Hokusai paintings for Fernanda and the parents. The inns are easier to find before Christmas than at New Year.
- **Kanazawa, 3 nights**: Region: kanazawa · From Nagano the Kagayaki takes about 65 minutes on 27 Dec, a busy day, but a short ride, so the seats are booked the day they go on sale. Kenroku-en and the 21st Century Museum on arrival, Shirakawa-go on Monday 28 Dec, when the museums close, then Omicho market, Higashi Chaya and the samurai lanes. Laundry here, halfway through the cabin-bag week.
- **Shirakawa-go as a day trip, not as a base**: Region: shirakawago · A van with a driver, or the Nohi bus, takes about 75 minutes each way from Kanazawa, so we see the thatched village in snow without moving our bags. No farmhouse takes a group of seven over New Year, the houses have ladder-like stairs, and the light-ups are in late January.
- **Kyoto for New Year, 6 nights**: Region: kyoto · On 30 Dec the Hokuriku shinkansen to Tsuruga and the Thunderbird take about two hours, one change with lifts, in reserved Green Car seats. The train runs towards Kansai, against the crowds leaving the cities (our reading, to check). Then we stay put: New Year's Eve in Higashiyama, a slow New Year's Day, and three days of museums and day trips while the rest of Japan travels home. Kyoto University Hospital and the Japan Baptist Hospital are close by.
- **Kawaguchiko, 2 nights, Fuji across the lake**: Region: kawaguchiko · From the north shore of Lake Kawaguchi, Fuji stands straight across the water. Early January is its clearest month, and the lake is new for all seven of us. The move from Kyoto is the longest of the trip, about four and a quarter hours by shinkansen to Mishima and the bus, so the first afternoon is only the shore. Hakone is the alternative, about three hours from Kyoto.
- **Tokyo again, 2 nights**: Region: tokyo · Two hours on the Fuji Excursion train into Shinjuku. Then the museums that were closed in December (the Musée d'Orsay show, the Tokyo National Museum open late on Friday 8 Jan), a single act of kabuki, the farewell dinner, and a night near Haneda before a Saturday flight on a holiday weekend.
