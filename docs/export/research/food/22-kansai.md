# Kansai food research: Kyoto, Osaka, Kobe, Nara

Scope: places worth the family's time if they base in Kansai, especially over New Year (29 Dec 2026 – 4 Jan 2027).
Researched 18 Oct 2026. The brief's rules apply: Tabelog is the primary validation, and nothing here is invented. Where something could not be checked, it says **unverified**.

## How this was checked (read first)

- **Tabelog data** (score, review count, awards, seats, private rooms, holidays, hours) comes from each restaurant's Tabelog detail page, fetched on 18 Oct 2026. Award history comes from the "受賞・選出歴" block on the same page. Scores move every week, so treat them as a snapshot.
- **百名店 (Hyakumeiten) lists** were pulled from award.tabelog.com for Kyoto, Osaka, Hyogo and Nara, using the latest edition of each list: ramen WEST/OSAKA 2025, udon WEST 2024, soba WEST 2025, sushi WEST 2025, 日本料理 WEST 2025, tempura 2025, okonomiyaki 2025, tachinomi 2025, yoshoku WEST 2025, steak/teppanyaki WEST 2025, 喫茶店 2026, 和菓子・甘味処 WEST 2026, 中国料理 WEST 2026, 居酒屋 WEST 2026 and 食堂 2026.
- **Gaps:** the session's shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so few trusted-foodie traces could be checked. Mark Wiens' site (migrationology.com) was read directly. Kenji López-Alt, Little Meg Siu Meg, Fuchsia Dunlop, We Are Hungry Tourists and the Brazilian accounts were **not searched**. "None found" below means **not searched**, not that the person never went. A follow-up pass could close this gap.
- **New Year status:** a closure is listed only when Tabelog or the shop's own site states it. "Open daily" on Tabelog does **not** guarantee opening on 1–3 Jan. Phone the shop (or have the hotel phone) in mid-December.
- **Dates in 2026–27:** 24 Dec is a **Thursday**, 31 Dec is a Thursday, 1 Jan is a Friday, and 4 Jan is a Monday. Thursday-closing places (Daiichi Asahi, Okutan Kiyomizu, Yaekatsu) are shut on Christmas Eve.
- **Two classics are in doubt:** Tabelog marks **Honke Owariya Honten** and **Okutan Nanzenji** as 掲載保留 (listing on hold: closure or move unconfirmed). Do not plan around either without checking. Alternatives are given below.

# 1. KYOTO

### Kikunoi Honten (菊乃井 本店) — the splurge, with private rooms for six
- Higashiyama (Maruyama Park area) / Higashiyama Sta. · Ryōtei kaiseki · Tabelog budget band ¥20–29k / ¥30–39k (review-based), plus a 15% service charge
- Tabelog: **4.10** (611 reviews) · https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260301/26001346/ · Awards: **Tabelog Award Bronze** 2018, 2019 and 2021–2026; **日本料理 WEST 百名店** 2021, 2023, 2025
- Japanese expert backing: Kikunoi's own news page says the Honten kept **3 stars in the Japanese-language "ミシュランガイド京都・大阪2026"** (https://www.kikunoi.jp/). Chef Murata Yoshihiro is the best-known Kyoto ryōtei chef in Japan.
- Trusted-foodie trace: not searched
- Reservations: phone or the official site, well ahead. 120 seats in 10 tatami rooms; Tabelog lists private rooms for 4, 6 and 8+. **Group of 6: fits in a private room, the ideal format.**
- Pregnancy: courses include sashimi (mukōzuke) and sometimes raw shellfish or uni. Say so at booking ("妊娠中のため、生もの・生卵は火を通したものに変更をお願いします"); ryōtei handle this routinely.
- Dec–Jan: **closed for the year-end and New Year holidays** (Tabelog). Exact dates unverified. Book it for 20–24 Dec (24 Dec is fine for a ryōtei: Japanese Christmas Eve is a French/Italian date night) or for after the 4th.
- Why it's here: this is the one kaiseki where a party of six gets a private tatami room, a garden and the full seasonal arc without cramming onto a counter. It is the benchmark against which Kyoto measures itself.

**Alternative splurge — Kōdaiji Wakuden (高台寺 和久傳):** Tabelog **3.94** (269 reviews) · https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260301/26000535/ · **Tabelog Award** every year 2017–2026 (Silver 2020, otherwise Bronze); 日本料理 WEST 百名店 · 6 private rooms, closed Sundays, band ¥50–59k dinner / ¥30–39k lunch. NY dates unverified.

### Pontochō Masuda (先斗町 ますだ) — obanzai, the literary classic
- Pontochō / Kyoto-Kawaramachi or Gion-Shijō · Obanzai and Kyoto home cooking with sake · ¥8–10k dinner
- Tabelog: **3.51** (118 reviews) · https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260201/26000698/ · Awards: none
- Japanese expert backing: listed in the Japanese-language Michelin Guide as a ¥¥ obanzai house with two generations behind the counter, 20+ dishes on the counter, and okara, hijiki and nishin-nasu as signatures (https://guide.michelin.com/jp/ja/kyoto-region/kyoto/restaurant/pontocho-masuda).
- Trusted-foodie trace: not searched
- Reservations: phone. 35 seats with **private rooms for 4, 6, 8 and 10–20**. Counter bookings are for 1–3 people only. **Group of 6: book a private room.**
- Pregnancy: obanzai is mostly simmered vegetables, so it's ideal. Skip kizushi (lightly vinegared raw mackerel) if she's being careful; everything simmered or grilled is fine.
- Dec–Jan: closed Sundays (27 Dec). NY dates unverified.
- Why it's here: the platters on the counter *are* the menu. This is Kyoto domestic cooking at its most honest, in a Pontochō house that hasn't turned into a theme restaurant.

- Japanese expert backing: none beyond Tabelog verified (web-search cap)
- Trusted-foodie trace: not searched

- Why it's here: a backup to Masuda with a room that fits six exactly, and the same Kyoto obanzai repertoire.

### Akagakiya (赤垣屋) — the old Kyoto izakaya
- Kawabata-Nijō / Jingū-Marutamachi or Sanjō · Izakaya with oden, simmered dishes and sake · ¥3–4k
- Tabelog: **3.71** (983 reviews) · https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260302/26001072/ · Awards: **居酒屋 百名店** 2021 and 2022, **居酒屋 WEST 百名店** 2024, 2025 and 2026

- Trusted-foodie trace: not searched

- Dec–Jan: closed Sundays. NY dates unverified.
- Why it's here: wood, lamplight, a long counter and serious sake. It's the Kyoto izakaya locals bring visitors to when they want to show them the real thing.

### Nanzenji Junsei (南禅寺 順正) — yudōfu for a group
- Nanzenji / Keage Sta. · Yudōfu (tofu hot pot) and kaiseki · ¥4–6k
- Tabelog: **3.50** (834 reviews) · https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260301/26001013/ · Awards: none
- Japanese expert backing: none verified beyond Tabelog
- Trusted-foodie trace: not searched

- **Alternative — Okutan Kiyomizu (奥丹 清水):** Tabelog **3.47** (435 reviews) · https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260301/26001625/ · the Okutan house (founded 1635, per Japanese guides dime.jp and aumo.jp), which takes reservations and has private rooms. **Closed Thursdays (including 24 and 31 Dec).**

### Honke Owariya (本家尾張屋 本店) — soba, status uncertain
- Kurumaya-chō, Nijō / Karasuma-Oike · Soba and sweets (soba-mochi) · ~¥1–3k
- Tabelog: **3.49** (640 reviews) · https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260202/26000657/ · Awards: none. **The listing is marked 掲載保留** (status unconfirmed: closure or move undetermined).
- Japanese expert backing: Kyoto's oldest-lineage soba house (founded in the Muromachi period, per All About / LiveJapan). It has supplied the Imperial Household and temples.
- Trusted-foodie trace: not searched
- Reservations: none. It had 92 seats, so six fit easily.
- Pregnancy: avoid tsukimi (raw egg) toppings.
- Dec–Jan: the Tabelog record says "Closed on January 1 and 2 only". Historically **31 Dec is its big toshikoshi-soba day**. **Verify that the honten is trading at all.** If it isn't, use Matsuba (below) or Nikora.
- Why it's here: the name every Kyoto soba conversation starts with. Its 百名店-level rivals are newer artisan shops.

### Sōhonke Nishin Soba Matsuba (総本家にしんそば 松葉 本店) — the New Year's Eve soba
- Shijō-Ōhashi, next to Minamiza / Gion-Shijō (37 m) · Nishin (herring) soba · ~¥1.5–2.5k
- Tabelog: **3.48** (855 reviews) · https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260301/26001629/ · Awards: none
- Japanese expert backing: Kyoto Prefecture's food page credits Matsuba's second-generation owner with creating nishin soba (shop founded 1861) (https://www.pref.kyoto.jp/co-kyoto/gourmet/221221.html). Serai magazine also covers it.
- Trusted-foodie trace: not searched
- Reservations: none. Shared tables; six may need to split 4+2 at peak.
- Pregnancy: fine (sweet-simmered herring, hot broth).
- Dec–Jan: open daily per Tabelog. Nishin soba is Kyoto's toshikoshi soba, so **expect long queues on 31 Dec**; go 29–30 Dec or at opening on the 31st. Exact NY hours unverified.
- Why it's here: if the family wants one Kyoto year-end ritual, this is it: herring soba by the river on the last days of the year.

### Izuu (いづう) — Kyoto saba-zushi
- Gion / Gion-Shijō · Saba sugata-zushi and Kyoto pressed sushi · ~¥2–3k
- Tabelog: **3.69** (1,177 reviews) · https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260301/26000585/ · Awards: none (sushi 百名店 lists favour nigiri counters)
- Japanese expert backing: Keihan Railway's "Kyoto meibutsu" pages and tabi-mag date it to 1781 (Tenmei 1) (https://www.keihan.co.jp/navi/famous/famous110.html)
- Trusted-foodie trace: not searched
- Reservations: dining room none; **takeout saba-zushi can be reserved by phone from 8 a.m.** 16 seats (four 4-tops); **six split 4+2, or simply take sushi back to the hotel.**
- Pregnancy: the mackerel is salted and vinegared but not cooked, so it's a judgement call. Japanese OBs generally allow it. Mackerel is low-mercury. Safer orders: **anago (cooked eel) sushi or inari.**
- Dec–Jan: closed Tuesdays (29 Dec); open on public holidays. Saba-zushi is a traditional Kyoto New Year and festival food. NY dates unverified; pre-order for 30–31 Dec.
- Why it's here: the definitive version of Kyoto's festival food, with fat mackerel on kombu-wrapped rice. Its department-store branches (JR Isetan 3.57, Daimaru 3.51) are a good plan B.

### Nishiki Market (錦市場): what locals respect vs the tourist stalls
Honest verdict: since about 2015 Nishiki has filled up with walk-and-eat skewer stalls (octopus with a quail egg, wagyu sticks, oysters, uni-on-a-stick). Many street signs now ask people not to eat while walking. The shops Kyoto people still use are the old specialists:
- **Miki Keiran (三木鶏卵)** — dashimaki tamago · Tabelog **3.45** (296 reviews) · https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260201/26004870/ · 9:00–17:00, takeaway. Fully cooked egg, so it's pregnancy-safe.
- **Fuka Nishiki (麩嘉 錦店)** — fu-manjū (wheat-gluten dumplings wrapped in sasa leaves) · Tabelog **3.69** (481 reviews) · https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260201/26007146/ · **和菓子・甘味処 WEST 百名店** 2023 and 2026 · closed Mondays. This is the 百名店 inside Nishiki.
- **Uchida Tsukemono (打田漬物 錦小路店)** — Tabelog 3.15 (116 reviews) · https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260202/26015723/ · **closed only on 1 Jan**
- **Aritsugu** — knives (see Shops)
- **Fumiya (冨美家)** — just off Nishiki; Kyoto-style nabeyaki udon, the "Fumiya nabe" · Tabelog **3.53** (347 reviews) · https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260201/26017337/ · counter and small tables, so six must split
- **Skip:** the skewer and tasting stalls at the Teramachi end, raw-oyster and uni stands (off-limits for her anyway), and anything with an English-only menu and a QR-code queue.
- **Year-end market:** Nishiki is the city's New Year shopping street. 28–30 Dec is the peak for kazunoko, kuromame, white miso, mochi and kamaboko, and it is crushingly busy. Most shops close for roughly 1–3/4 Jan. Shop-by-shop dates are unverified; the brief's calendar applies. Go on the morning of 28 or 29 Dec to see it at its most Kyoto, and expect shoulder-to-shoulder crowds.

### Kyoto year-end and New Year foods
- **年越しそば (toshikoshi soba):** in Kyoto this means **nishin soba**, so go to **Matsuba** (above). Honke Owariya is the traditional alternative if it is trading.
- **Kyoto ozōni:** **white-miso broth with round mochi and kashira-imo**. It's served at home on 1–3 Jan, so few restaurants offer it. No restaurant serving it over 1–3 Jan was verified. Best routes: (a) ask the ryokan or hotel whether New Year breakfast includes 白味噌雑煮; (b) buy saikyō white miso, maru-mochi and kashira-imo at Nishiki on 29–30 Dec if the accommodation has a kitchen.
- **Saba-zushi** (Izuu) and **osechi** boxes: Kyoto ryōtei and department stores (Takashimaya, Daimaru) sell osechi by pre-order, typically closing in early to mid December. The deadline is unverified, so order by late November if wanted.

# 2. OSAKA

### Kuromon Market (黒門市場): the honest verdict
**Yes, it has gone tourist.** Prices on grilled wagyu, crab legs, uni and scallops are aimed at inbound visitors, and the eat-on-the-spot stalls have pushed out much of the old wholesale-to-restaurant trade. Mark Wiens visited in 2015 (https://migrationology.com/kuromon-ichiba-market-osaka/), before the inbound boom. The one Tabelog-solid sit-down nearby is **Kuromon Miyoshi** (an okonomiyaki/teppan izakaya, Tabelog 3.56, 145 reviews, https://tabelog.com/osaka/A2701/A270202/27000399/; closed at year-end and New Year). The stalls themselves have no meaningful Tabelog standing ("Kuromon Yatai Mura" is 3.00).

**Where locals go instead:** **Kizu Market (木津卸売市場)** in Daikokuchō/Namba-naka, a working wholesale market open to the public in the early morning. It's best before 9 a.m. and closed Sundays/holidays. Year-end trading is busy, but its exact dates are unverified. Its in-market eateries on Tabelog are weak (Ten Hana Kizu Ichiba Honten, tempura, **3.09**, https://tabelog.com/osaka/A2701/A270206/27150290/), so go to look and shop, not for a meal. **Osaka's department-store food halls** (Hanshin Umeda, Hankyu Umeda, Takashimaya Namba) are where Osaka families actually buy year-end osechi ingredients. This is general knowledge and was not verified this session.

# 5. SHOPS

### Knives
- **Aritsugu (有次), Nishiki Market, Kyoto**: the Kyoto knife house that claims descent from the swordsmith 藤原有次 (per its site; the commonly cited 1560 founding date is unverified). It engraves names in kanji, and buying here is a Kyoto ritual. Official site: https://www.aritsugu.jp/ (it posts monthly holiday notices; check in December for year-end dates). Go early: the shop counter is narrow and the queue builds by 11:00.
- **Jikkō (實光 / 実光刃物), Sakai**: founded 1900 (明治33年) in Sakai, per its site (https://www.jikko.jp/). It sells chef-grade Sakai knives, offers sharpening and also has Ura-Namba and Nishiki-Teramachi shops (opening announcements on its site). Phone enquiries Mon–Sat. NY dates unverified.
- **Sakai tour (self-guided, half a day from Namba on the Nankai line):** visit the **Sakai Traditional Crafts Museum (堺伝統産業会館)** for the knife-making exhibits and multi-maker shop, then the Jikkō head shop. Also consider Sakai Ichimonji Mitsuhide (堺一文字光秀), known for English-speaking sales, though it wasn't verified this session. No Japanese-led guided knife-forge tour could be verified (web-search cap), so don't book an unknown "forge experience" from an English listing without checking it. Note that most Sakai workshops close from about 29 Dec to 4–5 Jan, so do Sakai before the 28th.
- **Dōguyasuji** (Osaka): see the Osaka section.

### Kyoto kitchenware
- **Tsujiwa Kanaami (辻和金網)**, Sakaimachi-Takoyakushi: hand-woven copper and stainless wire tofu scoops, tea strainers and grill nets. Site: https://www.tsujiwa.com/. NY dates unverified. Small shop, so go in pairs.

### Tea
- **Ippodo Honten (一保堂茶舗 京都本店)**: Tabelog 3.12 (shop), https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260202/26031878/. Buy gyokuro, sencha, matcha and hōjicha; the tea room is reviewed above.
- **Uji**: **Nakamura Tōkichi** (above). For buying tea, Uji's riverside lanes have many family merchants; no specific shop was verified.

### Sake (Fushimi)
- **Gekkeikan Ōkura Sake Museum (月桂冠大倉記念館)**: Tabelog 3.47 (126 reviews), https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260601/26007339/. Museum with tasting; closed Tuesdays (29 Dec). NY unverified.
- **Fushimi Yume Hyakushu (伏見夢百衆)**: a sake bar and café in a historic Fushimi building (said to be Gekkeikan's former head office; unverified), tasting flights from many Fushimi breweries. Tabelog 3.44 (133 reviews), https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260601/26004759/. **Closed Mondays and over the year-end and New Year holidays.**
- **Torisei Honten (鳥せい 本店)**: Yamamoto Honke brewery's own izakaya in a sake storehouse. Tabelog 3.41 (1,073 reviews), https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260601/26001213/. 250 seats with **rooms for 6+**, the best lunch for six in Fushimi. Closed Mondays.
- Pregnancy: no sake for her. **Fresh amazake made from rice kōji is alcohol-free, but sake-kasu amazake is not.** Ask "麹の甘酒ですか?" (Is this kōji amazake?)

### Tsukemono
- **Uchida Tsukemono, Nishiki (京つけもの 打田漬物)**: Tabelog 3.15, https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260202/26015723/. **Closed only on 1 Jan.**
- **Murakami-ju Honten (村上重本店)**, Nishikiya-machi: founded 1832 (unverified), known for senmai-zuke. Tabelog 3.16 (168 reviews), https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260201/26007678/. **Closed 1–3 Jan.**
- Kyoto's winter pickle is **senmai-zuke** (thin-sliced Shōgoin turnip), in season exactly in December, so buy it for the family. Pickles are fine in pregnancy apart from salt; Nara-zuke is the exception (alcohol).

### Tofu and yuba
- **Saga Tōfu Morika (嵯峨豆腐 森嘉)**, Saga-Arashiyama: Tabelog 3.44 (225 reviews), https://tabelog.com/kyoto/A2601/A260403/26002454/. Takeaway with 8 bench seats; closed Wednesdays. This is the tofu Kyoto's yudōfu restaurants use. Buy and eat it as it is, cold, on the bench.

# 6. TOURS AND EXPERIENCES

**Search limit:** the shared web-search budget was used up before the tour operators could be vetted. Below is what can be said honestly; book nothing here without a check.

- **Fushimi sake (self-run, recommended):** Gekkeikan Ōkura Museum, then a tasting flight at Fushimi Yume Hyakushu (not after 28 Dec, when it's closed), then lunch at Torisei. Everything is Japanese-run and walkable from Chūshojima. No guide is needed.
- **Uji tea:** Byōdō-in, then Nakamura Tōkichi, then the tea lanes. For a tea-farm visit, **Obubu Tea Farms** in Wazuka (Japanese-founded, runs English farm tours) is the name usually cited. Its winter and New Year schedule is **unverified**, and tea fields are dormant in December.
- **Cooking with a kappō chef:** no Japanese-led class could be verified. The better substitute is to **book the counter or chef's table at Kigawa (Osaka) or Menami (Kyoto)** and watch. If a cooking class is wanted, look for one run by a working Kyoto chef or obanzai home cook, held in Japanese with interpretation, and ask whether the dashi is made from scratch on the day.
- **Knife sharpening:** Aritsugu and Jikkō both sharpen knives. Ask about having a new knife's edge set and its name engraved on the spot.
- **Traps to skip:**
  - Nishiki and Kuromon skewer and uni stalls
  - "Maiko dinner" packages in Gion
  - "Kobe beef" teppan counters steps from Sannomiya Station with English-only set menus
  - Kushikatsu Daruma's flagship (go to Yaekatsu)
  - Dōtonbori-riverfront takoyaki queues (go to Yamachan, Aizuya or Hanadako)
  - Arashiyama "yudōfu set" lunches on the main street (go to Junsei, Okutan Kiyomizu or Morika)
  - Uji matcha-parfait chains on the approach to Byōdō-in

## Pregnancy cheat-sheet for Kansai
- **Avoid or modify:** raw egg (tsukimi udon, the sukiyaki dipping egg, nikusui's "tama", soft oyakodon); rare beef (Aragawa or any teppan; order ウェルダン); raw shellfish at Tōyō and Nishiki; Nara-zuke and sake-kasu amazake (alcohol).
- **Judgement calls:** saba-zushi and kakinoha-zushi (cured but raw), and the mukōzuke in kaiseki courses.
- **Safe stars:** obanzai, yudōfu, udon and soba, okonomiyaki, takoyaki, kushikatsu, tempura, yōshoku, wagashi, dashimaki tamago, butaman, shumai.
