# Critic personas: the Brazilian-leaning accounts

Research date: 18 Oct 2026. Covers @laerciomunechika, @ocritico.antigourmet, @eduardo.takemi, @sucrilhos and @we.are.hungry.tourists.

**Method and limits.** Instagram needs a login, so I did not read any feeds. I used three kinds of evidence:
- **Instagram's own public link-preview metadata** (`og:title` / `og:description`, plus the bio and a few recent captions when the logged-out page served them). These are cited as "IG public profile page, fetched 18 Oct 2026".
- **Threads public profile metadata.**
- **Web search and fetches** of press articles, blogs and company sites.

The web-search budget for this session ran out before a last pass on Brazilian print media (Folha, Veja Comer & Beber, Paladar) for Danilo Nakamura. More could turn up there.

**The surprises:**
- Only two of the five are public food voices in Brazil: Ian Oliver (@ocritico.antigourmet) and Danilo Nakamura (@sucrilhos).
- @eduardo.takemi and @laerciomunechika are private-citizen foodies. Both appear to be São Paulo lawyers, and both have Nikkei surnames.
- @we.are.hungry.tourists is not Brazilian at all. It is the tour account of The Hungry Tourist (David "Dudi" Califa, Tel Aviv-born, NY-listed company).

## 1. @ocritico.antigourmet: Ian Oliver, "O Crítico Antigourmet"

### Identity
- **Real name: Ian Oliver.** The Underchef interview is titled "Underchef entrevista: Ian Oliver 'o crítico antigourmet'" and says he is "@ocritico.antigourmet como é conhecido" ([BR Spices / Underchef](https://brspices.com.br/video/underchef-entrevista-ian-oliver-o-critico-antigourmet/); [YouTube: "Ian Oliver: É O MESMO QUE COMPARAR TOM JOBIM COM TATI QUEBRA-BARRACO"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YB82TriCo0Q)). Estadão Paladar also names him as the critic behind the profile ([Terra/Paladar, 2 Jan 2023](https://www.terra.com.br/vida-e-estilo/degusta/gastronomia-afetiva-vive-polemica-entre-a-farsa-e-a-memoria-da-avo,6d7edbbe120f2476edf2c14c408abf8b5zvu0c3r.html)).
- **What he does:** He writes as a food critic for O Globo. His columns run as "Especial para O GLOBO – São Paulo", for example "As 8 maiores mentiras da gastronomia paulistana", 20 Feb 2026 ([IPO News reprint](https://iponews.com.br/noticias.asp?idnoticia=12630)). He also teaches sensory-analysis courses, such as an olive-oil fraud and defects course with Glenda Haas in São Paulo on 1 Oct 2026 ([Gastronominho, 30 Sep 2026](https://gastronominho.com.br/comidas/2026/09/curso-sensorial-azeite-lagar-h-sao-paulo/)), and sells consulting.
- **Training:** Letras (USP), a postgraduate degree in Gastronomia Autoral (PUCRS), Le Cordon Bleu gastronomy studies, and WSET Level 3 (same Gastronominho piece). His IG bio says: "Crítico de gastronomia de O Globo. Gastronomia (@lecordonbleu), WSET 3. Primeiro a comida, depois a moral." (IG public profile page, fetched 18 Oct 2026). The motto is Brecht's line from the Threepenny Opera, "Erst kommt das Fressen, dann kommt die Moral".
- **Audience:** 243K Instagram followers and 389 posts (IG `og:description`, 18 Oct 2026). 40.7K on [Threads](https://www.threads.com/@ocritico.antigourmet).
- **Detractors:** One regional blog questioned whether his credentials were proven ([Blog do BG](https://www.blogdobg.com.br/artigo-uma-critica-ao-critico-do-camaroes-por-marcus-aragao/)). Worth knowing he is polarising.

### Palate and spirit
- **Myth-buster first.** The O Globo "8 lies" column (20 Feb 2026) is his manifesto:
  - "A pizza de São Paulo é a melhor do mundo." False. The average is heavy, badly fermented dough with too much cheese, and he says the average is better "em várias cidades da Itália, do Japão".
  - "Restaurante cheio é sinônimo de qualidade." False. A full room reflects marketing, location and Instagram appeal.
  - "Artesanal" or "orgânico" is "o novo 'gourmet'". It is a word that masks amateur production.
  - "Se tem estrela, então é bom." False. Michelin rewards a theatrical model, inflates prices and egos, and guides err.
  - "O ponto da casa" is a pre-emptive excuse. Doneness is an objective temperature, and the kitchen should own it.
  - Gloves are not hygiene. Glove wearers wash their hands less.
  - Truffle oil is a petroleum-derived aroma (2,4-dithiapentane), "cheap cologne posing as Chanel Nº 5".
  - House-made is not automatically better. Good dried pasta beats fresh for heavy sauces, and Tabasco beats most house pepper sauces.
- **Anti-"gastronomia afetiva".** "Culinária é uma prática do mundo privado… A gastronomia é diferente. É a comida pública." He also says "Muitas vezes o rótulo de culinária afetiva é usado para compensar erros técnicos grosseiros." The profile's line "o afeto é a morte da gastronomia" is attributed to it ([Paladar via Terra, 2 Jan 2023](https://www.terra.com.br/vida-e-estilo/degusta/gastronomia-afetiva-vive-polemica-entre-a-farsa-e-a-memoria-da-avo,6d7edbbe120f2476edf2c14c408abf8b5zvu0c3r.html)).
- **How he judges.** Technique, consistency and product integrity (fermentation, cooking temperature, fraud-free oils) come first. Hype, labels, stars and the room's buzz come nowhere. He holds a high-culture/low-culture hierarchy: the interview title compares Tom Jobim to Tati Quebra-Barraco. He is sceptical of fine-dining theatre but not populist. Bad technique in a cheap place is still bad.
- **Tone.** Didactic, polemical and listicle-friendly ("mitos", "mentiras"). He corrects received wisdom with a technical fact.
- **He would never recommend** a place sold on "experiência", "afeto", "artesanal" or truffle oil, the star alone, or a queue alone.

### Japan
- **No Japan trip or specific Japanese restaurant found in public sources.** The only Japan reference is that Japanese cities have a satisfactory average pizza (O Globo, 20 Feb 2026, above).
- Instagram's logged-out page for @eduardo.takemi shows an O Crítico Antigourmet photo dated 13 Dec 2024 (post `DDiN8V0SlBr`). It is probably a tag or collab, which links the two accounts socially. Content not verified.

### How to channel Ian Oliver
Ask of every candidate:
- What exactly is the technique, and is it executed correctly? Is the rice seasoned and at body temperature, is the tempura batter thin and dry, is the yakitori cooked to a stated doneness?
- Is the evidence about the food or about the room, the star or the queue?
- Is any premium ingredient real? Truffle, "wagyu" grade claims, "artisanal" labels, olive oil.
- Does the Tabelog score come with reviews that describe execution, or only atmosphere?

**Red flags (veto):**
- Truffle-oil anything.
- "Fusion" or "afetiva" storytelling covering weak cooking.
- Places famous mainly for Instagram-ability or queues.
- A Michelin star cited as the only argument.
- Gloved sushi chefs as a "hygiene" selling point.
- Gourmet-ized street food (gold leaf, foie gras on ramen) at multiples of the classic's price.

**He would favour** the technically canonical specialist, such as a shop that has done one thing for decades, over a fashionable omakase. In his voice: "Primeiro a comida, depois a moral."

### Confidence
**High** on identity and stance, from multiple independent press sources. Unverified: any Japan travel, any specific Japanese recommendations, and his current O Globo column frequency (globo.com is blocked to my fetcher).

### Palate and spirit
- **Drinker first.** He is a cocktail creator and sake student. ELLE Brasil credits him with a Togarashi Sour (shochu, lemon, egg white, togarashi) and "The Other Woman" (gin, umeshu, sherry, umeboshi) at Hirá Ramen Izakaya. It also says he and his wife Valéria Cedano Hirata are "sérios estudiosos do fermentado de arroz japonês" ([ELLE Brasil, 22 Nov 2022](https://elle.com.br/lifestyle/bares-apostam-no-japonismo-na-taca)).
- **Contrarian but fair.** He defends places foodies sneer at when they deliver value. Le Jazz is "mais do que aceitável, é um bom negócio – para eles e para nós" ([LeFigado, 15 Feb 2023](https://lefigadocom.wordpress.com/2023/02/15/sobre-o-le-jazz/)). He judges a restaurant on its own terms: Noma is a runway, "não está mesmo lá para agradar, mas para propor", and "Ninguém te obriga a gostar do Noma" ([LeFigado, 28 Feb 2023](https://lefigadocom.wordpress.com/2023/02/28/o-noma-e-quem-decide-o-que-e-bom-e-o-que-e-ruim/)).
- **Hype-aware.** "quem decide o que é bom e o que é ruim é a indústria." On expensive tasting menus: "É quase obrigatório achar deslumbrante." (same Noma post).
- **Pro-restaurant on business rules.** He defends corkage, deposits and no-show fees: restaurants "são todos empresas com fins lucrativos" ([LeFigado, "Death and Taxes", 19 May 2022](https://lefigadocom.wordpress.com/2022/05/19/death-and-taxes/)). He would not flinch at Japanese cancellation policies.
- **What he loves:** Holes-in-the-wall with no pretension. His 2009 Liberdade review praised Kidoairaku precisely because it "não tem sushi, nem carta de vinhos, trufas ou taças de cristal" and cost under R$60 with drinks. Bistros that nail the classics (steak au poivre, frites). "Botecos de boa comida". Moody, well-lit date places with a real wine list (Chou).
- **Critic's creed:** "o crítico é um foodie que sabe perceber e fazer os outros perceberem". Explain what a dish represents, not just "gostei / não gostei" (LeFigado, 4 Nov 2021).
- **Tone:** Colloquial, funny, self-deprecating and sarcastic. Tics include "Eu juro.", "Sejamos francos", "convenhamos", "De nada.", "Vitória." He uses football metaphors.

### How to channel Danilo Nakamura
Ask of every candidate:
- What is the drink programme? Sake list depth, shochu, a serious highball, whether there is a sake specialist.
- Does the place know what it is trying to be, and does it deliver that at a fair price?
- Is this the neighbourhood regulars' izakaya or the one built for foreigners?
- Would I come back on a random Tuesday, or is it a one-time "experience"?
- What does the dish represent? Region, season, the cook's story.

**Red flags:**
- Pretension without delivery: crystal glasses, truffles, and a sushi menu at a place that should be a kakuni izakaya.
- Tourist-oriented "omakase experiences" that exist to be photographed.
- A weak or token drinks list.
- Foodie-bait newness.

**He is relaxed about** strict booking rules, cancellation fees and cover charges (otōshi). Those are the house's right.

**His ideal Japan pick** is a counter-seat izakaya or sake bar where you can order kakuni, umaki, house pickles and a flight of junmai from a knowledgeable master, plus one argued-for "this is overrated, but here's why you'll still enjoy it" take.

### Confidence
**High** on identity and voice, from his own blog plus multiple press pieces. Unverified: Japan trips, recent IG content, and whether the IMDb credits are the same person.

### How to channel Eduardo Takemi
Ask of every candidate:
- Would I come back three or four times? Does the flavour justify the hype?
- What does it pair with? A sake or wine pairing, or a good glass list.
- Is it where locals eat lunch, or a checklist stop?

### How to channel The Hungry Tourist
Ask of every candidate:
- Is this the best-in-class counter in its genre (sushi, tempura, yakiniku, yakitori, kaiseki), and can we actually get in? Through which channel or concierge?
- Is it a place worth returning to, with a chef-host relationship and a signature "moment" bite (the wagyu-fat rice, the uni-shiso tempura)?
- Does the night feel like a celebration with the table, the people as much as the plate?

**Red flags:**
- Average or "fine but forgettable" places.
- Anything you could book on a whim.
- Restaurants that won't accommodate a small group.
- Big, impersonal rooms.

**Warning for this family:**
