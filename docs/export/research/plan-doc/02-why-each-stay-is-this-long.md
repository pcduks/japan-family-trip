# Why each stay is this long

The number of nights in each place follows from three things. First, how much is genuinely worth doing there. Second, how much the group does in a day, which is the pace you agree on. Third, what the calendar allows. Agree the pace first, and the nights follow from it.

## How the nights are decided

Everything worth doing in a place is sorted into two sizes:

- **Main outing:** half a day, about 3–4 hours, and the reason you go there. Examples: Kenroku-en with the castle park, or the Arashiyama bamboo grove with Tenryū-ji.
- **Short visit:** 1–2 hours. Examples: a museum, a market, a teahouse or a viewpoint.

Evenings are not counted. Dinner, an izakaya or the light displays happen at every pace.

The pace sets how many of these fit in a day:

| Pace | A full day | A travel day | Rest | What it feels like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Gentle** | 1 main outing in the morning, then rest | Travel only | An empty day every 4–5 days | You see less, but nobody is ever tired |
| **Balanced** | 1 main outing, a 2–4pm rest, then 1 short visit | 1 short visit after arriving | An easy day every 4–5 days | Full days with a real break in the middle |
| **Full** | 2 main outings, or 1 main outing and 2 short visits | 1 main outing | None planned | A classic sightseeing trip; tiring by week two |

**The rule: nights = the arrival day + the full days needed to fit the main outings at your pace + any rest day.** Short visits fill the afternoons. The calendar then shifts things, because of Monday museum closures, the New Year closures and the peak travel days.

**Recommendation: Balanced for the group, with Gentle available every day for her and the parents.** On any day the group can split. The early crew does the dawn outing or the second visit, and the others rest and join for lunch or dinner. Full pace is not a good fit at 29–32 weeks.

### Why each length, and what changes with one night more or less

- **Tokyo (4 + 2).** The first stay absorbs jet lag and gives the four first-timers the three classic days. One night less loses a whole side of the city. One night more is a rest day or a day trip to Mt Takao. The January stay exists because the Orsay show and the National Museum only fit then.
- **Kanazawa (4).** This is above the usual 2–3 days, deliberately. It is the week's rest bank before New Year, it has the museums you said you value, and everything must be done by 27 Dec because museums close on Mon 28. A rental house with a washing machine makes it the mid-trip reset. With one night less, the day becomes a long one or the Museum of Art and Kaga Onsen drop out. If the family wants Kanazawa at 3, move the night to Takayama for an Okuhida snow day.
- **Takayama (2).** The town fills a day, and the Jinya closes on 29 Dec. With one night more you get the Okuhida ropeway and snow baths. At Gentle pace, 3–4 nights works because the days stay half-empty.
- **Gero (2).** Fixed by the calendar. Check in on 30 Dec, spend New Year's Eve at the ryokan, and leave after the New Year breakfast on 1 Jan. One night would mean moving on 31 Dec, a peak travel day.
- **Kyoto (4).** Three main outings plus Nara. With one night less, Nara or Arashiyama drops out. One night more gives a gentle day for Kibune or Ōhara. Arrival on 1 Jan is late afternoon, so it counts as no day.
- **Hakone (2).** One full day for the loop and a museum, and two dawns for a clear Fuji. One night would make the ryokan a quick stopover and give one chance at Fuji instead of two.
- **Tokyo, January (2).** Museums, shopping and a buffer before the flight. One night is possible but leaves no slack if a train or the weather slips.

## Who each version suits

Balanced is the only version that nobody rates poorly. The split days let your brother and sister-in-law move close to Full pace while your wife and parents stay close to Gentle.

| Who | What matters most | Gentle | Balanced | Full |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Your wife, 29–32 weeks | Rest, beds, few moves, private baths | Best fit | Good: she can skip every short visit | Poor: 8 moves and travel on peak days |
| Your parents, first visit | The classic sights without exhaustion | Good | Best fit | Poor: little rest |
| Brother and sister-in-law, first visit | Seeing a lot | Fair: less variety | Good | Best fit |
| You two (you know Tokyo and Kyoto) | New places | Fair: Kanazawa and Takayama are new | Good: four new places | Best fit: six new places |
| Your sister, if she joins | Depends on her | Fine | Fine | Fine |

## What stays fixed in every version

These come from the calendar, not from taste, so they hold whichever pace you choose:

1. Start and end in Tokyo, for the flights.
2. Kanazawa's museums are done by Sun 27 Dec. Most close on Mon 28 and again from 29 Dec.
3. New Year's Eve is at a ryokan with dinner included, because most restaurants close.
4. No long-distance moves on 29–31 Dec. 1 Jan is the quiet day to travel.
5. In Kyoto, museums go on 2–3 Jan and Nara on Mon 4 Jan.
6. Every day has a rest window, and the group may split.

## What the family needs to agree

These few answers settle the nights. Everything else in the plan follows from them.

- [ ] Each person picks a pace: Gentle, Balanced or Full
- [ ] Each person names their two must-dos, which the plan protects
- [ ] Splitting up for a morning or an afternoon is normal: nobody waits, and nobody feels bad about skipping
- [ ] The 2–4pm rest window is part of the plan, not a failure
- [ ] Your wife can change or cancel any day's plan on the day
- [ ] Kanazawa at 4 nights, or 3 plus an Okuhida snow day from Takayama
- [ ] Your sister: in or out, and sharing a room or her own
