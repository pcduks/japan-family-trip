**Style:** classic

**Theme:** Kyoto New Year, with Takayama

**Tagline:** Two nights in each small place, Takayama before Kyoto.

**Feel:** An easy start in Tokyo while the jet lag wears off and the first-timers get the classic days. On Christmas Eve the shinkansen takes us to Nagano: Zenko-ji on the way, then a hot-spring inn in Yudanaka, Shibu Onsen's lantern lane after dinner, the snow monkeys the next morning and Obuse's Hokusai paintings in the afternoon. Then Kanazawa for two full days: Kenroku-en with its snow ropes, the 21st Century Museum open late on Saturday, the market, the teahouses and crab in its last week. A bus over the mountains stops in Shirakawa-go's thatched village and goes on to Takayama for the old town, the morning market and Hida beef. On 30 December we ride to Kyoto and stay six nights in Higashiyama: the Okera fire and the temple bells on New Year's Eve, a slow New Year's Day, then museums and easy days out to Nara, Uji or Himeji. Then Lake Kawaguchi, with Fuji straight across the water and two dawns to catch it clear, and two last Tokyo days for the museums that were shut in December.

- Kyoto: Higashiyama and Kiyomizu-dera, Nishiki market, the Okera fire and the temple bells on New Year's Eve, Ohara or Nanzen-ji on New Year's Day, the National Museum and Kitano Tenmangu on 2 Jan
- Days out from Kyoto: Uji's Byodo-in and tea street, Nara's Great Buddha and deer, or Himeji Castle
- Kawaguchiko: Fuji straight across the lake, the Panoramic Ropeway, the Itchiku Kubota kimono museum, hoto noodles, Chureito Pagoda at dawn for whoever wants the climb

**Why:** It keeps every place to the nights it can fill. No small place gets more than two, every day has a plan, and the only long stay is Kyoto, where day trips and museums fill the days after New Year. New Year's Eve is the real event in Kyoto, close to two big hospitals, and four of the seven bases are new to Pedro and Fernanda. Days can split: João and Carolina can do two big things a day while Fernanda and the parents do one.

**Suits:** Fernanda at 29–32 weeks (beds, private baths, light afternoons after long moves, every night from 29 Dec to 3 Jan near a big hospital), Carlos and Nadir on a first visit, and João, Carolina and Tatiana, who get the classic sights plus snow, monkeys and a Kyoto New Year.

**Trade:** One more move than the main route: four moves between 24 and 30 Dec, including a bus day with the bags on 28 Dec and the 3.5-hour Hida train on 30 Dec, and no ski or Obuse day. The big cases wait in Kyoto while we travel with cabin bags. Kyoto costs the most at New Year, and Kyoto to the lake is about 4 h 15, the one move over four hours (Hakone instead is about 3 h).

**New Year's Eve:** New Year's Eve in Higashiyama. Kiyomizu-dera and the old lanes early, before the crowds, then a rest at the house. Anyone can ring a temple bell in daylight: in 2025 Myoman-ji let visitors ring 15:00–17:00 and Zuiko-ji 14:00–17:30 (times for 2026 to check). Dinner in, with toshikoshi soba, the noodles that close the year. From about 19:30 the Okera fire burns at Yasaka Shrine: we go early, carry the flame home on a twist of rope, and are back by 21:00, so Fernanda can be in bed by then if she likes. For the midnight bells, Kodai-ji gives numbered tickets from 22:00 to the first 108 groups, and Eikando and Chishaku-in also let people ring (2025 pattern, to check). Buses and the subway do not run all night, so we walk home through Higashiyama.

### Stay notes

**Yudanaka:** Zenko-ji on the way in and the lantern lane on Christmas Eve, then one full day: the snow monkeys in the morning and Obuse in the afternoon. A third night would only buy a ski day.

**Kanazawa:** Two full days: Kenroku-en and the museums on Saturday, the market, the teahouse district and the D.T. Suzuki Museum on Sunday. We leave on Monday 28 Dec, when the museums close anyway.

**Takayama:** Shirakawa-go and the old town at dusk on the way in, then one full day for the market, the breweries, the floats and the folk village. The town fills one good day, and the Jinya is shut from 29 Dec.

**Kyoto:** Arrival on 30 Dec, New Year's Eve, a slow New Year's Day on purpose, then three days of museums and day trips: Nara, Uji, Ohara or Himeji. Kyoto is a hub, so every day has a plan. One night less drops a day out.

**Kawaguchiko:** One full day for the ropeway and the lake, and two dawns for a clear Fuji. The move from Kyoto is about 4 h 15, the longest of the trip, so day one is only the shore and the hotel.

### Story

- We land in Tokyo on 20 December, while the Christmas lights are still up. Four easy days: markets, old gardens, museums, good food, and time for the jet lag to pass.
- On Christmas Eve a fast train takes us into the snowy hills of Nagano. We stop at Zenko-ji, an old temple, and sleep at a hot-spring inn. The next morning wild monkeys sit in a steaming pool in the snow. Then Kanazawa, on the Sea of Japan: a great garden, teahouses and winter crab.
- A bus climbs into the mountains to Shirakawa-go, a village of thatched farmhouses in the snow, and on to Takayama, an old mountain town of sake breweries, morning markets and Hida beef. On 30 December a train takes us down to Kyoto.
- Kyoto is our New Year. We carry the sacred fire home from Yasaka Shrine, hear the temple bells ring in the year, and walk back through the old lanes. Then calm days for museums, Nara's deer and the tea town of Uji. Last comes Lake Kawaguchi, with Fuji straight across the water, two more days in Tokyo, and home.
