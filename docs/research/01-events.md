## 9. Surprises & gotchas for exactly these dates

1. **Japan "closes" 29 Dec – 3 Jan**: markets (Toyosu/Tsukiji outer ~31 Dec–4 Jan), many restaurants, banks, museums (TNM 26–31 Dec; Nintendo Museum 30 Dec–3 Jan; Ghibli Museum 27 Dec–2 Jan; Ghibli Park 29 Dec–3 Jan), small shops and some ryokan dining rooms. Plan 1–3 Jan around things that are *open because* it's NY: shrines, Kabuki-za (from 2 Jan), Imperial Palace (2 Jan), department stores (2 Jan), teamLab, theme parks, illuminations, TNM "Hatsumōde" (from 1 Jan).
2. **Most Christmas illuminations end 25 Dec**: Roppongi Keyakizaka, Shibuya Blue Cave, Yokohama Red Brick market, Tokyo Christmas Market, Osaka Hikari-Renaissance, Sapporo Munich market, Sendai Pageant (~27 Dec), Midōsuji (31 Dec). Survivors into Jan: Marunouchi (14 Feb), Yebisu Baccarat (~11 Jan), Tokyo Mega Illumi (10 Jan, final season, closed 26–27 Dec & 1 Jan), Nabana no Sato (May; closed 6–7 Jan), Ashikaga (14 Feb; closed 31 Dec), Kyoto Station (31 Mar).
3. **Kobe Luminarie is 29 Jan–7 Feb 2027** (moved to late Jan since 2024) — not in December anymore; many guides are wrong.
4. **Shirakawa-gō light-ups are 11/17/24/31 Jan 2027** — the first is two days after you leave, and all are lottery/bus-tour only. Gokayama light-ups also late Jan/Feb. Daytime snow villages are fine any day.
5. **Zaō snow monsters are not "monsters" yet in late Dec/early Jan** — partial rime; full shape late Jan–Feb. Sell it as "snowy ropeway + light-up", not as the postcard.
6. **Sumo Hatsu Basho opens Sun 10 Jan 2027** — one day after departure. Tickets go on sale 5 Dec. If the return flight can move to 10 Jan evening, day 1 is worth it; otherwise book a morning stable-practice visit.
7. **Ginzan Onsen day trips are rationed**: 20 Dec–1 Mar daytime = compulsory park-and-ride; dusk (17:00–20:00) = 100 people/hour by pre-bought ticket; closed to day visitors after 20:00. The only relaxed way is an inn stay (sold out for NY long ago).
8. **Jigokudani's monkeys require a 1.6 km icy forest walk each way** (no wheelchair, "slopes and stairs"); monkeys may not show. Fine for the active couple; questionable for a 31-week pregnancy and older parents without spikes.
9. **Chion-in bell: you watch, you do not ring**; booking opens 1 Dec 14:00 online, ¥3,000, 4×500 first-come. If you want to *ring*, that's Tōdai-ji (free, 800 people, queue from 22:00) or Zenkō-ji (108 people, queue from ~22:00) or Zōjō-ji (¥2,000 tickets sold out 1 Dec morning).
10. **Imperial Palace 2 Jan is the same day as Hakone Ekiden day 1 and hatsu-uri** — three Tokyo spectacles in one morning; choose one. Palace later slots (13:30/14:20) are shorter queues and still see the Emperor.
11. **Hakone on 2–3 Jan is both wonderful and jammed**: roads on the R1 course close in waves; Hakone-Yumoto is the ideal "see both days from one spot" base; the Tōzan railway still runs. Book Hakone lodging for 1–3 Jan essentially a year ahead.
12. **Comiket 109 = 29–31 Dec, three days**: avoid Ariake/Odaiba/Toyosu mornings (teamLab Planets, Toyosu market tours) those days.
13. **Tokyo Disney has no countdown** — people still buy 31 Dec expecting one. USJ does (31 Dec 19:00 → 1 Jan 21:00), ticketed separately in October.
14. **Nintendo Museum lottery for 4–9 Jan is open this month (October 2026) and closes 31 Oct** — act now; December dates are already past the lottery (first-come leftovers only).
15. **Ghibli Museum January tickets drop 10 Dec 10:00 JST; Ghibli Park January tickets 10 Nov 14:00 JST** — set alarms; agents resell at a markup.
16. **Kinosaki has 6 outer baths, not 7** (Sato-no-yu closed permanently in 2024) and they rotate closures 29 Dec–1 Jan; female crab (seiko) ends 31 Dec.
17. **Kamikōchi and the Alpine Route are closed** for the entire window; Takayama–Shirakawa-gō–Kanazawa buses are the Alps route.
18. **Osaka Expo site is demolished** — nothing to see at Yumeshima until a memorial park (date TBD).
19. **Miyajima Oyster Festival (13–14 Feb 2027) is cancelled again** — and would have been outside your window anyway; oyster huts are the real thing, closed ~31 Dec–3 Jan.
20. **Tōka Ebisu**: only Sat 9 Jan (yoi-ebisu) falls inside the trip; the Nishinomiya "lucky man" dash and hon-ebisu are 10 Jan.
21. **Takachiho kagura skips 31 Dec and 1 Jan**, and seating is on tatami with no chairs — book online 30 days ahead and bring a cushion for parents.
22. **Hatsuhinode decks sell out in minutes in early Dec** (Skytree 540 online seats, ¥9,000). Enoshima beach via the 04:50 Odakyū New Year Romancecar is the gentle fallback.
23. **Trains**: JR East and most private lines no longer run all-night NYE service except to shrine lines (Keiō to Takao, Keikyū to Kawasaki Daishi, Odakyū extra Enoshima trains); plan Tokyo midnight hatsumōde within walking/taxi distance.
24. **Mochi and pregnancy/elderly**: ozōni mochi is the top NY choking cause; cut small. Raw crab, raw oysters, tessa, kōji-vs-kasu amazake are the pregnancy watch-outs; hot springs are fine in moderation (short soaks, no mixed icy paths).
25. **Weather**: Pacific side (Tokyo/Kyoto/Osaka/Hiroshima/Fukuoka) is dry and sunny 70 % of days, −1 to 10 °C; Japan Sea side (Kanazawa, Kinosaki, Ginzan, Nagano north) is snow/sleet most days. Diamond Fuji at Yamanakako (15:30–16:30) is in season every day of the trip.
