# 08 — Travelling in Japan at 29–32 weeks pregnant (20 Dec 2026 – 9 Jan 2027)

> **Framing.** This is a research compilation of official airline, government, obstetric-society and insurer sources, not medical advice. Every threshold below should be confirmed with the traveller's own obstetrician and, for airline and insurance rules, with the carrier/insurer in writing shortly before departure, because policies change. Where a source could not be fetched directly (several airline pages block automated access), the wording is taken from the airline's own FAQ as indexed by search and is marked **[verify on page]**.
>
> **Working assumptions used for date arithmetic** (replace with the real values from the prenatal record): 29 weeks + 0 days on 20 Dec 2026 → 28w0d ≈ 13 Dec 2026 → EDD (40w0d) ≈ **7 Mar 2027** → return 9 Jan 2027 ≈ 31w6d–32w0d. If the EDD is earlier, every threshold moves earlier.

## 1. Airlines

### 1.1 Singapore Airlines (international, SIN–TYO–SIN)

Source (fetched 1 Oct 2026): https://www.singaporeair.com/en_UK/sg/travel-info/special-assistance/expectant-women/

Exact wording from the page (periods "calculated based on the expected date of delivery"):

| Period | Restriction (SIA wording) |
|---|---|
| 28th week and earlier | "No medical certificate is required. However, a medical certificate must be presented for your return flight if it is scheduled beyond the 28th week of your pregnancy. Due to flight safety requirements, seats in the emergency exit row are not available." |
| Uncomplicated single pregnancy: 29 to 36 weeks / Uncomplicated multiple pregnancy: 29 to 32 weeks | "Provide a medical certificate stating: fitness to travel; number of weeks of pregnancy; estimated date of delivery. **The certificate must be dated within ten days of the first flight after 28 weeks of pregnancy.** Upon request, please present this certificate at check-in." No exit-row seats. |
| Single: beyond 36 weeks / Multiple: beyond 32 weeks | "Air travel is not allowed." |

The page also says: "Before booking your flight, it's important to inform your gynaecologist of your travel plans and get the medical guidance you need."

**Applied to this trip**
- Outbound SQ to Tokyo on **20 Dec 2026** at ~29 weeks: certificate **required**; it must be **dated 10–20 Dec 2026** (within ten days of 20 Dec). Practical target: issued **11–18 Dec 2026**.
- Return on **9 Jan 2027** at ~32 weeks: SIA's rule dates the certificate to "the first flight after 28 weeks", so the same certificate (dated 10–20 Dec) is what the wording requires. Because check-in staff sometimes ask for a certificate "within ten days of travel", get written confirmation from SIA Customer Service that one certificate covers both sectors, and carry the certificate in English with both flight dates, the EDD, and gestational weeks on each date. If the EDD turns out to put her at ≤28 weeks on 20 Dec, the outbound needs no certificate but the return still does.
- **Multiple pregnancy**: the 32-week cut-off would fall on or about 9 Jan 2027 — a twin pregnancy would need a return no later than the last day of week 32 (and SIA may count conservatively). Treat as a hard blocker to check with SIA before booking.
- Request non-exit-row aisle seats near a lavatory; SIA will not allocate exit rows to pregnant passengers.
- SIA's general "medical clearance / MEDIF" process applies if there are complications; the expectant-women page only covers uncomplicated pregnancies.

### 1.2 JAL (domestic)

Sources: JAL "ママとベビーのおでかけサポート" https://www.jal.co.jp/jp/ja/dom/support/smilesupport/mama.html ; JAL domestic FAQ https://faq-jp.jal.co.jp/ja/s/article/jdsp000000R0000000030971dom ; JAL certificate form page https://www.jal.co.jp/jp/ja/jalpri/pre-application/certificate.html **[verify on page — site blocked automated fetch]**

- Boarding within **28 days of the EDD (counting the EDD itself)**: a doctor's certificate (診断書) **issued within 7 days of the flight date (counting the flight day)** stating the passenger has no health problem undertaking air travel ("航空旅行を行われるに当たり、健康上支障がない").
- Boarding within **7 days of the EDD**: certificate **plus a doctor must accompany** the passenger.
- More than 28 days before the EDD: no document required.
- JAL's certificate form is downloadable from the "pre-application / certificate" page above; JAL also offers pre-boarding (事前改札) for pregnant passengers on request at the gate.

### 1.3 ANA (domestic)

Sources: https://www.ana.co.jp/ja/jp/guide/reservation/support/domestic/maternity/ ; ANA medical-certificate page https://www.ana.co.jp/ja/jp/guide/flight_service_info/assist/medical-info/ ; ANA certificate PDF (診断書) https://www.ana.co.jp/share/assist/pdf/shindan.pdf **[verify on page — timed out on fetch]**

- Within **28 days of the EDD (counting the EDD)**: certificate required.
  - **15–28 days before EDD**: certificate only.
  - **Within 14 days of EDD**: certificate **and doctor accompaniment**.
- Certificate must be **issued within 7 days of boarding (counting the boarding day)** and state the passenger has no health problem undertaking air travel. ANA asks that the PDF form be printed, completed and brought to the airport.
- ANA also hands out a maternity-mark keychain and offers pre-boarding on request (reported by passengers; confirm at check-in).

### 1.4 Peach (LCC, domestic + international)

Sources: English page https://www.flypeach.com/en/lm/ai/airports/special_support/pregnant ; Japanese help-centre article https://cs.flypeach.com/hc/ja/articles/4690988844830

- Passengers whose departure date is **within 28 days of the EDD (including the EDD)** must submit in advance a **Medical Information Form (MEDIF) with the doctor's section completed** plus a Special Assistance Request form.
- Contact the Peach contact centre (chat/phone/form) at least **3 days** before departure for any assistance needs.
- Some third-party summaries describe Peach as "certificate from 28 **weeks**, no boarding from 36 weeks" — the airline's own English page uses the **28-days-to-EDD** rule. **Confirm directly with Peach** if a Peach sector is booked.

### 1.5 Jetstar Japan (LCC)

Source: https://www.jetstar.com/jp/ja/help/pregnancy-and-air-travel (Japanese) / https://www.jetstar.com/jp/en/help/pregnancy-and-air-travel **[verify on page]**

- No certificate needed up to 28 weeks for uncomplicated pregnancies.
- Certificate required from **week 29 on international** flights and from **week 36 on Japan domestic** flights; boarding may be refused without it.
- Upper limits: flights **under 4 hours** — up to **40 weeks** (multiple pregnancy **36 weeks**); flights **4 hours or more** — up to **36 weeks** (multiple **32 weeks**). All Jetstar Japan domestic sectors are under 4 hours.

### 1.6 Skymark (domestic)

Source: https://faq-mobile.skymark.co.jp/app/answers/detail/a_id/1835/c/400 (site's TLS certificate was expired on 1 Oct 2026 — content from indexed FAQ) **[verify on page]**

- **More than 28 days before EDD**: no special procedure.
- **8–28 days before EDD**: certificate issued **within 7 days of the flight** stating no objection to air travel; if a return sector is booked, the certificate must be within 7 days of the **return** departure date too.
- **Within 7 days of EDD**: certificate plus accompanying doctor **or midwife**.

### 1.7 Comparison vs this trip

With an EDD ≈ 7 Mar 2027, the "28 days before EDD" window opens ≈ 8 Feb 2027 — **after** the trip. So under JAL, ANA, Peach and Skymark rules **no certificate is required for Japan domestic flights on 20 Dec–9 Jan**, and Jetstar Japan's domestic threshold (36 weeks) is also not reached. Carry the SIA certificate anyway; Japanese gate staff may ask to see something if the pregnancy is visibly advanced. Re-run this arithmetic with the real EDD; if the EDD is before ~6 Feb 2027 the domestic rules bite.

Japanese obstetric commentary (産婦人科オンラインジャーナル, 2021/2026 — https://journal.obstetrics.jp/2021/09/15/preg_plane/ and https://journal.obstetrics.jp/2026/05/22/air_travel/) summarises the domestic convention as "free to board until 29 days before the EDD; certificate inside 28 days; doctor accompaniment inside 7 days", and stresses asking the treating doctor before every flight because of thrombosis risk and cabin-pressure effects.

## 2. Onsen in pregnancy

- **The 2014 change.** Japan's Ministry of the Environment removed 「妊娠中（特に初期と末期）」 (pregnancy, especially early and late) from the statutory list of onsen contraindications (禁忌症) posted under the Hot Spring Act, effective **1 July 2014**, because the ministry's expert panel found no medical basis for it. Sources: Nikkei 24 Apr 2014 https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXNASDG2404T_U4A120C1CR8000/ ; Kanko Keizai Shimbun https://www.kankokeizai.com/環境省、妊婦の温泉入浴認める、禁忌症から削除/ ; Morioka City notice on the revised posting rules https://www.city.morioka.iwate.jp/kenkou/hokenjo/shokuhineisei/seikatsueisei/1006708.html ; Gunma Prefecture Pharmaceutical Affairs Division guidance for pregnant bathers https://www.pref.gunma.jp/page/6192.html.
- **What official guidance now says (Gunma Prefecture page, which mirrors the MOE's 「あんしん・あんぜんな温泉利用のいろは」 leaflet):** wet floors are "非常に滑りやすく" (extremely slippery) and pregnancy makes it hard to see one's feet — use handrails at every step; avoid long soaks, especially in hot water; keep sessions short; hydrate and rest between baths; consult an obstetrician or balneotherapy physician for added reassurance.
- **Consensus from Japanese obstetrician-reviewed guidance** (Zexy/医師監修 https://zexy.net/newlife/manual/maternity/life_002.html ; Kao Merries Q&A https://www.kao.co.jp/merries/maternity-navi/qa/ans030/ ; Nakagawa Ladies Clinic https://nakagawa-iin.net/column/onsen_during_pregnancy/ ; Tamahiyo https://st.benesse.ne.jp/ninshin/content/?id=20313): water **38–41 °C**, **≈10 minutes per soak**, **1–2 soaks a day**, never bathe alone (orthostatic dizziness), drink ~200 ml before, avoid very hot (>42 °C) or strongly acidic/sulphurous/high-salt waters only because they sting and dehydrate more, prefer **kashikiri (貸切) private baths or in-room baths** for hygiene and so a partner can be present, and stop at any sign of dizziness or contractions. **Foot baths (足湯)** are consistently recommended as the low-risk option.
- **What ryokan allow.** There is no rule barring pregnant guests; many ryokan sell "マタニティプラン" with private baths, low-step access and later check-out (e.g. Mamari list https://mamari.jp/4637 ; Sunplaza https://www.the-sunplaza.co.jp/maternity/). Some older ryokan post the pre-2014 sign still listing pregnancy; staff will usually defer to the guest. Book a room with a private bath or reserve kashikiri slots so bathing can be short and supervised.
- **Rule of thumb for the route builder:** onsen stays are fine; prefer rooms with private/in-room baths; no long outdoor-bath sessions in sub-zero air (rapid temperature swings), no sauna/steam room, and no bathing on days with long travel until rehydrated.

## 3. Winter risks

### 3.1 Ice and falls
- Snow-country mothers' advice (Zexy Baby experience column https://taikenki.zexybaby.zexy.net/entry/2023/04/13/special4747 ; Sapporo parenting site https://sapporo-kosodate.com/fuyugutsu/50/ ; Elevit/Bayer shoe guidance https://www.elevit.jp/ninshin/articles/worries/shoes/): flat (≤2–3 cm heel) boots with a deep-lug or Vibram **Arctic Grip** sole; **strap-on crampons/spikes** (sold at convenience stores and Don Quijote in Hokkaido/Tohoku) for Sapporo, Zao, Takayama, Kanazawa in cold snaps; short steps, hands out of pockets, use handrails. Pregnancy shifts the centre of gravity and hides the feet — treat **any icy slope, temple stairs and ryokan stone paths** as no-go without a hand to hold.
- Snow-free bases in the window (Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, Hiroshima, Fukuoka) still ice over on bridges and station stairs on clear mornings after rain.

### 3.2 Cold, dry air, dehydration
- Japanese winter indoor humidity routinely drops to 20–30 %; nosebleeds, dry cough and reduced thirst perception are common. RCOG/NHS travel guidance: drink water at regular intervals and limit caffeine (NHS "Travelling in pregnancy" https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/travelling/ ; RCOG patient leaflet https://www.rcog.org.uk/for-the-public/browse-our-patient-information/air-travel-and-pregnancy/). Carry a 500 ml bottle, refill at hotel; use the hotel humidifier (most business hotels lend one).
- Layering (heat-tech base layer, mid layer, windproof shell) rather than one heavy coat, because stations and trains are heated to 22–25 °C.

### 3.3 Long sitting — DVT
- ACOG Committee Opinion 746 (2018) https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2018/08/air-travel-during-pregnancy : for pregnant travellers, "use of support stockings, periodic movement of the lower extremities, occasional ambulation, maintenance of adequate hydration, and avoidance of restrictive clothing"; an aisle seat.
- RCOG Scientific Impact Paper No. 1 https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/jw4jyghl/sip_1.pdf : graduated compression stockings for flights **>4 h**; LMWH only if additional risk factors (discuss with own obstetrician).
- NHS DVT-and-travel leaflets https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/complications/deep-vein-thrombosis/ : journeys >4 h roughly double DVT risk; get up every 30–60 min; ankle circles while seated.
- Practical translation for trains: the SIN–NRT/HND sector (~7 h) and any shinkansen leg >2 h → compression stockings on, aisle seat, stand and walk the car every hour, water every 30 min. Green Car or Gran Class gives more leg-room; reserve seats near the car-end toilet.

### 3.5 Crowd crush: hatsumōde, Ekiden, New Year
- Japan's National Police Agency chief instructed all prefectural forces on 31 Oct 2022, immediately after the Itaewon crush, to apply crowd-control (雑踏警備) measures properly at Halloween and subsequent mass events; Japanese practice (one-way flows, "DJ police" loud-speaker officers, timed release at shrine approaches) is based on the Hyōgo police manual written after the 2001 Akashi bridge crush. Sources: TBS/NPA report https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nC-ottTWyw0 ; Nikkei (Oct 2023) https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOGM256FO0V21C23A0000000/ ; Kobe Shimbun on the manual https://www.kobe-np.co.jp/rentoku/omoshiro/202211/0015771726.shtml.
- Biggest hatsumōde crowds (≈3 million each over 1–3 Jan): **Meiji Jingū (Tokyo), Naritasan Shinshōji (Chiba), Kawasaki Daishi (Kanagawa)**, then Sensōji, Fushimi Inari (Kyoto), Sumiyoshi Taisha (Osaka), Atsuta Jingū, Dazaifu Tenmangū (Fukuoka), Hokkaidō Jingū (Sapporo). Meiji Jingū waits reach 3–4 h and the approach is a managed, stop-start crush between about 22:00 on 31 Dec and 03:00 on 1 Jan and again from 10:00 on 1–3 Jan. Sources: Travel Watch 2023 top-10 https://travel.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1467275.html ; 2025 ranking https://www.arakurenote.com/3936.html ; Meiji Jingū 2026 congestion guides https://colorfulmixblog.hatenablog.com/entry/2025/11/22/130000.
- **Route rule:** no top-10 hatsumōde shrine on 31 Dec night or 1–3 Jan daytime; if a shrine visit is wanted, go to a neighbourhood shrine or a major shrine **before 08:00 on 2–3 Jan** or **after 4 Jan**. Avoid the Hakone Ekiden roadside (2–3 Jan, Tokyo Ōtemachi–Hakone) and Shibuya/Harajuku on New Year's Eve. Rail: the first trains of 1 Jan run all night (終夜運転) on many Tokyo lines and are packed.

### 4.2 How to see a doctor as a visitor
JNTO's step list: reception (ask for a cost estimate) → questionnaire → consultation → pay (cash at clinics; cards only at large hospitals) → pharmacy. Short-term visitors pay **100 % of the fee** (residents pay 30 %), plus a first-visit fee; large hospitals add a **non-referral surcharge (選定療養費), ¥7,700+ at designated hospitals**, and some charge foreign visitors at 150–300 % of the standard tariff. Typical: clinic consult ¥5,000–15,000; hospital outpatient with tests ¥15,000–50,000; ER after hours more. Unpaid bills can lead to future entry refusal (JNTO). Sources: JNTO guide above; Tokyo Metropolitan Government medical-cost page https://www.hokeniryo.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/iryo/iryo_hoken/medical_info_eng/system ; Japan Healthcare Info https://japanhealthinfo.com/japanese-healthcare-services/costs/.

**New Year (29 Dec–3 Jan).** Most clinics and hospital outpatient departments close; general hospitals keep **emergency departments open 24/7** but may triage to "emergency only". Each municipality runs **休日急患診療所 (holiday emergency clinics, typically 09:00–17:00)** and a rota of on-duty doctors (当番医) published on the city website — search "〔city〕 休日診療 年末年始". Obstetric emergencies (bleeding, regular contractions, reduced fetal movement, severe headache/visual change, rupture of membranes) go **directly to a hospital with an obstetrics department via 119 or by taxi after phoning the hospital's OB line** — not to a holiday clinic, which usually cannot see obstetric cases. Sources: Sakai City New Year notice https://www.city.sakai.lg.jp/kenko/iryokusuri/iryo/kinkyubyoin/nenmatunenshi_sinryo.html ; Yokohama Medical Association https://www.yokohama.kanagawa.med.or.jp/yakan_kyujitsu/nenmatu-nenshi.html ; Chiba Prefecture list format https://www.pref.chiba.lg.jp/kenfuku/documents/00-gairai-nenmatsunenshi.pdf.

## 6. Maternity mark, priority seats, access

- **Maternity mark (マタニティマーク) keychain.** Free. Issued by municipalities with the mother-and-child handbook (residents only), but **also handed out at railway station offices to anyone who asks, including family members, no proof needed.** JR East: every staffed station in Tokyo, Kanagawa, Saitama, Chiba, Ibaraki, Tochigi, Gunma, Yamanashi (plus Atami, Itō and some Fukushima stations); at Tokyo, Ueno, Ikebukuro and Shinjuku go to the お客さま相談室. Source: https://www.jreast.co.jp/en/baby/maternity/. Toei Subway and Tokyo Metro: ask at the station office (駅事務室) — https://www.kotsu.metro.tokyo.jp/subway/kanren/matenity.html. ANA/JAL also give one at check-in on request. Say: 「マタニティマークをいただけますか」.
- **Priority seats (優先席)** at car ends on all metro/JR commuter lines; the mark signals the entitlement, but Tokyo etiquette means people often don't offer — ask politely. Shinkansen: reserve seats; no priority seats, but the multipurpose room (多目的室) on each train can be used by pregnant passengers if unwell (ask the conductor).
- **Women-only cars (女性専用車)** run on weekday **morning rush only** (roughly 07:00–09:30) on many Tokyo/Osaka lines; avoid rush hour altogether (07:30–09:00, 17:30–19:30) — this is a routing constraint.
- **Elevators / step-free routes.** Toei has elevators at every station; use **Rakuraku Odekake Net** https://www.ecomo-rakuraku.jp/en/ (station barrier-free maps, English) and the Toei barrier-free guide https://www.kotsu.metro.tokyo.jp/eng/tips/barrier_free.html. Large JR stations have elevators but transfers can be 300–500 m; allow 15 min extra per transfer.
- **Toilets.** Every station, convenience store and department store has free toilets; multi-function toilets (多機能トイレ) are step-free. Carry tissues outside cities.
- **Luggage-free travel.** Yamato TA-Q-BIN hotel-to-hotel next-day (two days for long hops) ~¥2,500–3,500 per bag; same-day Tokyo↔Kyoto "LUGGAGE EXPRESS" via JR Central/JTB since Nov 2025; airport-to-hotel on arrival. Sources: https://www.kuronekoyamato.co.jp/ytc/en/send/services/same-day-delivery/ ; https://global.jr-central.co.jp/en/news/_pdf/2025/0009.pdf ; https://tokyocheapo.com/travel/luggage-delivery-services/. The pregnant traveller should carry nothing heavier than a day-bag.

## 7. Daily pattern and food

### 7.1 Daily pattern (from ACOG/RCOG/NHS travel guidance, adapted)
- ACOG: third-trimester travel "may be riskier … harder to move around or sit for a long time"; stop every **1–2 h** to walk; keep total travel to **≈5–6 h a day**. https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/travel-during-pregnancy
- NHS/RCOG: fluids regularly, limit caffeine, compression stockings for >4 h sitting, move every 30–60 min. https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/travelling/
- Translation into route rules: **≤4 h of transit per day (door to door), one long-haul leg per day max; walking ≤8–10 km/day with a sit-down every 60–90 min; one "anchor" activity per half-day; late start (no departures before 09:30, avoids rush hour) and back at the hotel by 19:00; a rest day after any travel day ≥3 h; three meals plus two snacks (Japanese portions are small; keep onigiri/nuts in the bag); 1.5–2 L water; no itinerary item that requires standing in a queue >30 min.**
- Warning signs that end the day's plan and trigger a call to the hospital/119: vaginal bleeding, fluid leak, regular tightenings (≥4 in an hour), reduced fetal movement, severe headache/visual disturbance/upper-abdominal pain/sudden swelling (pre-eclampsia), calf pain/swelling on one side, chest pain or breathlessness.

### 7.2 Food — nuanced, not blanket bans

**Raw fish (sashimi/sushi).** Japanese obstetric opinion (Dr Daisuke Shigemi, Yahoo! Expert https://news.yahoo.co.jp/expert/articles/0579957961a8eb6dfbc9b1a79a2236662d38da54 ; Hashimoto OB/GYN https://www.hashi-sanfu.com/tidbits/post467/ ; Mynavi 医師監修 https://kosodate.mynavi.jp/articles/7089) does **not** ban raw fish: fresh, properly handled fish from a reputable restaurant is acceptable in moderation (one clinic suggests ~5 pieces/week as a guide); the risks are **food poisoning (Vibrio, Anisakis, Listeria)** — more consequential in pregnancy because of lowered immunity — and **methylmercury** in large predatory species. International guidance (ACOG/FDA, NHS) is stricter on raw fish mainly because of Listeria in cold-smoked and ready-to-eat products; NHS allows sushi if the fish was frozen first (standard in Japan for most species except farmed salmon and tuna). Practical rule: cooked or seared items freely; raw fish only at reputable sushi counters/depachika, same day, not from convenience-store packs that have sat warm; skip raw shellfish/oysters entirely (norovirus/Vibrio).

**Mercury (MHLW 妊婦への魚介類の摂食と水銀に関する注意事項, 2005, rev. 2010):** https://www.mhlw.go.jp/topics/bukyoku/iyaku/syoku-anzen/suigin/dl/100601-1.pdf and plain-language summary https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/000533682.pdf
- **≤1 serving (80 g) per week**: kinmedai (alfonsino), mekajiki (swordfish), kuro-maguro (bluefin tuna), mebachi (bigeye tuna), Etchū bai snail, baird's beaked whale, sperm whale.
- **≤2 servings (160 g) per week**: kidai (yellowback seabream), makajiki (striped marlin), yumekasago, minami-maguro (southern bluefin), yoshikirizame (blue shark), Dall's porpoise.
- **≤1 serving per 2 weeks**: bandōiruka (bottlenose dolphin); **≤1 per 2 months**: kobireg­ondō (short-finned pilot whale).
- **No restriction needed**: kihada/binnaga (yellowfin/albacore) and canned tuna, salmon, saba, aji, iwashi, sanma, tai, buri, katsuo, shrimp, squid, crab, etc. The MHLW note stresses that fish remains beneficial and should not be avoided wholesale.

**Listeria / toxoplasma / salmonella** (MHLW Listeria page https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/0000055260.html ; Boshi Eiyō Kyōkai https://boshieiyou.org/listeria/): avoid **unpasteurised soft cheeses** (imported camembert/blue; Japanese-made natural cheeses are pasteurised and generally fine), **raw ham/prosciutto and pâté**, **cold-smoked salmon**, **raw or rare meat** (yukke, rare steak, basashi horse sashimi, tataki), and **raw egg** (tamago-kake-gohan, sukiyaki dipping egg, raw-egg sauces) — Japanese eggs are salmonella-controlled but Japanese obstetric advice still says cooked. **Liver** (レバー, foie gras, ankimo in large amounts): high retinol — limit; the Japanese 妊産婦のための食事バランスガイド recommends avoiding regular liver intake in pregnancy. Natto, tofu, pasteurised milk, well-cooked yakitori (order "よく焼き"), tempura, tonkatsu, ramen (watch sodium), oden, nabe are all fine. Caffeine: ≤200 mg/day (two small coffees; matcha ~70 mg/cup). Alcohol: none.

## 8. Hard constraints for the route builder (summary)
1. SIA sectors only on 20 Dec and 9 Jan; **certificate dated 10–20 Dec 2026**, singleton confirmed; if twins, the return must be inside week 32 or the trip is not bookable on SIA.
2. Japan domestic flights allowed (no certificate if EDD ≥ ~8 Feb 2027), but prefer ≤1 flight and none on 30 Dec–3 Jan (full flights, long queues).
3. Transit ≤4 h door-to-door per day; one shinkansen leg >2 h max per day; aisle/Green Car seats; a rest day after every ≥3 h travel day.
4. No departures 07:30–09:00 or 17:30–19:30 on weekdays (rush hour; women-only cars only then).
5. Walking ≤8–10 km/day, seated break every 60–90 min, no queue >30 min, no standing event.
6. No top-10 hatsumōde shrine 31 Dec night–3 Jan daytime; no Hakone Ekiden roadside; no Shibuya/Harajuku on NYE.
7. No icy hikes, no Ōwakudani alighting, no ropeway summit stays in storm conditions; crampons for Sapporo/Zaō/Takayama days.
8. Every base must have a listed hospital with 24-h obstetrics within 30 min; 29 Dec–3 Jan bases should be cities with a university/Red Cross/perinatal centre (Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, Fukuoka, Sapporo, Sendai, Hiroshima, Kanazawa), not a rural onsen town.
9. Onsen: rooms with private/in-room bath; ≤10 min at ≤41 °C, ≤2 soaks/day, never alone; foot baths freely.
10. Luggage forwarded hotel-to-hotel; pregnant traveller carries a day-bag only.
