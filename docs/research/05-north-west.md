# 05 — North & West of Tokyo: experience catalog (20 Dec 2026 – 9 Jan 2027)

Research date: 1 Oct 2026. Group: 6 (planner + partner at 29–32 weeks, brother + SIL active/ski-curious, two very fit parents). PT-BR speakers, place names kept in English. Currency ¥; costs are per adult unless noted.

## 0. Read this first — cross-cutting facts for these dates

**Calendar reality.** 29 Dec–3 Jan is Japan's single busiest domestic travel week. Museums, castles' interiors, many restaurants and most markets close 29/30 Dec–3/4 Jan; shrines/temples are open (and packed for *hatsumode* from midnight 31 Dec through 3 Jan); onsen towns, ski resorts, illuminations and convenience stores stay open. Shinkansen reserved seats on 27–30 Dec (outbound from Tokyo) and 2–4 Jan (inbound) sell out days ahead — Hokuriku *Kagayaki* and Tōhoku *Hayabusa/Komachi* are all-reserved, no standing. Book seats the moment the 1-month window opens (eki-net / smart-ex / JR East app). Six people in reserved seats on those days is the single hardest logistics item in this file.

**Snow likelihood in-window.** Sea-of-Japan side (Nozawa, Myōkō, Yuzawa, Ginzan, Nyūtō, Shirakawa-gō, Takayama) — snow on the ground is near-certain by 25 Dec most years; 2025–26 was a thin December, so plan for "probably white, not guaranteed deep". Nagano city, Matsumoto, Kanazawa, Sendai, Nikkō town — intermittent, often bare ground with cold. Fuji area/Hakone/Izu — dry, sunny, cold; Fuji is at its clearest (best visibility of the year) and snow-capped.

**What is NOT in the window (don't be sold it).** Ōuchi-juku Snow Festival (14–15 Feb 2026; 2027 will be the 2nd weekend of Feb), Shirakawa-gō light-up (11/17/24/31 Jan 2027, lottery-only), Zaō snow monsters at full size (late Jan–Feb), Yamanakako Diamond Fuji Weeks (Feb; the lake's window opens ~18 Jan), Kawaguchiko winter fireworks (from 23 Jan 2027), Hirosaki snow lantern festival (Feb), Towada winter festival (Feb), Nozawa fire festival (15 Jan), Kawazu sakura (from 6 Feb 2027), Hachinohe Enburi (Feb), Gokayama Ainokura light-up (20–21 Feb 2027).

**Bump rules used in this file.** `bump_ok` 5 = flat, warm, bathrooms, can bail; 1 = long stairs/ice/no exit. Shinkansen is bump-friendly (wide seats, toilets, smooth); local buses on snowy mountain roads are the weak link. Pregnant partner: onsen soaking is culturally fine and common in Japan, but keep to ≤10 min, lukewarm-ish baths, private/family baths (*kashikiri*) ideal; skip mixed outdoor rotenburo where you have to walk far on ice. Carry a *maternity mark* (free from JR station offices) — gets priority seats.

**Six-person lodging reality.** (a) **Machiya/kominka rentals** (Takayama, Kanazawa, Hida-Furukawa, Nozawa, Nikkō) — the best fit: 3 bedrooms, kitchen, one bill, usually ¥60–120k/night for 6. Over NY they go 9–12 months out; as of Oct 2026 expect slim pickings for 30 Dec–2 Jan, decent for 20–28 Dec and 4–9 Jan. (b) **Ryokan with 2 connecting rooms or a 6-tatami-mat "large room"** — pricey over NY (¥30–60k pp with meals, surcharge 31 Dec–2 Jan), and many require 2-night minimums 31 Dec–1 Jan. (c) **Apartment hotels** (Mimaru, Tokyu Stay, Dormy Inn connecting) — reliable in Kanazawa, Sendai, Nagano, Niigata, Toyama; less so in onsen villages. Recommendation: build the loop so the 30 Dec–2 Jan block sits in ONE place with a kitchen (Kanazawa or Takayama machiya), and use ryokan for 1–2 "splurge" nights outside that block.

**Rail-pass note.** For a loop Tokyo→Nagano→Kanazawa→Takayama→Tokyo the JR East-South Hokuriku Arch Pass or JR East Nagano-Niigata pass can pay; for a Tōhoku loop the JR East Tōhoku Area Pass (5 days, ¥30k) pays after one Sendai return. Full JR Pass almost never pays anymore.

## 1. Nikkō & Kinugawa (Tochigi)

**Why these dates.** Nikkō's shrine complex under a dusting of snow is quieter than any other time; Tōshō-gū is open daily year-round and is the easiest "World Heritage + snow" hit from Tokyo (2 h). Lake Chūzenji / Yumoto Onsen (1,500 m) are reliably snowy and Kegon Falls is partly frozen by late Dec. Kinugawa is a big-ryokan river valley that absorbs a group of 6 easily and has family baths. NY: Tōshō-gū and Futarasan are packed 31 Dec night–3 Jan (midnight hatsumode); the town itself is sleepy and half the restaurants close 31 Dec–2 Jan.

**Honest downsides.** Tōshō-gū's main hall sits up ~200 stone steps from the bus stop and the Ieyasu tomb is another 207 — the pregnant partner should stop at the Yōmeimon gate (bump_ok 3 to there). Winter bus to Chūzenji is on the Irohazaka hairpins (48 bends — motion sickness). Kinugawa town is faded (closed hotels on the riverbank). Edo Wonderland is cheesy but genuinely fun for a mixed group.

**Bases for six.** *Nikkō town* — a few kominka rentals (3 br) near the Shinkyō bridge, plus Nikkō Kanaya (connecting rooms) and Fufu Nikkō (expensive). *Kinugawa* — big ryokan (Kinugawa Kanaya, Asaya) with 2-room suites; easy for 6. NY booked-out level: medium (Tokyo day-trippers, not hometown travellers).

**Transit.** Tokyo (Asakusa) → Tōbu-Nikkō by Spacia X / Revaty 1 h 50 direct (bump 5, reserved, toilets); JR Tokyo→Utsunomiya→Nikkō 1 h 50 with 1 change. Nikkō ↔ Kinugawa 35 min local. Onward: Nikkō → Aizu-Wakamatsu via Tōbu/Aizu Line (Revaty to Aizu-Tajima, change) ~3 h — this is the natural link into Tōhoku-south (Ōuchi-juku) without returning to Tokyo (bump 3; single-car diesel for the last leg).

**Shapes.** 2 nights: Tōshō-gū + Kanmangafuchi walk, 1 night Kinugawa ryokan with family bath. 3 nights: add Chūzenji/Yumoto Onsen snow day (parents + brother hike to Senjōgahara snowshoe; partner stays at Yumoto bath). 4 nights: too many for this group — move on to Aizu.

### Cards
- **NW-NIK-01 · Santuários de Nikkō na neve / Nikkō shrines (Tōshō-gū, Rinnō-ji, Futarasan)** — gilded gates under cedar and snow. Suits all to Yōmeimon; parents/brother to the tomb. Stairs: ~200 to gate, +207 to tomb. Outdoor. ¥1,600 Tōshō-gū. 2–3 h. No booking. NY: open, crushing on 1–3 Jan; go 20–28 Dec or 4+ Jan at 8:00.
- **NW-NIK-02 · Passeio Kanmangafuchi / Kanmangafuchi Abyss & the 70 Jizō** — flat 1.5 km riverside path, dusted statues, 20 min from the shrines. All; bump 4 (short gravel). Free. 1 h. NY fine.
- **NW-NIK-03 · Lago Chūzenji e cascata Kegon congelada / Lake Chūzenji & frozen Kegon Falls** — elevator (paid ¥570) to the falls viewing deck — no stairs. Bus 45 min up Irohazaka (bump 2, motion sickness). All. 3–4 h. NY: buses run, cafés partly closed.
- **NW-NIK-04 · Raquetes de neve em Senjōgahara / Senjōgahara marsh snowshoe** — flat 6 km boardwalk under snow, guided ¥6–8k with gear. Parents + brother/SIL only (split). Outdoor, effort 3. Half day. Book 1–2 weeks ahead; runs from ~late Dec once snow sets. NY: some guides run 2–3 Jan.
- **NW-NIK-05 · Yumoto Onsen (banho sulfuroso de montanha) / Yumoto Onsen milky sulphur baths** — 1,500 m, reliable snow, day-use baths ¥1,000. Pair with 04 for the partner (rest + soak while others snowshoe). Bump 4. 2 h.
- **NW-NIK-06 · Edo Wonderland / Edo Wonderland Nikkō Edomura** — ninja/samurai theme park, flat paths, indoor shows, good for a cold day; cheesy but a group hit. ¥5,800. 3–4 h. Closed Wed; open NY (short winter hours 9:30–16:00). Bump 4.
- **NW-NIK-07 · Ryokan de rio em Kinugawa com banho privativo / Kinugawa riverside ryokan with kashikiri bath** — 2 connecting rooms or 1 large room for 6, kaiseki, private bath for the partner. ¥25–40k pp. Overnight. Book 3–6 months; 31 Dec–1 Jan surcharge and 2-night minimum common.
- **NW-NIK-08 · Yuba (tofu skin) e nikkō-zushi / Yuba cuisine lunch** — Nikkō's specialty; several set lunches ¥1,500–3,000 near the bridge. Indoor. 1 h. Closed 31 Dec–2 Jan mostly.

## 2. Kusatsu, Shima & Ikaho (Gunma)

**Why these dates.** Kusatsu (1,200 m) is Japan's most-ranked onsen town: the steaming *Yubatake* with its Christmas-tree illumination (ran 22 Nov–23 Feb last season, free, 17:00–23:00), six daily *yumomi* water-stirring shows (open through NY), and reliable snow from mid-Dec. It's the best "pure onsen town, no stairs needed" option within 3.5 h of Tokyo. Shima Onsen (Sekizenkan, the Spirited-Away-ish 1691 bathhouse) is a quieter, cheaper alternative. Ikaho's famous 365 stone steps are precisely what the partner must not do (and they ice over).

**Honest downsides.** Kusatsu is reached by bus (Naganohara-Kusatsuguchi station → 25 min bus, or 4-h direct highway bus from Shinjuku) and has NO rail; the town's public baths are hot (42–46°C — pregnant partner should use lukewarm private baths only); it's a dead end for onward travel (bus to Karuizawa ~1 h 20 in winter via Kusatsu–Karuizawa JR bus, weather-dependent). Over NY the town is full of Tokyo families; ryokan prices double.

**Bases for six.** Kusatsu — big ryokan with 2-room suites (Kusatsu Hotel, Hotel Ichii, Kirishimaya) and a handful of 6-pax rental houses; apartment hotels: none. NY booked-out: high (book 6+ months). Shima — Sekizenkan has large rooms; cheaper, quieter.

**Transit.** Tokyo→Takasaki shinkansen 50 min → Naganohara-Kusatsuguchi 1 h 10 (limited express *Kusatsu-Shima*, direct from Ueno 2 h 30) → bus 25 min. Bump 3 overall. Kusatsu→Karuizawa JR bus 80 min (bump 3, snow chains). Kusatsu→Nagano: via Karuizawa shinkansen, ~3 h total.

**Shapes.** 2 nights is right (arrive, Yubatake night, yumomi, Sainokawara park bath, leave). 3 nights only if someone wants Kusatsu Kokusai ski (small, beginner-friendly). Skip 4.

### Cards
- **NW-KUS-01 · Yubatake iluminado / Yubatake hot-water field & illumination** — the steaming wooden troughs under lights; free; flat, 10 min from any hotel; bathrooms around. All, bump 5. 1 h at dusk. NY: on, busy.
- **NW-KUS-02 · Show de yumomi / Yumomi water-stirring performance (Netsu-no-yu)** — 20-min folk show, seated, indoors, warm; 6×/day incl. NY. ¥700. All, bump 5. Audience participation for the parents.
- **NW-KUS-03 · Sainokawara rotenburo / Sainokawara open-air park bath** — 500 m² outdoor bath in a snowy park, 10-min flat walk. ¥800. Hot (41–43°C): parents/brother/SIL yes; partner only feet/short. Bump 3. 1.5 h. Open NY.
- **NW-KUS-04 · Banho privativo em ryokan / Ryokan kashikiri bath night** — book a ryokan with reservable private bath; the pregnant partner's safe soak. ¥25–45k pp over NY. Overnight.
- **NW-KUS-05 · Dia de esqui Kusatsu Kokusai / Kusatsu Onsen Ski Resort day** — small resort 5 min by bus; beginner/intermediate; ¥5,500 lift. Brother/SIL (split day). Dec snow: usually OK by 25 Dec. Open NY.
- **NW-KUS-06 · Sekizenkan em Shima / Sekizenkan historic bathhouse, Shima Onsen** — 1691 bathhouse, Roman-arch bath; day-use ¥1,500 or stay in large rooms. Bump 4 (some steps inside old building). Half day/overnight. Access: Nakanojō station + bus 40 min.
- **NW-KUS-07 · Ikaho — só a vista, não os degraus / Ikaho stone steps (parents/brother only)** — 365 steps, steamed buns, retro arcades; ice in Dec. Partner: skip or sit at the bottom café. Free. 2 h. Only if routing past Shibukawa.

## 3. Karuizawa (Nagano, east)

**Why these dates.** 1 h from Tokyo by shinkansen (bump 5), the resort town does a long illumination season (Karuizawa Winter Festival, 22 Nov–28 Feb last season) and has the single most bump-and-beginner-friendly ski hill in Japan: Karuizawa Prince, open from 1 Nov on machine snow, right at the station, with tubing, sledding and a huge outlet mall + food halls alongside. It is the obvious "snow day for the brother + warm mall day for the partner" split spot, and a near-zero-risk NY base (everything open, chains like Prince run through 1 Jan).

**Honest downsides.** Thin natural snow (expect brown ground with white pistes), very Tokyo-weekend, expensive, little "Japan" feel; Prince slopes get crowded on 29 Dec–3 Jan. Shiraito Falls in winter is pretty but a 25-min bus and a short slope.

**Bases.** Dozens of 6-pax rental villas (Karuizawa is villa country) — the easiest 6-person house market in this file; Prince hotels' cottages sleep 6. NY booked-out: high but large supply.

**Transit.** Tokyo→Karuizawa 1 h 05 (Hokuriku Shinkansen). →Nagano 30 min. →Kusatsu JR bus 80 min. →Matsumoto via Shinonoi ~2 h.

**Shapes.** 1–2 nights only (a stopover with a ski day); 3+ is wasted on this group.

### Cards
- **NW-KAR-01 · Dia de esqui/tubing em Karuizawa Prince / Karuizawa Prince ski & snow-play day** — 10 min walk from the shinkansen; rentals, lessons in English, tubing/snow escalator. Lift ¥6,500. Brother/SIL ski; parents try; partner reads in the mall food hall. Open NY (busy). Bump: n/a (split).
- **NW-KAR-02 · Iluminação de inverno Karuizawa / Karuizawa Winter Festival lights (Kyū-Karuizawa & Harunire Terrace)** — strolling lights, flat, cafés. Free. 1–2 h. All, bump 5.
- **NW-KAR-03 · Harunire Terrace e Hoshino Onsen Tombo-no-yu / Harunire Terrace + Tombo-no-yu bath** — forest-terrace shops/restaurants and a clean modern onsen (¥1,350; lukewarm outdoor pool). Bump 4. Half day. Open NY.
- **NW-KAR-04 · Cataratas Shiraito no gelo / Shiraito Falls in winter (illuminated some winters)** — curtain falls that partly freeze; bus 25 min; 5-min flat path. Free. 2 h. Bump 4.

## 4. Nagano, Zenkō-ji, Yudanaka/Shibu & the snow monkeys, Nozawa, Hakuba, Myōkō

**Why these dates.** This is the strongest "winter Japan" cluster in the file and the simplest to reach (Tokyo→Nagano 80 min, bump 5). Snow monkeys bathe most reliably when it's coldest (late Dec–Feb) and the park is open daily incl. NY (winter 9:00–16:00). Zenkō-ji's *o-asaji* dawn service and NY (the temple does the Jan 1 bell; 2027 is a *Gokaichō* year — the once-in-7-years unveiling runs ~Apr–Jun 2027, NOT in window, but the town is already dressing up). Nozawa Onsen (lifts open 28 Nov 2026–5 May 2027) is the one place that gives everyone something: 13 free village bathhouses, a cobbled village, and a big mountain for the brother/SIL. Hakuba is bigger/better skiing but a worse village for non-skiers; Myōkō gets the deepest snow (13 m/season) but is diffuse.

**Honest downsides.** The monkey park requires a 1.6 km forest walk each way on packed snow/ice plus a final steep rugged section — NOT stroller/wheelchair accessible; for the partner at 30 weeks this is a 2/5 (doable with crampons, poles, and 40 min each way, but a fall risk; honest call is parents/brother/SIL go, partner stays in Shibu Onsen at a café or the ryokan). Monkeys are famous-but-can-underwhelm: crowded viewing platform, 30 min of looking. Nozawa village is steep and icy (bump 2 for street-walking), lodging is ski-chalet style and books 9–12 months out for NY. Hakuba lodging for 6 at NY: very hard (Australian/Chinese NY peak).

**Bases for six.** *Nagano city* — apartment hotels, cheap, bump 5, trains both ways: good hub for the partner. *Shibu Onsen* — 9 bathhouses on one lane; old ryokan with large rooms (Kanaguya has 6-pax rooms, ~¥35k pp, books out a year for NY). *Nozawa* — self-catered lodges/apartments for 6 (Nozawa Peaks, Nozawa Hospitality); NY = premium, 5–7-night minimums common 26 Dec–3 Jan. *Hakuba* — chalets for 6 exist in Wadano/Echoland; NY minimum stays 7 nights.

**Transit.** Tokyo→Nagano 1 h 20–1 h 40. Nagano→Yudanaka 45–50 min (Nagaden limited express *Snow Monkey*, bump 4) + 15-min bus or 1.6 km walk. Nagano→Nozawa: shinkansen Iiyama 12 min + Nozawa Liner bus 25 min (bump 3). Nagano→Hakuba express bus 70 min (bump 3, snow). Nagano→Myōkō-Kōgen: Shinano Railway/Echigo-Tokimeki 50 min (bump 4). Nagano→Kanazawa 65 min (bump 5). Nagano→Matsumoto 50–80 min JR Shinonoi (bump 4).

**Shapes.** 2 nights: Nagano hub — Zenkō-ji evening + morning, monkey half day. 3 nights: add Nozawa (1 night: free bathhouses, ski half-day for brother/SIL). 4 nights: Nagano 1 + Shibu Onsen 1 + Nozawa 2 (ski day) — the recommended shape; or swap Nozawa for Myōkō's Lotte/Arai if you want a resort hotel that does everything indoors (zipline, tubing, kids room, spa) for the group.

- **NW-NAG-01 · Macacos da neve de Jigokudani / Jigokudani snow monkeys** — the icon. Parents/brother/SIL (partner optional with crampons + poles, 2/5). 1.6 km each way, final rugged climb. Outdoor. ¥800. 3 h incl. transit from Yudanaka. No booking. Open NY (busiest days of the year 29 Dec–3 Jan; arrive 9:00).
- **NW-NAG-02 · Zenkō-ji ao amanhecer (o-asaji) / Zenkō-ji morning service & o-kaidan pitch-dark passage** — 7-c. temple, dawn service (~7:00 in winter) then the underground "key to paradise" tunnel (flat, dark, handrail). ¥600 inner hall. Bump 4 (10 steps up to the hall). 1.5 h. Open NY — Jan 1 is the largest hatsumode in Nagano; 31 Dec midnight bell. Stay on Nakamise lane for 1-min access.
- **NW-NAG-03 · Shibu Onsen 9-banhos com yukata / Shibu Onsen nine-bathhouse stamp walk** — one 400 m lane, nine tiny free bathhouses (key from your ryokan), geta and yukata. Bump 3 (icy lane, hot water — partner just does the foot bath + the big 9th bath briefly). 2 h. Only for ryokan guests.

- **NW-NAG-07 · Ryokan Kanaguya (Shibu) / Kanaguya ryokan night** — 1758 wooden ryokan said to inspire Spirited Away; 6-pax rooms exist. ¥30–45k pp. Book 12 months; NY nearly impossible by Oct 2026 — check cancellations.

## 5. Matsumoto

**Why these dates.** The black castle in snow (uncertain — Matsumoto is dry; maybe 30% chance of a white day in-window), the easiest Alps views of the year (clear winter air), a compact grid for the partner, and a good night-stop between Nagano and Takayama. NY: castle closed 29–31 Dec, open 1–3 Jan 10:00–15:30 (reduced), regular from 4 Jan. Castle interior = six floors of steep (ladder-like) stairs — partner stays in the grounds (free, flat).

**Honest downsides.** One real sight. Yayoi Kusama museum (Matsumoto City Museum of Art) closed 29 Dec–3 Jan. Nawate/Nakamachi streets charming for 1 h. Kamikōchi closed (Nov–Apr). The Takayama bus crosses Hirayu pass (chains, 2 h 30; bump 2).

**Bases.** Hotels/apartment hotels fine for 6 (2–3 twins or a couple of 3-pax rooms); a few kominka rentals. NY booked-out: low-medium.

**Transit.** Tokyo→Matsumoto 2 h 40 (Azusa limited express, bump 4). Nagano→Matsumoto 50–80 min. Matsumoto→Takayama bus 2 h 30 (reserved; runs NY, weather risk). Matsumoto→Nagoya 2 h (Shinano) for the Hida line to Takayama (another 2 h 20).

**Shapes.** 1 night (ideal), 2 nights if adding Hotaka/Shirahone Onsen (snowy, milky, 1 h bus; 6-pax ryokan rooms available).

### Cards
- **NW-MAT-01 · Castelo de Matsumoto / Matsumoto Castle (grounds + keep)** — grounds free, flat, moat and Alps backdrop; keep ¥700 and 6 floors of steep stairs (not partner). 1.5 h. NY: closed 29–31 Dec; 1–3 Jan 10:00–15:30.
- **NW-MAT-02 · Ruas Nakamachi e Nawate / Nakamachi kura warehouses & Nawate frog street** — flat, cafés, miso and sake shops. Free. 1 h. All. Many shops closed 1–3 Jan.
- **NW-MAT-03 · Shirahone Onsen (banho leitoso) / Shirahone Onsen milky baths** — 1,400 m, deep snow, 1 h bus; day bath ¥1,000 (Awanoyu is mixed-outdoor, famous). Bump 3. Half day or overnight for 6.
- **NW-MAT-04 · Museu de arte Matsumoto (Kusama) / Matsumoto City Museum of Art (Kusama rooms)** — warm indoor hour. ¥410. Closed 29 Dec–3 Jan + Mondays.

## 6. Takayama, Hida, Shirakawa-gō & Gokayama

**Why these dates.** Shirakawa-gō under heavy snow (near-certain in-window; the villages sit in Japan's snow belt) is the postcard of this trip, and you can have it WITHOUT the lottery light-up: a daytime visit on any day, or stay overnight in a gasshō farmhouse (sleep 6 across 2 rooms in a few houses — these are the hardest beds in Japan to get for NY but Dec 20–27 is realistic if you act now). Takayama's old town works year-round, morning markets run even 1 Jan, Hida beef is winter comfort food, and the 1 Jan–3 Jan period is busy-but-open (Nohi bus runs a holiday timetable 29 Dec–3 Jan with some stops skipped — book seats). Gokayama (Ainokura/Suganuma) is the quieter alternative (no in-window light-up: Suganuma 26 Jan, Ainokura 20–21 Feb 2027).

**Honest downsides.** Shirakawa-gō daytime is a theme-park crowd 10:30–15:00 (coaches from Kanazawa/Takayama); the Shiroyama viewpoint is a 15-min uphill path (shuttle bus ¥300 exists — bump-friendly). Snow on Takayama's streets = slush and ice; Takayama Jinya has 20 steps. Hida Folk Village is hilly (bump 2). Light-up: 2027 dates 11/17/24/31 Jan, lottery in Oct 2026 for lodging, parking sold out in minutes — out of window regardless. Takayama→Tokyo is 4–4.5 h via Nagoya (bump 4, 1 change) or 2 h 30 bus to Matsumoto (bump 2).

**Bases for six.** *Takayama* — the best machiya-rental town in the file: Ichika (6), Hanasatomachi Aono Hana (6) / Yukino Hana (7), Hida House; ryokan Iroriyado Hiraya and Oyado Koto no Yume have 6-pax rooms. NY booked-out: high — "popular properties 9–12 months ahead"; still check. *Hida-Furukawa* (15 min by train) — quieter, Yoshiki Stay (7 futons), Yatsusankan ryokan. *Shirakawa-gō* — gasshō minshuku (Kōemon, Yokichi, Magoemon…), 1 night, dinner incl., booked via the village's reservation line / Japanican; NY essentially gone by now, 20–27 Dec possible.

**Transit.** Tokyo→Takayama: Nozomi to Nagoya 1 h 40 + Hida limited express 2 h 20 (bump 4, scenic). Or Tokyo→Toyama (Hokuriku, 2 h 10) + Hida 1 h 30 north route. Takayama↔Shirakawa-gō bus 50 min, ¥2,800, reserve (Nohi; bump 3). Shirakawa-gō↔Kanazawa bus 75 min, ¥2,800, reserve (Hokutetsu). Takayama↔Kanazawa direct 2 h 15. Takayama↔Matsumoto 2 h 30.

**Shapes.** 2 nights (Takayama 2: old town, markets, Hida beef; day trip Shirakawa-gō). 3 nights: Takayama 2 + gasshō overnight 1 (the keeper). 4 nights: + Hida-Furukawa quiet day or Okuhida (Shin-Hotaka ropeway — double-decker to 2,156 m snow deck, bump 4, 1 h bus) — Okuhida is strongly recommended for the parents/brother.

### Cards
- **NW-TAK-01 · Shirakawa-gō de dia na neve / Shirakawa-gō Ogimachi daytime** — gasshō village; Wada house (¥400), Shiroyama viewpoint (shuttle). Bump 4 with shuttle. 3–4 h. No booking except bus. NY: open, very busy 1–3 Jan.
- **NW-TAK-02 · Noite numa casa gasshō / Gasshō farmhouse overnight (minshuku)** — irori hearth dinner, futons under thatch, village empty after 16:00 when coaches leave. ¥12–18k pp with 2 meals; max 2 rooms per house → fits 6. Book 6–12 months; shared bathrooms (bump 3). The single most atmospheric night in this file.

## 7. Kanazawa, Noto/Wajima, Toyama/Himi, Fukui

**Why these dates.** Kanazawa in winter is its best season: Kenroku-en's *yukitsuri* rope-cones (up Nov–mid-Mar), occasional wet snow on the garden, crab (*kōbako/zuwai*) and *buri* at peak, and a compact, flat, bus-served city with the best big-group lodging on the Sea-of-Japan side. Kenroku-en is free and open all night 31 Dec 17:00–1 Jan 8:00 (no lights). Ōmichō market (the food reason to be here) closes 1–4 Jan — plan Kanazawa for 20–30 Dec or 5 Jan+. 21st Century Museum and the samurai/geisha houses close 29 Dec–3 Jan. Toyama Bay's *Himi kan-buri* season has been declared as late as 6 Jan in a warm year; late Dec is usually prime. Fukui got the shinkansen in 2024 (Tokyo→Fukui 2 h 50 direct): Echizen crab (male 6 Nov–20 Mar; female *seiko* only to 31 Dec), Eihei-ji temple in snow (open NY), and the Dinosaur Museum (closed 29 Dec–2 Jan, reservations on busy days).

**Noto/Wajima status (as of Oct 2026).** The Wajima Morning Market operates as a ~30-stall indoor/temporary market at Wajima Wai-Plaza (mornings to 12:00); the original street is an empty lot with rebuilding planned toward 2027. Noto Satoyama Airport reopened July 2026 (Pokémon-themed). Noto Railway and the Wakura Onsen ryokan cluster are partially back; many large ryokan are still closed or rebuilding; roads to the tip (Rokkō, Suzu) are passable but still damaged in places and winter driving on the Noto coast is for confident drivers only. Ishikawa runs "Travel to support Noto" subsidies through Mar 2027. Verdict for this group: a day trip to Wakura Onsen/Wajima from Kanazawa (bus 2 h each way, bump 2) is a meaningful "support" visit but a long, cold day with limited sights; cut unless someone feels strongly. Noto Kongō coast and *senmaida* rice terraces illumination (Aze no Kirameki, runs Oct–Mar at Shiroyone) are the two things worth it.

**Honest downsides.** Kanazawa weather in late Dec = cold rain/sleet most days (the famous "forgets your lunch, don't forget your umbrella"). Kenroku-en snow photos are a lottery. Higashi Chaya is pretty for 40 min then souvenir shops. Geisha-house interiors closed over NY. Kanazawa Station is 2 km from everything (bus/taxi, bump 4).

**Bases for six.** *Kanazawa* — best in file: machiya rentals (Kanazawa has >100 restored machiya for rent; 3-bedroom ones ¥50–90k), apartment hotels (Mimaru Kanazawa, Tokyu Stay, OMO5), connecting-room hotels. ## 11. Fuji area & Izu (Kawaguchiko, Yamanakako, Oshino Hakkai, Hakone, Atami/Shuzenji/Shimoda)

**Why these dates.** Late Dec–early Jan is Fuji's clearest season: snow-capped, visible most mornings (statistically the best visibility of the year). Kawaguchiko has flat lakeside paths, the Mt Fuji Panoramic Ropeway (3 min cabin, bump 5 for the view), Oshino Hakkai's spring ponds with Fuji behind, and plenty of 6-pax rental houses with Fuji views and *kashikiri* baths. Yamanakako: Diamond Fuji (sun setting exactly on the summit) is a Feb event at the lake (window ~18 Jan–20 Feb), so NOT in window — but the Yamanakako shore gives the classic reflection and the "Red Fuji" dawn. Hakone: full loop (railway→cable→ropeway→pirate ship) is bump-friendly and fun; but note the ropeway took maintenance closures 13 Jan–20 Feb 2026 in sections — expect a similar Jan 2027 closure window (check Odakyu's notice ~Nov 2026); Ōwakudani's black eggs. Izu: Atami is the "warm" option (10–14°C, palms), with winter fireworks on 6 Dec and 25 Dec 2026 (20:20–20:40 in the bay — the 25 Dec one is in window), Atami Baien plum garden opens ~mid-Jan (in window at the end: ~9 Jan is early), MOA museum (indoor, Fuji-view escalators). Shuzenji is a 1-h-walk bamboo-path onsen town; Shimoda is far (2 h 40 from Tokyo) and winter-quiet.

**Honest downsides.** Kawaguchiko is Tokyo's weekend playground: 29 Dec–3 Jan buses from Shinjuku sell out and the lake road jams; the Fuji Q Highland crowds. Hakone is extremely busy 31 Dec–3 Jan (hatsumode at Hakone shrine + the Hakone Ekiden marathon relay on 2–3 Jan closes the main road and fills every hotel — avoid Hakone those two days or embrace the spectacle). Hakone lodging for 6 at NY is poor value. Izu is a dead end (return via Tokyo), and in winter it is "pleasant", not "wow".

**Bases.** *Kawaguchiko* — many 6-pax holiday houses (Fuji-view, kitchen, ¥50–90k), Mimaru-style none; ryokan Konansō has 6-pax rooms. NY booked-out: high. *Hakone* — Yumoto big ryokan; houses rare. *Atami* — large resort hotels with 6-pax Japanese-Western rooms (cheap for 6, old-school), Risonare Atami (families).

**Transit.** Shinjuku→Kawaguchiko 2 h by Fuji Excursion limited express (reserved, direct, bump 5) or highway bus 1 h 45. Kawaguchiko→Hakone bus 2 h via Gotemba (bump 3). Shinjuku→Hakone-Yumoto 1 h 25 (Romancecar, bump 5). Tokyo→Atami 45 min (Kodama/Hikari, bump 5); Atami→Shuzenji 50 min (Mishima + Izu-Hakone line); Tokyo→Shimoda 2 h 40 (Odoriko). Kawaguchiko is the natural first or last block.

**Shapes.** 2 nights Kawaguchiko (ropeway, Oshino Hakkai, lakeside, a Fuji-view house night). 3 nights: + Hakone loop (via Gotemba outlets) overnighting Hakone-Yumoto. 4 nights: + Atami 1 (fireworks night 25 Dec, MOA, warm walk) — or swap Hakone for Atami entirely if Hakone's Jan closure/Ekiden dates collide.

## 12. Suggested loop logic (for the planner, not the catalog)

Two coherent loops fit 20 nights with ≤3 h moving days and one fixed 4-night NY block:

**Loop A — Hokuriku/Alps (recommended for mixed bump + ski):** Tokyo 2 (arrival) → Kawaguchiko 2 → Nagano 1 → Nozawa 2 (ski day) → Shibu/Nagano 1 → Kanazawa 4 (29 Dec–2 Jan: NY block, machiya with kitchen; Himi buri day; Fukui day) → Shirakawa-gō gasshō 1 → Takayama 2 → Matsumoto 1 → Tokyo 3. Moving days all ≤3 h except Takayama→Tokyo (4 h; split via Matsumoto). Snow likelihood high in the middle third; Fuji clear at the start.

**Loop B — Tōhoku (recommended if snow scenery outranks food/ryokan luxury):** Tokyo 2 → Nikkō/Kinugawa 2 → Aizu/Ōuchi-juku 1 → Sendai 2 (Pageant until 27 Dec; Matsushima) → Yamagata/Zaō 2 (ropeway light-up from ~27 Dec; ski day) → Ginzan 1 (Ginzanso) → Morioka/Nyūtō 2 → Oirase Keiryū 2 (NY block, big hotel, ice falls) → Hachinohe/Sendai → Tokyo 3. Colder, longer transfers, more "wow-white", fewer 6-pax houses.

Hybrid: Loop A plus a Sendai-Zaō 3-night add-on after Tokyo at the end (Tokyo→Sendai 1 h 30) if the snow-monster night is wanted.
