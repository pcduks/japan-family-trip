# Critic personas: the international accounts

Research date: 18 Oct 2026. Covers @migrationology, @pete_wells, @kenjilopezalt, @fuchsiadunlop, @jwang815, @little_meg_siu_meg and @hitode47.

**Method and limits.**
- Instagram feeds need a login, so I did not read them. I did use what Instagram serves to logged-out visitors: the public profile embed (`instagram.com/<handle>/embed/`), which carries the display name, follower and post counts and the latest ~6 posts with captions, plus `og:` metadata. These are cited below as "IG embed, fetched 18 Oct 2026".
- Restaurant and place names for Mark Wiens come from his own blog (through the Migrationology WordPress API, which also gives publish dates) and from the descriptions of his YouTube videos, which list every stop.
- Pete Wells' NYT reviews are confirmed through Wikipedia citations, because nytimes.com blocks automated fetches. Kenji's Patreon articles are paywalled, so for him I have titles and dates only.
- The session's web-search budget ran out partway through. Kenji's 2010s Serious Eats coverage of Japan and any Japan writing by Wells or Dunlop in other outlets were never searched. A follow-up session could fill those gaps.

**The surprises (read these first):**
1. **@jwang815 is NOT Jason Wang of Xi'an Famous Foods.** He is a different Jason Wang: a former Silicon Valley tech executive, co-founder of the expedition company Waypoint Journeys, who has visited 191 of 193 UN countries and climbed the Seven Summits. He also runs the food-photo account @cityfoodsters with his partner Grace.
2. **@little_meg_siu_meg is Margaret Lam, a Hong Kong-linked writer now based in Kyoto.** She is a high-end Japanese dining insider (165K followers, 24K posts). She logs thousands of kakigori a year and wrote for Noma's Noma Kyoto magazine. She is the most Japan-relevant voice on the whole list.
3. **@hitode47 is almost certainly 田代 秀人 (Hideto Tashiro), the chef of 鈴田式 (Suzutashiki).** Suzutashiki is a wood-fire (薪焼き) washoku counter in Nishi-Azabu, Tokyo. This makes him a chef, not a critic.
4. Mark Wiens' 2017 "Best of Tokyo" tour was run by **The Hungry Tourist** (David Califa), which is also the @we.are.hungry.tourists account on the user's list (see `10-critics-brazil.md`).

## 1. @migrationology: Mark Wiens

### Identity
- American food-and-travel vlogger, born 26 Feb 1986 in Phoenix, based in Bangkok with his Thai wife Ying and son Micah. Blogging since 2009 (Migrationology.com), on YouTube full-time since 2012. He co-owns Phed Mark (Bangkok, phat kaphrao) and hosted Nat Geo's *Epic Food Journeys* (2024) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Wiens)).
- The handle is confirmed: his video descriptions link `instagram.com/migrationology` ([e.g. Takayama video, 30 Nov 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7dYaVC-5LmE)). IG embed: "Mark Wiens", 2,674,124 followers. He also runs a second channel, "Mark Wiens Abroad".

### Palate and spirit
- **Hunts for local, crowded and family-run places.** Quote: "One of the best ways to find great restaurants when traveling is to just look for a place that's busy with lots of locals eating" ([Tokyo guide, 27 Mar 2014](https://migrationology.com/tokyo-travel-guide-for-food-lovers/)). He praises "a small Uncle and Aunty family run seafood rice bowl restaurant" ([Sapporo, 2019](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l35cs9_Pb-M)) and Okinawa's family seafood huts.
- **Values lunch for money.** Quote: "I made it my priority to have lunch be our main meal – sometimes I even ate two or three lunches." He queues 15–30 minutes before opening.
- **Likes big flavour and chilli.** Quote: "I'm a spicy kind of guy … I love things like raw garlic and chilies", yet he calls sashimi "the purest of all foods" ([Best meals 2014](https://migrationology.com/21-best-meals-ate-2014/)). He seeks out the spiciest ramen (Kikanbo, Lashowhan) and orders soup curry at level 40.
- **Rates on rice, meat, seafood, freshness and the cook.** He prefers rice-and-protein meals; okonomiyaki is "not my favorite" ([Sometaro, 2014](https://migrationology.com/okonomiyaki-in-tokyo/)). Toyama black ramen is "a bit intense, not my favorite" ([2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9f8vJwYLgCY)). Ichiran "may not be the best bowl of tonkotsu ramen you'll eat when you're in Fukuoka" but is a fun experience ([2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bBD8LwbJB3E)).
- **Discloses comped meals.** He writes "NOTE: I did not pay for the food in this video" (the Hungry Tourist tour, 2017) and noted that his Narisawa meal was sponsored by Chowzter.
- **Tone:** relentlessly enthusiastic, generous and never cruel. He gives prices for everything.
- **Would never recommend:** places that are mainly tourist theatre without flavour, or sterile chains when a family shop is next door. His negatives are mild ("wasn't my favorite", "quality is not the best, but it is quite budget").

### How to channel Mark Wiens
Ask: *Is this where locals actually eat, and is there a queue of Japanese customers? Is it family-run, with a cook you can watch? Is it the place that defines this city's signature dish (yatai in Fukuoka, kushikatsu in Shinsekai, jibu-ni in Kanazawa, Hida beef in Takayama)? Is the lunch set a steal compared with dinner? Can we eat it at a counter, standing bar or market stall? Does it taste bold, generous and fresh?*
**Veto:** tourist-trap "experiences" whose food is mediocre; overpriced places where a ¥1,200 lunch set elsewhere is better; chains when a legendary independent is nearby (he will still try a flagship, Ichiran, as an experience but say so); anything bland or timid. He rarely vetoes on price alone if the quality is "mind-blowing" (Obana, Sushi Arai), but he flags when a meal was comped.
For this family: his Takayama, Kanazawa, Toyama and Kyushu lists map directly onto the candidate regions.

- **High** on identity and on every entry above (taken from his own blog and video descriptions).
- **Not covered:** YouTube channel search returns a capped set, so a handful of Japan videos could be missing. The Kobe beef bento video gives no shop name. I did not open the full text of every 2015 Kyoto description.

## 2. @pete_wells: Pete Wells

### Identity
- Peter Andrew Wells (b. 1963). New York Times dining editor from 2006 and chief restaurant critic from Jan 2012 to Aug 2024. His farewell essay was "I Reviewed Restaurants for 12 Years. They've Changed, and Not for the Better" (6 Aug 2024). He has won six James Beard awards ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Wells)).
- IG embed: "Pete Wells", 101,018 followers, 486 posts. His Jan 2026 caption refers to "my latest piece", so he is still writing.

### Palate and spirit
- **Value and honesty above prestige.** He took down blue-chip names: Per Se cut to two stars (2016, the menu "random and purposeless"), Peter Luger at zero stars (2019), and the Guy Fieri review (2012), which Forbes called "the most scathing review in the history of the New York Times" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Wells)).
- **Rewards obsessive single-genre craft.** In Japanese food his top marks went to small, chef-driven counters. Quote: Kono's chef is "the city's most accomplished yakitori chef and, by extension, one of its greatest chicken cooks" (3 stars, 25 Jul 2022, via [Wikipedia: Kono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kono_(restaurant))).
