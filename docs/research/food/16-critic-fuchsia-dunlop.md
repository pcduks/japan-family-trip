# 16 — Critic deep-dive: Fuchsia Dunlop (@fuchsiadunlop, 扶霞 / 邓扶霞)

Research date: 18 Oct 2026. Follows `CRITIC-DIVE-BRIEF.md` and `BRIEF.md`. Instagram was not opened. Instagram captions quoted below come from search-engine snippets (DuckDuckGo index) and are marked as such. Tabelog data was fetched from the Tabelog pages on 18 Oct 2026.

**Headline:** Dunlop has been to Japan, but only briefly. She says her first trip was in 2018. She has named **one** restaurant in Japan in public: **私厨房 勇 (Shichubo Yung, "Yung's"), Shirokane, Tokyo**. She praises it at length in *Invitation to a Banquet* and called it "absolutely superb" on *Conversations with Tyler*. It is a Tabelog 3.86 Chinese 百名店, so her taste and local diners agree here. Everything else in this file applies her stated principles to Tabelog-validated Chinese restaurants. Those picks are labelled as **lens picks, not Dunlop visits**.

---

## 1. Who

- English cook and food writer, raised in Oxford and based in London. She has specialised in Chinese food since the 1990s. She read English at Cambridge, then went to Sichuan University on a British Council scholarship in 1994, and was the first Westerner to train at the Sichuan Higher Institute of Cuisine in Chengdu. She is a fluent Chinese speaker. Sources: [fuchsiadunlop.com/about](https://www.fuchsiadunlop.com/about), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuchsia_Dunlop).
- **Books:** *Sichuan Cookery* / *Land of Plenty* (2001), *Revolutionary Chinese Cookbook* (Hunan, 2007), *Shark's Fin and Sichuan Pepper* (memoir, 2008), *Every Grain of Rice* (2012), *Land of Fish and Rice* (Jiangnan, 2016), *The Food of Sichuan* (2019), *Invitation to a Banquet: The Story of Chinese Food* (2023), and *Five Tastes: The Magic of Flavour in a Chinese Kitchen* (UK Sept 2026, US Oct 2026; [book page](https://www.fuchsiadunlop.com/books/five-tastes)).
- **Awards:** four James Beard Awards, Fortnum & Mason Cookbook of the Year 2024, Guild of Food Writers Food Writer of the Year 2022, André Simon Award 2017, IACP Jane Grigson Award 2009, and an honorary fellowship at Magdalene College, Cambridge (2026) ([about page](https://www.fuchsiadunlop.com/about)).
- **Journalism:** FT Weekend, The New Yorker, Observer Food Monthly, Guardian, Lucky Peach, Saveur, The Economist. She now also writes for the Scribehound newsletter.
- **Reach:** Instagram about 105k followers and about 7,967 posts (search snippet of the [profile](https://www.instagram.com/fuchsiadunlop/)). X has about 23k followers; she is also on Bluesky. She is a celebrity in China, where the press calls her "the English person with a Chinese stomach" ([Guardian long read, Nov 2025](https://www.theguardian.com/food/2025/nov/20/how-fuchsia-dunlop-became-a-sichuan-food-hero)).
- **Other roles:** she has led WildChina gastronomic tours (Beijing/Shanghai May 2027, Yunnan Sept 2026; [WildChina](https://wildchina.com/tour/gastronomic-tour-of-china-with-fuchsia-dunlop-2026/)). In London she consulted for Barshu (Sichuan), Baozi Inn and Bashan (Hunan) ([Restaurant Online 2012](https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Article/2012/07/06/Fuchsia-Dunlop-interview)).
- No earlier profile of her exists in this repo: `11-critics-international.md` is not present.

## 2. Her palate in her own words

| # | Principle | Quote / close paraphrase | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Texture (kougan 口感) is central** | "the Chinese totally appreciate and understand texture to a degree that is unheard of anywhere else… That's why they enjoy eating so many slithery, rubbery, and also often tasteless foods that have interesting textures." | [Conversations with Tyler ep. 199, Dec 2023](https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/fuchsia-dunlop-3/) |
| 2 | Texture is a skill you learn | "once you appreciate texture, the possibilities of ingredients expand because you can eat things that are more complicated in the mouth, like a chicken's foot, or which have no flavor, like jellyfish." | [Radio Ca' Foscari interview (PDF)](https://unive.it/web/fileadmin/user_upload/comunicazione/radiocafoscari/doc/Incontri_ravvicinati_Intervista_Fuchsia_Dunlop.pdf) |
| 3 | **MSG: not evil, but a crutch** | The book calls the case against MSG "totally debunked". She also warns that "its hammer-headed umami assault blunts the palate, deadening its sensitivity to more subtle flavours." | *Invitation to a Banquet* (Google Books search-inside, ch. "Tasting the Invisible") |
| 3b | MSG in her own cooking | "Pretty much every Chinese kitchen… will have a jar of MSG tucked away somewhere. I don't find it a necessary product myself – I buy good ingredients and make my own stocks." | [Restaurant Online, 2012](https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Article/2012/07/06/Fuchsia-Dunlop-interview) |
| 3c | Stock over powder | Of Dai Jianjun at Dragon Well Manor, Hangzhou, whose kitchen "insists that his chefs use stock and traditional seasonings rather than MSG". | [Cathay Pacific, by Dunlop, 29 May 2024](https://www.cathaypacific.com/cx/en_GB/inspiration/dining/fuchsia-dunlop-favourite-restaurants-china.html) |
| 4 | **"Chinese food abroad" is a simplified offshoot** | "a simplified, adapted, even bastardized form of Cantonese cuisine, first developed in North America and then scattered like confetti all over the world… While western consumers are willing to pay exorbitant sums for sushi or European tasting menus, Chinese restaurateurs still struggle to persuade customers that fine Chinese food is worth its price." | *Invitation to a Banquet*, prologue pp. 5–6 ([Penguin sample PDF](https://cdn.penguin.co.uk/dam-assets/books/9780141997216/9780141997216-sample.pdf)) |
| 5 | Japan's prestige versus China's | "prestige food is often about money. Japan got rich first. Japanese food is very prestigious. People will spend loads of money on Japanese sushi, but not so much on Chinese food." | [CWT ep. 199](https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/fuchsia-dunlop-3/) |
| 6 | Japanese cuisine has Chinese roots | "Many of the core techniques and preparations of Japanese cuisine, now so esteemed abroad, originated in China, including sushi, tofu, tea, soy sauce and ramen." | *Invitation to a Banquet* p. 13–14 ([sample PDF](https://cdn.penguin.co.uk/dam-assets/books/9780141997216/9780141997216-sample.pdf)) |
| 7 | **How to order** | "You need to communicate that you do not want to eat General Tso's chicken… you really want to eat the food that they're going to have for dinner tonight themselves… One great mistake you can make is just order all dishes which are deep-fried or red-braised… You always want to have some lighter dishes." | [CWT ep. 15, Nov 2016](https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/fuchsia-dunlop/) |
| 7b | Group ordering | "In a group in a Chinese restaurant, benevolent dictatorship is always best." If each person orders what they fancy, "the result will be a lopsided menu". | *Invitation to a Banquet* (search-inside, ch. "Fire and Time") |
| 8 | Michelin and tasting menus are a poor fit | "Michelin… is not very well equipped to judge Chinese food"; "the way you should eat is with a group and having a good variety of dishes." | [CWT bonus, Nov 2019](https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/fuchsia-dunlop-2/) |
| 9 | Where to look | "I would look at where Chinese students are eating, partly, so then you'll get the more recent trends in China." | [CWT ep. 199](https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/fuchsia-dunlop-3/) |
| 10 | Hidden menus | "A lot of Chinese restaurants hide away the interesting dishes." | [Restaurant Online 2012](https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Article/2012/07/06/Fuchsia-Dunlop-interview) |

**Other judging threads, paraphrased from her books:** she values *benwei* / *yuanwei* (the "root" or original flavour of a good ingredient), seasonality and terroir. A meal should balance bold dishes with plain ones. She praises precision of cutting and of heat ("Fire and Time"). The title *Shark's Fin and Sichuan Pepper* reflects her ambivalence about luxury and endangered ingredients. She is sceptical of "authenticity" as a fixed idea ("who owns culture and what is authentic… it's really complicated", [Ndo Better podcast, Aug 2023](https://www.thendobetter.com/arts/2023/8/28/fuchsia-dunlop-chinese-cuisine-history-philosophy-podcast)), but she insists on regional specificity.

## 5. Japan tips (hers, or derived from her stated rules)

She has given no Japan-specific practical tips. Her general Chinese-dining rules transfer well:
- **Order as a group, and let one person compose the meal.** "Benevolent dictatorship is always best." Balance one rich or deep-fried dish with a soup, a plain green vegetable, a steamed dish and rice. ([book](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/443155/invitation-to-a-banquet-by-dunlop-fuchsia/9780141997216); [CWT 2016](https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/fuchsia-dunlop/))
- **Ask for what the kitchen would eat, not the tourist menu.** "A lot of Chinese restaurants hide away the interesting dishes." In Japan this means choosing the chef's omakase/course or asking about seasonal dishes rather than the ebi-chili/mabo standard menu.
- **Value the vegetable course.** At Yung's the dish she wrote about was essentially scallops and mixed vegetables.
- **Don't expect Japanese Chinese to beat Chengdu.** She did not rebut Cowen's view that Tokyo's starred Chinese restaurants are "very good… but… middling" next to Chengdu ([CWT 2023](https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/fuchsia-dunlop-3/)). She called her own impression "superficial", and her one meal was "superb".
- **Shark's fin:** her memoir title points to her discomfort with it. Several Japanese high-end Chinese restaurants feature it (Es Sawada, Chikushirō). Order around it.

## 6. Tabelog cross-check

### 6a. Her one Japan restaurant

### 私厨房 勇 / Shichubo Yung ("Yung's")
- Shirokane 6-5-5, Minato; Shirokanedai Stn (724 m) / Shirokane-Takanawa (10 min) · Chinese private kitchen (私房菜), counter · Dinner ¥10–15k listed, reviews suggest ¥15–20k
- Tabelog: **3.86 (312 reviews)** · https://tabelog.com/tokyo/A1316/A131602/13168401/ · Awards: **Tabelog Award Bronze 2017–2023**; **中国料理 TOKYO 百名店 2021, 2023, 2024, 2026**
- Japanese expert backing: a 2020 DIME article (dime.jp) describes Yung (opened 2014) as a chef's-table 私房菜 that helped set the Tokyo trend for counter-style Chinese. Chef: 原 (Hara), who trained under a well-known Cantonese owner-chef in Kashiwa (from the Tabelog listing and search snippets; not independently verified).
- Trusted-foodie trace: **Fuchsia Dunlop**, *Invitation to a Banquet* p. 265 and [CWT ep. 199](https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/fuchsia-dunlop-3/)
- Reservations: reservation only, online (phone closed). 10 seats; **a group of 6 takes 6 of the 10 seats**, so book early or ask about a private booking.
- Pregnancy: a cooked Chinese course is broadly safe. Ask them to skip raw or drunken shellfish and any raw-fish appetiser.
- Dec–Jan: open Tue–Sun 18:00–22:00, closed Mon. New Year closure not listed.
- Verdict: **confirmed by both sides.** Dunlop loved it, and Japanese diners rate it highly.

## 7. Persona card (≤150 words)

**Channel Fuchsia Dunlop:** "Is this a *regional* cuisine I can name, cooked with stock rather than a jar of MSG?" She looks for precise heat and texture (kougan): each ingredient cooked to its own point, slippery against crisp. She wants plain greens and soup to balance the rich dishes, and she orders for the table, family style. **Vetoes:** shark's fin, menus full of sweet-sour and deep-fried dishes, "ebi-chili for foreigners", and tasting-menu theatre that stops the table sharing. **Sample verdict:** "It may have looked like scallops and mixed vegetables, but it was an astonishing work of engineering. This is Chinese cooking with real sensibility." **To this family:** "Chinese food is your friend this trip. Nearly everything is cooked, so it suits a pregnant woman far better than a sushi counter. Book a round table, let one person order, and include a steamed fish, a soup and a dish of greens. Skip drunken crab (raw and boozy) and anything raw."
