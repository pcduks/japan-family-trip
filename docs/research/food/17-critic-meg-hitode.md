## Part 1. @little_meg_siu_meg: Margaret Lam ("Little Meg", 小Meg)

### 1. Who
- **Identity confirmed.** Her OpenRice HK reviewer page, "Little Meg", says "My Instagram: little_meg_siu_meg" and gives her Weibo as 小瑪姬Little-Meg ([OpenRice via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/2023/http://littlemeg.openrice.com/)). The IG `og:title` is "Margaret Lam (@little_meg_siu_meg)" (per file 11, and Google's index of the profile). Departures once called her "Margaret Siu"; that is probably a slip from the handle. I treat "Lam" as correct.
- **Background, in her own words on OpenRice:** "After living in Canada and the US for the last 15 years, I am returning to my beloved Hong Kong. Having lived in Manhattan for my last 7 years…". She lists her favourite cuisines as "Guangdong, Zhejiang, Japanese, Contemporary French & Italian, and New American". She wrote 130 OpenRice reviews, 55 of them Editor's Choice, mostly in 2010–2012 ([OpenRice EN review list, Wayback 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20140417083811/http://littlemeg.openrice.com:80/english/home/reviews.htm?lang=en&con=rvwer)).
- **Base:**

  - Now: Kyoto. Her bio reads "Currently in 🇯🇵. Kyoto based. Moved to Japan for 🍣 Stayed in Japan for #kakigori🍧" and logs 2,086 to 2,613 kakigōri a year for 2016–2019 (per file 11).

### 2. Palate in her own words
1. **Judge a kitchen by its dashi; simplicity is the hardest thing.** "They make simplicity not simple at all… You can hide imperfection in a sauce. But here, a single piece of grilled yam can be mind-blowing." She explained that a Japanese kitchen is judged by its dashi ([Departures, Christine Muhlke, 25 Nov 2020](https://web.archive.org/web/2020/https://www.departures.com/lifestyle/food/tokyo-new-young-chefs-update-modern-japanese-cuisine)).
2. **Follows young chefs out of Tokyo.** She is "now following young chefs to the cities of Kyoto, Kanazawa, and Fukuoka". She describes a trend of young chefs opening early because top counters are impossible to book (same Departures piece).
3. **Everyday food is the real test.** As Chang paraphrases her: "the best way to judge a food culture is not by the best restaurants, but it's by the places that are not quote unquote 'the best,' convenience stores, cheap eats, etc." ([CNN/KVIA, 2026](https://kvia.com/entertainment/cnn-style/2026/09/20/eat-the-dodgy-sandwich-says-chef-david-chang/)).
4. **Price-blind, and honest about mediocrity.** "I enjoy food that is cooked with heart, regardless of price… from hole-in-the-wall to fine dining." On 22 Ships (HK): "Gambas… Too ordinary, too predictable in taste." On a burger: "if you don't tell me I wouldn't know it was iberico pork" ([OpenRice, Oct 2012](https://web.archive.org/web/20140417083811/http://littlemeg.openrice.com:80/english/home/reviews.htm?lang=en&con=rvwer)).
5. **Offal and the unusual.** "from meat and seafood to offals and the unusual!" She calls herself "a BIG fan of sweetbreads" and an "egg fanatic" (OpenRice bio and reviews).
6. **Salt and seasoning discipline.** On 麵鮮醬油房周月 (HK ramen) her review headline reads 「有湯的拉麵, 真的鹹到 X…」: the soup ramen is far too salty (OpenRice home page, Wayback).
7. **She repeats and tracks seasons.** "Jimbocho Den, Tokyo (9th visit, 9.1.2015)" ([Pinterest copy of her IG caption, Dec 2015](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/little_meg_siu_meg-on-instagram-anago-shiso-beetroot-sandwich-jimbocho-den-tokyo-9th-visit-912015-from-the-last-visit-whic--11540542773599736/)). Per file 11: "I visit these restaurants regularly and have eaten all 12 seasons".
8. **Diner ethics and omotenashi.** Per file 11, she calls "customers are always right" "completely wrong" in a Japanese omakase context, and notices a pine needle placed under a glass at Ogata.
9. **Kakigōri purism.** Her ideal is "just ice and high quality simple syrup… the highest and purest form of kakigori" (per file 11).

### 5. Japan tips (hers)
- **Go outside Tokyo.** Young chefs in Kyoto, Kanazawa and Fukuoka are where the excitement is (Departures, 2020).
- **Judge Japanese food by dashi and restraint, not sauce.** Same source.
- **Don't skip convenience stores and cheap eats.** They are the real measure of a food culture (Chang quoting her, CNN 2026).
- **Omakase etiquette.** Trust the chef. Don't treat omakase as made-to-order. Be "appreciative, empathetic, kind, respectful, and humble" (per file 11, IG Jan 2024).
- **Go back.** She revisits a counter across seasons (Den "9th visit"; Ogata "all 12 seasons"). Pick one place and return rather than collecting trophies.
- Nothing found from her on depachika, booking mechanics or queues. Unverified.

Fill the rest with kakigōri, konbini and depachika, because that's how you really judge Japan. Your parents will love a quiet room more than a busy counter."

## Part 2. @hitode47: 田代 秀人 (Hideto Tashiro), chef of 鈴田式 Suzutashiki

### 1. Who
- **Identity: confirmed.** Google's index of the Instagram profile reads "田代 秀人 (@hitode47)". The indexed bio describes him as the chef of 「SUZUTASHIKI」(鈴田式), with reservation enquiries through OMAKASE: about 3,871 followers and 1,044 posts at indexing time. File 11 counted 3,965 and 1,049. Guests tag him as co-author on posts at 鈴田式 (per file 11). The handle reads as Hi(de)to → ヒトデ (starfish), plus 47.
- **He is a chef, not a critic or a Tabelog reviewer.** I found no Tabelog reviewer page, note.com, Ameblo or YouTube under this handle.
- **Career** ([Hitosara chef profile](https://hitosara.com/0020007533/)):
  - Born 1987 in Tokyo; his father and uncle were washoku cooks.
  - Trained from age 15 at his uncle's 左京ひがしやま in Ginza (now closed).
  - Then teppan-French cooking, a three-month Mediterranean cruise as private chef, then kappō and bistro work.
  - Joined 鈴田式 in 2019.
  - Other profiles add head chef of 東京十月 (Aoyama) for four years and chef of GINZA NARASHIBA cuisine (sake pairing) from 2018 (TasteLink / Primechef, via search snippets).
- **The restaurant:**
  - The name joins 鈴野翔吾 and 田代秀人. It is a new concept from the yakiniku house 肉匠堀越 ([Takemachelin, Sep 2019](https://www.takemachelin.com/2019/09/suzuta.html)).
  - It opened in Azabu-Jūban, closed there for redevelopment, and reopened at Nishi-Azabu 1-9-5 on 9 Jan 2024, with a larger room and two wood ovens (Hitosara).

### 2. Palate in his own words (few direct quotes exist)
- On the move: 「一段と焼きの精度があがりました」 ("the precision of the grilling has gone up a notch") (Hitosara).
- **Signatures and method** (Hitosara; Goethe, Keiko Kodera, [2 Jun 2020](https://goetheweb.jp/gourmet/article/20200602-suzutashiki)):
  - Wood-grilled shiitake (Iwate ふくよ香) stuffed with fermented-shiitake paste, salted and vacuum-aged for a week.
  - Kuroge wagyu fillet 飯蒸し with a fermented onion 醤.
  - Wood-smoked donabe rice: a firebrand goes into the pot. One version has crab and morels; the nodoguro version is the one Akimoto Yasushi ate "six bowls" of.
  - Smoked ice cream.
- **A Japanese blogger's view.** Takemachelin calls it 「自由奔放に美味しいお店」, a free-spirited, delicious place. It has no classic hassun or plain sashimi, and is "accessible to beginners yet engaging for veterans". He rated the wild Nagara eel above うなぎ時任 ([Takemachelin](https://www.takemachelin.com/2019/09/suzuta.html)).
- His guests' captions add 6-year kombu, 3-day cold-brewed dashi, aged potatoes and hand-cut soba (per file 11). These are the guests' words, not his.

### 7. Persona card: Chef Tashiro (the cook's eye)
He asks: *Where is the fish from and how was it killed? What is aged or fermented, and for how long? Is the fire doing real work (smoke, char, aroma in the rice), or is it decoration? Is the dashi drawn fresh? Does the menu move month to month?*
**Veto:** gas grilling sold as "charcoal", shortcut dashi, anonymous produce, static menus.
**Sample verdict (imagined, in his register):** "The smoke should reach the nose before the tongue; here the ayu does that."
**To this family:** "Book the six-seat room and tell us about the pregnancy. Fire cooking is perfect for that: everything can be cooked through and still taste alive, and your parents can sit comfortably away from the counter."
