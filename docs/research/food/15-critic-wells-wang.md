**Method notes.**

## Part 1. @pete_wells: Pete Wells

### 1. Who
- **Pete Wells** (b. 1963) was NYT dining editor from 2006 and chief restaurant critic from Jan 2012 to Aug 2024. He has six James Beard awards. He stepped down in July 2024 over health (cholesterol, blood sugar, hypertension) and stayed at the Times ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Wells)).
- **Since leaving the critic's job:** he writes for the NYT Food section as a reporter. His bio on the Sushi Sho piece (9 Dec 2024) reads "Pete Wells was the restaurant critic for The Times from 2012 until 2024". In January 2026 he wrote a series on how he changed his eating habits after a health crisis ("Our Former Restaurant Critic Changed His Eating Habits. You Can, Too."; The Daily episode "To Save His Life, Our Food Critic Reset His Appetite", Mar 2026; [search trace](https://www.newedgetimes.com/our-former-restaurant-critic-changed-his-eating-habits-you-can-too/), [podcast listing](https://poderato.com/shows/the-daily/the-sunday-daily-to-save-his-life-our-food-critic-reset-his-appetite)). In 2026 he appeared on Feed Me's *Expense Account* podcast, whose show notes quote him: "Restaurants don't need The New York Times — anyone who has a phone can get their message out" ([Feed Me, 14 Jan 2026](https://www.readfeedme.com/p/pete-wells)). He also spoke at a Hamptons "Stirring the Pot" talk (31 Aug 2025) ([listing](https://jameslanepost.com/hamptons-event-calendar/stirring-the-pot-florence-fabricant-pete-wells)).
- **Book or newsletter:** none found. Instagram @pete_wells has ~101K followers; see `11-critics-international.md` for its Jan 2026 Pizza Studio Tamaki post and the May 2026 Yamada co-post.
- **Successor context:** Yamada's 4-star NYT review (2025) was by Ligaya Mishan, not Wells ([Reporter Gourmet, 23 Dec 2025](https://reportergourmet.com/en/news/9573-yamada-just-one-chef-for-10-seats-the-newest-top-rated-japanese-restaurant-according-to-the-nyt)).

### 2. His palate, in his own words (Japanese reviews)
All quotes are from the NYT reviews as archived on Wayback. The links go to the NYT URL.
1. **Rice is the tell.** At Ichimura: "our first taste of the rice — warm, fragrant and assertive … Even the rice, seasoned with a blend of three vinegars, is unusually assertive; it may ruin other sushi for you" ([Ichimura at Brushstroke, 25 Sep 2012, ★★★](https://www.nytimes.com/2012/09/26/dining/reviews/restaurant-review-ichimura-at-brushstroke-in-tribeca.html)). At Yoshino: "The rice will be just sticky enough to hold together for a few seconds on its way to your mouth. It will be just tart enough that you can sense the presence of vinegar without quite tasting it" ([Yoshino, 15 Nov 2022, ★★★★](https://www.nytimes.com/2022/11/15/dining/yoshino-review-pete-wells-four-stars.html)). At Shion: "The rice is soft and not overbearingly tart" ([30 Nov 2021](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/11/30/dining/shion-69-leonard-street-review-sushi-nyc.html)). At Kappo Masa: "The rice was a gummy clump."
2. **Temperature, curing and each piece as its own event.** On Nakazawa: "Each slice has a slightly different temperature … Mr. Nakazawa seems to be able to hit any point on the thermometer with an assassin's aim." And: "Then he picked up a palmful of rice and began to serve one of the four most enjoyable and eye-opening sushi meals I have ever eaten. I had the other three at Sushi Nakazawa over the next few weeks" ([Sushi Nakazawa, 10 Dec 2013, ★★★★](https://www.nytimes.com/2013/12/11/dining/reviews/restaurant-review-sushi-nakazawa-in-the-west-village.html)).
3. **Price must match pleasure; he will zero a luxury name.** "The cost of eating at Kappo Masa is so brutally, illogically, relentlessly high, and so out of proportion to any pleasure you may get … Stars I might have given Kappo Masa if the prices were, say, 20 percent lower: one. Stars I am giving it: zero." Also: "a pantomime of service without the substance" ([Kappo Masa, 7 Jan 2015, Satisfactory = no stars](https://www.nytimes.com/2015/01/07/dining/restaurant-review-kappo-masa-on-the-upper-east-side.html)).
4. **Seasonality must be real.** "I doubted a tempura chef in Japan would bother frying asparagus when pumpkin was in season … I never wanted more tempura, though; I wanted more interesting tempura" ([Tempura Matsui, 17 Nov 2015, ★★](https://www.nytimes.com/2015/11/18/dining/tempura-matsui-murray-hill-review.html)). At Kajitsu, the dishes "are drawn from the season, but they are also drawings of the season" ([Kajitsu, 18 Jun 2013, ★★](https://www.nytimes.com/2013/06/19/dining/reviews/restaurant-review-kajitsu-in-murray-hill.html)).
5. **Minimalism is unforgiving.** "With minimalist cooking, the distance between dull and delicious can sometimes be measured in grains of salt." He also flags the bargain: "the package of seven chicken skewers plus three vegetables is a good deal at $65 … to experience Torishin you don't need to spend $150" ([Torishin, 11 Apr 2017, ★★★, Critic's Pick](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/04/11/dining/torishin-review-japanese-yakitori.html)).
6. **Craft he can see.** On Kono: "the aesthetic pleasure you get from the work of people with a fine-grained command of a craft … chicken is not one ingredient but many ingredients" ([Kono, 25 Jul 2022, ★★★, Critic's Pick](https://www.nytimes.com/2022/07/25/dining/kono-chinatown-review.html)).
7. **The chef is the restaurant.** "The success of such a meal depends almost entirely on the chef's ability … the problem with trying to open a branch of a restaurant whose fame rests on the touch of one chef" ([Torien, 13 Jul 2021, unrated during the pandemic](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/07/13/dining/torien-review.html)).
8. **Elite sushi as a class problem.** "The city finally has sushi that aspires to stand with the greatest in the world, but eating it has become a rich person's game" (Shion). On Yoshino at $646: "I can't tell you whether you, personally, should spend it on a single dinner."
9. **Value comes in many forms.** At Odo: "You could tell yourself that $200 is a large amount of money for a single meal or you could say that for the chance to visit all these different establishments without leaving your seat, it's a pretty good deal" ([Odo, 19 Mar 2019](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/03/19/dining/odo-restaurant-review.html)). On ramen: "The weightiest, richest possible broth is not necessarily the ramen ideal" ([Mu Ramen, 6 May 2015](https://www.nytimes.com/2015/05/06/dining/restaurant-review-mu-ramen-in-long-island-city-queens.html)).
10. **Ethics.** "I don't accept free meals from restaurants I write about" ([100 Best, 2024](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2024/dining/best-nyc-restaurants.html)).

**What he values, in short.** Rice first: warm, seasoned, and a match for the fish. Then the chef's personal touch on each piece (temperature, curing, knife work), seasonality that is real rather than decorative, and service that is warm without being theatrical. Value is judged as pleasure per dollar, not absolute price: he gave four stars at $646, and no stars to Kappo Masa's $78 steak.

### 5. Japan tips (transferable from his reviews)
- **Read the sign:** "An iron law of sushi holds that the more impressive the restaurant, the smaller the sign" (Sushi Sho, 2024).
- **"Edomae" is not a marketing word:** in Tokyo it means fish cured with kelp, salt and vinegar, the 19th-century methods "that also deepened their flavor"; some masters season "three batches of rice with varying amounts and types of vinegar" for different fish (Sushi Sho, 2024).
- **Yakitori etiquette:** grated daikon "plays the same role in a yakitori counter that pickled ginger plays at a sushi bar"; the soy-dashi pot is for the daikon, not the chicken (Torien, 2021).
- **Order smart at yakitori:** the set or à-la-carte skewers can be the better value; you "don't need to spend $150" (Torishin). At Kono, ask the chef which supplement to take.
- **Be wary of out-of-season produce** at a tempura or kaiseki counter (Tempura Matsui), and of omakase that peaks before the nigiri (Yoshino on Masa).
- **Branches are not the original:** "taking Shion Uino away from Sushi Saito … did not mean that his restaurant was as good as Sushi Saito" (Torien).

### 7. Persona card: Pete Wells (≤150 words)
Asks: *How's the rice: warm, seasoned, matched to each fish? Does every piece change temperature and texture on purpose? Is the season on the plate, or is it decoration? Is the price in proportion to the pleasure? Does the chef cook, or perform?*
Vetoes: luxury pantomime (gold, truffle, caviar piled on), out-of-season produce, gummy rice, upsold garnishes, branches trading on a famous name.
Sample verdict: "The tuna was fine; the rice was the problem. At ¥40,000, a counter has no excuse for shari that sits on the tongue like a wet sponge."
To this family: "Don't spend ¥60,000 a head to be photographed with a famous chef. Book one great sushi counter for the four of you who'll savour it, take the parents to a yakitori counter where the set menu is a bargain, and let the pregnant diner have cooked anago, tamago and grilled skewers. At the best places those are the dishes that tell you most about the cook."

### 5. Japan tips (his)
- **Queue early or go off-peak:** "We arrived early one November morning, before the day-trippers descended" (Daigo-ji); he queued three hours at Rurikō-in, which only opens seasonally ([autumn post](https://www.cityfoodsters.com/blog/autumn-foliage-japan/)).
- **Stay overnight for destination kaiseki:** "Miyamasou is best experienced not as a day visitor but as a guest staying overnight" (same post). His ryokan guide favours private-onsen rooms and in-room kaiseki ([ryokan post](https://www.cityfoodsters.com/blog/top-ryokans-japan/)).
- **Regulars-only booking is real:** most of his top Tokyo sushi (Saito, Sugita) is reachable mainly through repeat visits or connections. Wells makes the same point about Saito. His pattern is to return many times.
