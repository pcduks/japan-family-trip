# Food research brief (shared by every research agent)

## The trip
- Six people, 20 Dec 2026 – 9 Jan 2027. Tokyo in and out (Tokyo is certain: ~20–24 Dec and ~5–8 Jan).
  Other bases depend on the family's vote. The candidate regions are Kansai (Kyoto/Osaka/Kobe/Nara),
  the Alps and Hokuriku (Kanazawa, Takayama, Matsumoto, Nagano, Toyama), Fuji/Hakone, Setouchi (Hiroshima,
  Miyajima, Onomichi, Okayama, Takamatsu), Kyushu (Fukuoka, Beppu, Yufuin, Kumamoto, Nagasaki) and Tōhoku (Sendai, Yamagata, Zaō).
- Who: Pedro (planner, Brazilian, the serious foodie), his wife (29–32 weeks pregnant), his brother and
  sister-in-law (30s), his parents (older, very fit). Portuguese speakers. Budget ≈ S$15k per couple for 20 nights excluding flights, so food can be generous but not ¥60k-a-head every night.
- Pregnancy food rules: avoid raw egg, rare meat, raw shellfish (oysters, raw scallop, uni is a judgment call), cold-smoked salmon, unpasteurised soft cheese; limit bluefin/swordfish. Cooked versions are fine. Japanese OBs don't ban fresh sushi. Flag the dishes she can't have and say what she can order instead.
- Groups of 6 are hard at 8-seat counters. Always say whether 6 fits, or whether to split 3+3 or 2+2+2, or whether to book the whole counter.
- Calendar: the functional shutdown runs 29 Dec – 3 Jan (many restaurants close ~29/30 Dec – 3/4 Jan; markets are packed 28–30 Dec). 1 Jan is dead. Christmas Eve (24 Dec) is a couples' date night in Japan, so restaurants fill. Always note New Year closures when you can find them.

## The user's rules for sources (most important)
- NO Google-reviews-led, Western/tourist-led recommendations. Do not use Google Maps star ratings, TripAdvisor, or "top 10 for tourists" blogs as evidence.
- Every restaurant must be validated on **Tabelog** (tabelog.com): give the Tabelog score, the review count when you can, the Tabelog URL, and any Tabelog Award / 百名店 (Hyakumeiten) status with the year. The Tabelog score is the primary validation; as a rule of thumb, 3.50+ is excellent, 3.70+ is elite, and 4.0+ is rare. Ramen, soba and udon run lower, so 3.6+ is very strong there.
- Pair it with Japanese expert sources: Tabelog Award / 百名店 lists, Japanese food writers and critics, dancyu magazine, the Japanese-language Michelin, the TRY ramen awards, ラーメンデータベース, respected Japanese bloggers and Instagram/YouTube food accounts, and local Japanese media. Mark the Japanese-language source and summarise what it says.
- Where one of the user's trusted foodies has been somewhere, say so with a link to the evidence (a post, article, video or newsletter). The trusted list: @laerciomunechika, @ocritico.antigourmet, @eduardo.takemi, @hitode47, @little_meg_siu_meg, @migrationology (Mark Wiens), @pete_wells, @jwang815, @we.are.hungry.tourists, @fuchsiadunlop, @sucrilhos, @kenjilopezalt.
- Instagram itself is not reachable from here (it needs a login). Use web search for public traces: articles, interviews, YouTube, blogs, newsletters, Threads/X mirrors, and Google-indexed Instagram captions.
- Never invent a score, URL, price, or opening hour. If you couldn't verify it, say "unverified". Fetch the Tabelog page for each place (detail pages like https://tabelog.com/en/tokyo/A1301/A130101/13002251/ load fine; Japanese pages at https://tabelog.com/tokyo/... too; award pages at https://award.tabelog.com/hyakumeiten/... load).
