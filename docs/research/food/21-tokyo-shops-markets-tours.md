## 1. Depachika (department-store food halls)

**What the Japanese sources actually say.** No Japanese critic has published a ranking of Tokyo depachika that we could find. The Japanese material is three kinds of evidence:
- **(a) sales.** In the 2024-fiscal-year figures, Isetan Shinjuku is Japan's No. 1 department store, Nihonbashi Mitsukoshi No. 5 and Nihonbashi Takashimaya S.C. No. 6. These are whole-store figures, not food halls. https://saitoshika-west.com/blog-entry-9601.html
- **(b) reader polls.** In ITmedia's 2022 poll "デパ地下が充実していると思う関東の百貨店", Seibu Ikebukuro was No. 1, with Nihonbashi Mitsukoshi and Isetan Shinjuku also near the top. https://nlab.itmedia.co.jp/research/articles/780841/
- **(c) editorial descriptions.** Mynavi (Apr 2026) and the ITmedia piece describe Isetan as the "食文化のトレンド発信地" with the widest fresh produce and rare ingredients, "supported by professional cooks". Mitsukoshi Nihonbashi is described as the most even all-rounder. https://news.mynavi.jp/article/20260424-4240736/

## 2. Markets

### Tsukiji Outer Market (築地場外市場)
- **Days:** see the calendar spine. Shops that follow the wholesale calendar close on **23 Dec and 31 Dec – 4 Jan**, and open on **Sun 27 Dec**. From `02-closures.md`: 28–30 Dec are the busiest days of the year, so go 06:00–08:00. On 31 Dec most shops close by about 14:00–15:00, and on 1–3 Jan almost everything is shut. The market association's own note: "場外市場でも閉まっている店があります" (some outer-market shops close even on open days). https://www.tsukiji.or.jp/calendar/
- **Best stalls by Japanese sources:**

### 伏高 Fushitaka (katsuobushi, kombu, niboshi)
- Tsukiji 6-27-2 · dashi specialist · from a few hundred yen per bag; prices not listed
- Tabelog: not listed (retail)
- Japanese backing: the official market directory describes it as a dashi specialist "serving professional kitchens from ryōtei to izakaya and ramen shops". https://www.tsukiji.or.jp/shoplist/cat-e/cat-16/564/
- Hours: 06:30–14:30. Closed Sundays, holidays and **market holidays** (so 23 Dec and 31 Dec – 4 Jan). Tel 03-3541-0918.
- Why it's here: this is where a cook buys kezuri-bushi graded by use. A better buy than the tourist-packaged versions sold three streets away.

### きつねや Kitsuneya (horumon-don)
- Tsukiji outer market · offal stewed in Hatchō miso since 1947 · about ¥900–1,000 (reviews report ¥870 in 2021; current price unverified)
- Tabelog: **3.49** (2,744) · https://tabelog.com/en/tokyo/A1313/A131301/13007656/ · Awards: none
- Reservations: queue only, about five counter seats plus standing tables. **Group of 6: stand and eat in shifts.**
- Pregnancy: fine. It is long-simmered.
- Dec–Jan: Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri, Sat 06:30–13:00; closed Wed and Sun. New Year dates unverified (likely follows the market).
- Why it's here: the one Tsukiji dish that owes nothing to tourists. Old Tsukiji workers ate it before Instagram existed.

### 井上 Inoue (Tsukiji chūka soba): **currently suspended**
- Tabelog **3.54** (738), listed as **営業休止中** (suspended). https://tabelog.com/tokyo/A1313/A131301/13002337/ Do not plan around it.

### 杉本刃物 Sugimoto (knives): see §3.

- **Not verified this session:** 丸武 Marutake and 山長 (tamagoyaki), 鳥藤 (chicken), and Aritsugu's hours. Tamagoyaki is cooked, so it is fine in pregnancy.
- **Pregnancy at Tsukiji:** skip the **raw-oyster and raw-scallop stalls** and uni-on-a-stick. Grilled scallops and oysters are fine only when cooked through. Tuna sashimi is a "limit bluefin" item (Japanese OBs allow some). Kaisendon is the risky order. Tamagoyaki, horumon, grilled fish and kushiyaki are the safe ones.

### Toyosu Market (豊洲市場) and the first auction
- **Official 2027 fish calendar:** closed 1–4 Jan, **初市 Tue 5 Jan**, open 6–9 Jan.
- **Deck lottery:** monthly, free, 100 people a day, **max 5 per application** (so the family needs two applications), meeting point 3F PR corner at 05:30, viewing 05:45–06:25. The application window opens early in the previous month. Applicants must be junior-high age or older, and all members need ID. **No applications accepted for 25 Dec – 5 Jan**, so the first auction cannot be seen from the deck. https://toyosu.jcdlotterysite.jp/ and the TMG FAQ above.
- **What works on 5 Jan:** the **2F visitor corridor** (glass, looking down on the tuna floor) needs no booking and opens at 05:00 on market days (FAQ Q21). Whether it is crowd-limited on 初セリ day is **unverified**, so arrive by about 04:45. Press crowds are certain.
- **Better plan:** do the **lower deck on 6, 7 or 8 Jan** (an ordinary auction, same view). That needs the January lottery, which should open in **early December** (the November round closed 14 Oct, so expect about 2–8 Dec). Set an alarm.
- Pregnancy: the 05:00 start, long standing and cold halls are the issues, not the food. She can stay at the hotel and join the group for a cooked market breakfast later.

### Ameyoko (アメ横), year-end market 28–31 Dec
- About 400 shops. **30 Dec is the peak.** Least crowded from first trains to about 09:00; worst 11:00–16:00. On 31 Dec it is busy by day and thins toward evening; shops close about 17:00–18:00. Sources: banzokubiology.com/ameyoko-konzatsu/ and `02-closures.md`.
- **Critic's view:** go for the spectacle of hawkers auctioning crab, tarako, kazunoko and kuromame. It is not where Japanese cooks buy the good stuff (that is Tsukiji pros' shops or depachika). Bargaining is part of it. Watch for crab sold "for osechi" that is frozen imported stock. **With a 30-week pregnant walker, this is a hard crowd. 31 Dec late afternoon is the gentler slot.**
- Group of 6: keep together, with a meeting point at Ueno Station's Shinobazu exit.

### Sunamachi Ginza shōtengai (砂町銀座商店街, Kōtō-ku)
- A local shopping street, roughly 10:00–19:00 (GoTokyo: https://www.gotokyo.org/jp/spot/552/index.html). It is the "Japanese families buying osechi ingredients" experience that Ameyoko no longer is. Tokyo Shimbun: "年末に向けておせち用の食材も、この商店街ですべてまかなえる" (you can buy everything for osechi here). https://www.tokyo-np.co.jp/article/250256
- Stalls named by Japanese sources: **増英蒲鉾店** (oden ingredients since 1970, constant queues; in winter "it sells out as fast as it's made"; odendane.com/masuei-togo/) and **魚勝** (two shops, both with long queues).
- **Access warning:** about 20 minutes on foot from the nearest station (Minami-Sunamachi or Nishi-Ōjima). Take a taxi or bus. Year-end hours are unverified.
- Best day: **28 or 29 Dec**, in the morning. Pregnancy: oden is all cooked, so fine.

## 3. Kitchen and knife shops

**Ground truth from a Japanese pro shop.** Kama-Asa staff told Hot Pepper's メシ通 that Kappabashi customers are "mostly professionals", that carbon steel (鋼) is mainly for single-bevel deba and yanagiba, and that for a home first knife the answer is a santoku or a **gyuto of 18–21 cm**. https://www.hotpepper.jp/mesitsu/entry/yuuki-matsumoto/2022-00765

**New Year rule:** most Kappabashi shops close about 29/30 Dec – 4/5 Jan, and many close Sundays (`02-closures.md`). **Go on Mon 21 or Tue 22 Dec, or 5–8 Jan.**

### 釜浅商店 Kama-Asa Shoten
- Kappabashi (Matsugaya 2) · founded 1908 · about 80 knife types and roughly 1,000 pieces, plus Nanbu ironware. Sold **without makers' stamps**, a deliberate choice (Time Out Tokyo JP, 2020).
- **Prices read from its own online store, 18 Oct 2026** (tax-inclusive ¥, smallest to largest size):
  - V金1号 gyuto: 16,720 / 17,600 / 23,540 / 26,180
  - amane gyuto: 22,440–32,340
  - V10 wa-gyuto: 28,600–40,260
  - 白紙二号 deba: 22,869–33,330
  - 白紙二号 yanagiba: 26,180–39,160
  - 霞白紙二号 yanagiba: 17,820–23,540
  - 青紙スーパー wa-santoku: 20,570
  - 銀鍛 wa-gyuto: 63,360–71,390
  - Source: https://kama-asa.co.jp/products.json
- Hours and New Year closure: unverified (the site rate-limited us).
- Why it's here: the shop that made Kappabashi legible to non-Japanese buyers without dumbing it down. The name engraving and the sharpening advice are the real value.

### かまた刃研社 Kamata Hakensha
- Matsugaya 2-12-6 · founded 1923 as a **sharpening** house, now four generations · 800+ knives in stock · 10:00–18:00, irregular holidays. https://www.kap-kam.com/
- Why it's here: it began as a sharpener, which is what a Japanese chef cares about most. Engraving is done on the spot.

### つば屋包丁店 Tsubaya
- Nishi-Asakusa 3-7-2 · founded 1956 · 1,000+ knives you can handle uncased, including soba-kiri knives. Morning sharpening drop-off comes back the same day (Time Out JP). Online sales are currently suspended. https://www.tsubaya.co.jp/

### 日本橋木屋 Kiya, 本店 (COREDO Muromachi)
- Nihonbashi Muromachi 2-2-1 · about 1792 · **open every day except 元日 (1 Jan)** and COREDO inspection days, weekdays 11:00–19:00. https://www.kiya-hamono.co.jp/shop/kiya_honten.html
- Its 團十郎 nakiri is a Chūō-ku recommended souvenir. Resharpening is done on site. **The one serious knife shop open 2–3 Jan.**

### 杉本刃物 Sugimoto (Tsukiji 4-10-2)
- Closed Sundays, holidays and **Tsukiji market holidays** (so 23 Dec and 31 Dec – 4 Jan). https://www.sugimoto-hamono.com/
- Japanese chefs know it for Chinese cleavers. That reputation comes from the editor's prior knowledge and is **unverified this session.**

### 有次 Aritsugu (Tsukiji 4-13-6) and 正本総本店 Masamoto Sohonten (Higashi-Komagata, since 1866)
- A Yahoo!知恵袋 answer, a weak source, says "for a Japanese knife, Masamoto Sohonten or Aritsugu is the safe choice". Masamoto's own site confirms 1866 and the founder 松沢巳之助. Hours, prices and New Year dates are **unverified**.
- Pros' consensus (editorial view, needs verification): Masamoto is the sushi-counter brand, Aritsugu the Kyoto-lineage hand-forged one. Both cost more than Kappabashi for comparable steel.

### Not verified this session
- Union Commerce (its site's certificate failed), Nenohi, Jikkō (Kappabashi branch; a Sakai maker's own shop, 100+ high-end knives, https://www.jikko.jp/c/shop/kappa), and the ceramics shops (e.g. Dengama, Kappabashi Maeda; Time Out JP lists かっぱ橋 まえ田).

### Tourist vs serious
- **Red flags:** Damascus-pattern "VG10 Damascus" stacks at duty-free prices with no sharpener on site, shops pushing ¥80k+ "hand-forged" knives without naming the smith or village (Sakai, Seki, Echizen, Tosa, Sanjō), and staff who cannot tell you the hardness or the core steel.
- **What a Japanese cook buys:** one gyuto (V金1号 or 白紙二号) at ¥17k–30k, a whetstone (#1000/#3000, about ¥3k+ per Hot Pepper), and later a deba or yanagiba.
- **Carry rule:** knives go in checked luggage only.

### はせがわ酒店 Hasegawa Saketen (sake)

### 日本の酒情報館 Japan Sake and Shochu Information Center (Toranomon)

- Fits 21–24 Dec or 5–8 Jan. Tel 03-3519-2091.
- Why it's here: it is the cheapest, most neutral tasting in Japan, with no sales pitch.

**Plan:** an early lunch (10:30–11:00) at Kanda Yabu or Matsuya on 31 Dec, then wagashi at Takemura next door if open. Or take reserved soba home as takeaway. The queue-free alternative is a hotel soba plan (Palace Hotel, 31 Dec 21:30–22:30 for guests; `01-events.md`).

### Hatsumōde food stalls (屋台)
- Sensō-ji and Meiji Jingū have the big yatai rows on 1–3 Jan (`01-events.md`). What to eat: amazake, yaki-mochi, ikayaki, taiyaki, yakisoba.
- **Pregnancy: shrine amazake is often 酒粕 (sake-lees) based and contains alcohol. Ask "麹の甘酒ですか?" (is it koji amazake?). She can have the koji kind.** Avoid lukewarm stall food that has been sitting out. Hot, freshly cooked items are fine.

### Osechi, ozōni and kagami-mochi
- **Ozōni, Tokyo style:** clear dashi with grilled square mochi, chicken, komatsuna, kamaboko and mitsuba. For the parents and the pregnant wife, cut the mochi small and chew well.
- **Osechi components and pregnancy:**
  - fine: kuromame, datemaki, kurikinton, kōhaku namasu, nishime, tazukuri, kobumaki, kazunoko (salted roe)
  - be careful: **smoked-salmon rolls (cold-smoked, so avoid)** and **ikura** (a judgment call; many Japanese OBs allow small amounts). Raw shellfish in luxury boxes, such as abalone sashimi, should be avoided.
- **Kagami-mochi:** goes up around 28 Dec (by custom, not on the 29th, 苦 "suffering", or the 31st, 一夜飾り "one-night decoration"). It is broken and eaten at **kagami-biraki on 11 Jan, after the trip**. Small packaged kagami-mochi from a supermarket or depachika make a good souvenir.

### What is open 1–3 Jan for food (Tokyo)
- **1 Jan:** konbini, chains and hotel restaurants. **Hasegawa Saketen GranSta** was open 1 Jan 08:00–20:00 in the 2025/26 pattern. Owariya is closed. Depachika are closed. Kiya and the COREDO Muromachi shops are closed on 1 Jan only.
- **2 Jan:** Mitsukoshi, Isetan, Daimaru, Tokyu and Seibu depachika open (初売り). COREDO opens (Kiya; Ninben likely).
- **3 Jan:** + Takashimaya, Matsuya and GINZA SIX.
- **4 Jan:** Kanda Yabu normal (2025/26 pattern). Many soba and wagashi shops reopen. Tsukiji and Toyosu are still closed until **5 Jan**.
