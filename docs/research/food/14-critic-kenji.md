# Critic deep-dive: @kenjilopezalt (J. Kenji López-Alt)

Research date: 18 Oct 2026. This file follows `CRITIC-DIVE-BRIEF.md` and builds on the Kenji section of `11-critics-international.md` (identity, handle check and the first "Kenji Eats Japan" list). Read that section first. This file adds the following:
- His **earlier Japan trip** (late Dec 2011 to Jan 2012, with his wife and mother, over New Year in Kyoto and Tokyo). He wrote it up on Serious Eats, and the posts were recovered from the Wayback Machine.
- The visible text of his April 2026 Patreon dispatches ("Checking in from Japan!", "After Midnight in Japan").
- His views on technique (tempura, katsu, tonkotsu ramen, okonomiyaki, tofu) and his knife and gear advice.
- A Tabelog check of every named Japan restaurant.

**Method and limits (read first).**
- **YouTube:** I took video titles, dates and descriptions from his channel search pages and YouTube's public `next` endpoint. Watch pages and transcripts were blocked ("sign in to confirm you're not a bot"), so **I could not read what he says on camera**. Any place named only in the video itself (Tokyo ramen, yakitori, tempura, the depachika, the milk bar, the onsen) is still unknown.
- **Patreon ("Frienjis", frienji.kenjilopezalt.com):** most Japan posts are paywalled. I could read only the free opening paragraphs, and those were read through a summarising fetch tool. Quotes from Patreon below are the sentences that tool returned verbatim.
- **Serious Eats:** the site blocks automated fetches. I read his 2012–2017 articles from Wayback Machine snapshots and checked the byline on each one (several "Snapshots from Japan" pieces from 2012–2014 turned out to be by Jay Friedman, Brian Oh, Scott Haas or Sam Bresnick, **not** Kenji, so I left them out).
- **Instagram:** not used (login wall), per the brief.
- **NYT Cooking:** not reachable.
- **Kappabashi:** I searched and found **no** Kenji recommendation for Kappabashi or any Tokyo knife shop. The knife-buying advice in §5 comes from his general knife writing and his 2026 Tsubame-Sanjō trip, not from a Kappabashi post.

## 2. His palate in his own words

1. **Specialists beat generalists in Japan.** "Chefs, and indeed restaurants, specialize in one cooking method, and one cooking method only … if you enter a tempura restaurant, you're pretty likely to get some pretty darn stellar tempura." He compares the American "something for everyone" Japanese menu to a Cheesecake Factory. ([Tsunahachi, Serious Eats, 23 Feb 2012](https://web.archive.org/web/2016/http://www.seriouseats.com/2012/02/a-ten-course-tempura-only-meal-at-tsunahachi-tokyo-japan.html))
2. **Tempura is judged on colour and lace.** "Good tempura should be pale blond with an extraordinarily lacy, light, and crisp coating." At the best houses "all of your courses will be cooked by a single tempura chef who spent years in apprenticeship". ([Food Lab tempura, 2017](https://web.archive.org/web/2019/https://www.seriouseats.com/2017/09/the-food-lab-japanese-style-tempura-batter-how-to.html))
3. **Ingredient quality beats cleverness.** "The greatness of tofu in the shops near Kyoto is not in any fancy acts of cookery or wild seasoning. It's in the quality of the tofu itself." Also: "anybody who claims they don't like tofu simply hasn't ever had good tofu." ([Yudofu, 19 Mar 2012](https://web.archive.org/web/2016/http://www.seriouseats.com/2012/03/yudofu-hot-water-tofu-japanese.html))
4. **Everyday food is held to a high bar, without snobbery.** "even the most basic bowl of ramen in Japan is delicious". He called a Gion ramen bowl "workmanlike", served "with no glitz or glamour". ([After Midnight in Japan, Patreon, 27 Apr 2026](https://frienji.kenjilopezalt.com/posts/after-midnight-156685828))
5. **Tonkotsu is his ramen king.** "the bowl with the most soul is tonkotsu ramen … an intensely porky, opaque pale broth with a sticky-lipped intensity and the rich, buttery texture of light cream." He says he has eaten ramen "throughout different regions of Japan". ([Tonkotsu, Serious Eats, Feb 2012](https://web.archive.org/web/2016/http://www.seriouseats.com/2012/02/how-to-make-tonkotsu-ramen-broth-at-home-recipe.html))
6. **Katsu rules and loyalty to the classic brand.** "katsu must be made with panko … And second, it must be served with katsu sauce … to me katsu sauce is sort of like ketchup: The homemade version will never beat Heinz. Except, in the case of katsu sauce, the brand is Bull-Dog." He wants fatty cutlets so they stay juicy. ([Katsu, Serious Eats, Apr 2017](https://web.archive.org/web/2019/https://www.seriouseats.com/2017/04/how-to-make-tonkatsu-japanese-chicken-cutlet-katsu.html)) In the video he serves it with "very turgid cabbage" ([YouTube, 11 Dec 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9Dg7kSKrSnk)).
7. **He travels for technique.** "One of my favorite things about traveling isn't bringing recipes home—it's bringing techniques and ideas home." On the Kyoto izakaya stew: "comforting, deeply savory, and exactly the sort of thing I wanted to eat with a cold beer." ([Patreon, 27 Jul 2026](https://frienji.kenjilopezalt.com/posts/izakaya-inspired-164577412))
8. **Pro-MSG, anti-hang-ups.** "Like most Japanese people, I had no hang-ups about eating MSG then, and still don't now." ([Tamago kake gohan, 2016](https://web.archive.org/web/2019/https://www.seriouseats.com/2016/04/tamago-kake-gohan-japanese-egg-rice-tkg.html))
9. **Texture first.** He wants okonomiyaki "firm on the outside, but creamy and almost custard-like in the center", like a tortilla española ([okonomiyaki, 2016](https://web.archive.org/web/2019/https://www.seriouseats.com/2016/05/okonomiyaki-japanese-pancake-cabbage-soul-food-osaka-comfort.html)). On onigiri packaging: "It's frickin' genius!" ([2012](https://web.archive.org/web/2016/http://www.seriouseats.com/2012/02/coolest-part-about-japanese-rice-balls-onigiri-the-packaging.html))
10. **Honest about misses.** He found a bacon-wrapped rice ball at a Kyoto fair "tasted mostly of greasy rice", and Japanese corn "a little mealy" despite "hands down the best produce I've had anywhere in the world" ([Kyoto street fair, Feb 2012](https://web.archive.org/web/2016/http://www.seriouseats.com/2012/02/snapshots-from-japan-street-fair-food-in-kyoto.html), slideshow captions).

## 5. Japan tips, in his words (and his gear advice)

**Eating and etiquette**
- "it's frowned upon to eat while walking … Even at street fairs … you're expected to purchase your food and consume it while standing or sitting near the stand where you bought it." (2012; still good manners, although he overstates it a little for today's Tokyo.) [Source](https://web.archive.org/web/2016/http://www.seriouseats.com/2012/02/snapshots-from-japan-street-fair-food-in-kyoto.html)
- New Year in Kyoto means yatai: "street fairs—of which there are many in the days surrounding New Year's in the temple-rich city of Kyoto". **Directly useful for 29 Dec – 3 Jan, when restaurants close.**
- At takoyaki stalls: "Don't make the mistake of popping one in your mouth too quickly."
- Tempura counters: "you have a choice between three to four menus, each one representing a procession of individual fried foods". Use salt for some pieces and tentsuyu with grated daikon for others (he preferred the dip for kisu). [Tsunahachi](https://web.archive.org/web/2016/http://www.seriouseats.com/2012/02/a-ten-course-tempura-only-meal-at-tsunahachi-tokyo-japan.html)
- Konbini: "multiple convenience stores on every block, featuring a variety of freshly-made foods that are delivered multiple times a day". Go to 7-Eleven for egg sando and onigiri, FamilyMart for Fami-Chiki, Lawson for sweets (Patreon intros, Aug 2026).
- Depachika: a "world-class culinary experience"; ask a local chef which one (his came from Taichi Kitamura).
- Ekiben: buy at Tokyo Station before the shinkansen ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N0Lz1H1-ovs)).
- Late night: Japan is "one of the most interesting and delicious places to wander at night". Ramen-ya, konbini and karaoke after midnight ([Patreon](https://frienji.kenjilopezalt.com/posts/after-midnight-156685828)).
- Asakusa: see Sensō-ji once and get the hot senbei.

**What would make him judge a Japanese restaurant (technique checklist)**
- *Tempura:* one fry chef at one vat, clean oil, pale-blond lacy batter mixed with lumps left in, live or just-killed seafood, a fried-to-order procession, kakiage-don or tencha to finish.
- *Tonkatsu:* panko, Bull-Dog-style sauce, a fatty cut (sirloin or loin-blade) that stays juicy, a crisp crust that stays attached, and ice-crisp shredded cabbage.
- *Ramen:* broth body (gelatin and emulsified fat; tonkotsu "sticky-lipped"), the right noodle for the style, and a soft-yolk ajitama. He is happy with plain, honest bowls and lemon shio; hype isn't the point.
- *Tofu/yudofu:* the tofu itself (unpasteurised soy milk, custardy, clean). Seasoning should be minimal.
- *Okonomiyaki:* a crisp outside with a custardy middle (yamaimo, whipped batter). Cook-it-yourself teppan shops are part of the fun.
- *Karaage:* a soy-ginger-sake marinade, a starch dredge, and a double fry.

**Kitchen gear and knives (for Kappabashi or Tsubame-Sanjō shopping)**
- "I'd rather spend $75 on a high-quality chef's knife than get more knives of lesser quality." Skip knife sets. His most-used knife was "a Misono Santoku … a Japanese knife made with Swedish stainless steel". He likes carbon steel best (it takes a finer edge but needs more care), and he finds many knives "at flea markets and antique shops" ([Consumer Reports/Fox interview, 2013](https://www.foxnews.com/real-estate/talking-kitchen-knives-with-kenji-lopez-alt.amp)).
- Sharpening: "All you really need is a wet whetstone" ([short, 28 Aug 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VOP_9imYBpA)). He uses "a medium-grit and a fine-grit stone for most of my knives", hones often, and sharpens about once a year ([knife care, 2016](https://web.archive.org/web/2019/https://www.seriouseats.com/2016/05/ways-to-ruin-your-knives-knife-care-tips-safety.html)). So buy a ~1000 and a ~4000–6000 whetstone with the knife.
- His "never do" list: no glass or stone boards; no loose drawer storage; no sink or dishwasher; dry immediately; don't scrape with the edge (use a bench scraper); don't use it dull (same source). He prefers a wooden board ([Ask Kenji, Jan 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=021KahIfXfA)).
- San-mai (clad) steel: he explained "Why It Works for Kitchen Knives" after Tsubame-Sanjō ([short](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hAz6inJemg4)). A hard carbon core with softer cladding is what he'd look for in a Japanese gyuto or santoku.
