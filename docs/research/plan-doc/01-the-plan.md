## The New Year block: three options

You were right to question five nights in Takayama. The town fills one to two days ([japan-guide's Takayama itinerary](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e5912_takayama_full.html) is a single day). Its main museum, [Takayama Jinya](https://jinya.gifu.jp/en/guide/info/), also closes 29 Dec – 3 Jan. Five nights would have meant three slow days in a quiet town.

The nights now go to Kanazawa, which has more museums and gardens, and to an onsen town for New Year's Eve. Here are three ways to arrange 24 Dec – 1 Jan:

## Pace

The trip feels full because every day has a highlight. It feels unhurried because of these rules.

1. **One main thing a day, done in the morning.** That is when she has the most energy and the sights are quietest. Lunch is long, and the evening is food.
2. **A rest window from about 2 to 4pm.** Choose bases within a short taxi ride of the centre so this is easy. Anyone who wants to keep going can.
3. **At least two nights everywhere.** A one-night stop costs half a day packing and checking in.
4. **Moves are half days.** Travel in the morning, do one light thing in the afternoon, and plan nothing for the evening beyond dinner.
5. **Send the suitcases ahead.** Luggage forwarding (takkyubin) means moving days are done with a daypack. Allow two days instead of one over New Year.
6. **Split the group when it helps.** The early crew does dawn outings and harder walks. The others join for breakfast. Nobody waits for anyone.
7. **Plan taxis for six.** A normal taxi takes four people. Use two taxis, or book a jumbo taxi for station transfers with luggage.
8. **Keep the empty days.** The easy days are 20 Dec, 30–31 Dec and 8 Jan, and the five move days have light afternoons. Don't fill them in advance.

### Airbnb in Japan: what to check

- **The licence number.** Legal whole-home rentals show a registration or hotel licence number. If one is missing, don't book. Kyoto restricts private rentals heavily, so most machiya rentals there run under a hotel or ryokan licence, which is fine.
- **"Sleeps 6" often means futons on one floor.** For three couples, confirm three separate bedrooms, or book two units.
- **Beds, not futons, for her.** Getting up from a floor futon at 30+ weeks is hard. Ask for at least one room with real beds; at ryokan, ask for a Japanese-Western room.
- **Stairs and heating.** Machiya have steep stairs and can be cold in December. Put her on the ground floor and check there is heating in the bedrooms, not only a kotatsu.
- **Cleaning fees.** For 3+ nights with six people, a rental usually beats three hotel rooms. For one or two nights it rarely does.

Kyoto's accommodation tax went up on 1 March 2026. It is ¥400 per person per night for room prices of ¥6,000–¥19,999, and ¥1,000 for ¥20,000–¥49,999.

## Pregnancy at 29 to 32 weeks

The plan is shaped around her. These are the things to arrange or keep in mind.

- **Flights.** Most airlines want a doctor's letter after 28 weeks. Singapore Airlines, for example, needs one dated within 10 days of the first flight. Check your airline's rules for both flights, since she flies home at about 32 weeks.
- **Insurance.** Many policies exclude pregnancy complications or premature birth after a set week. Confirm yours covers both and includes medical evacuation.
- **A hospital at each base.** Save the nearest hospital with English help for each base, and a short note in Japanese on her due date and any conditions.
- **Food.** Eating out in Japan means plenty of raw food. Tell each ryokan when booking, and they will cook the raw courses. Cooked crab, beef, tempura, noodles and hot pot are all fine. Go easy on large tuna.
- **Onsen.** Japan's Environment Ministry removed pregnancy from the onsen warning list in 2014. Keep soaks short, avoid the hottest pools and watch for slippery floors. Private baths make all of this easier.
- **Snow and ice.** Shirakawa-go, Takayama and Kanazawa paths can be icy. Bring shoes with grip or slip-on ice cleats.
- **Trains.** Book reserved seats, ideally Green Car for legroom. Walk the aisle every hour and wear compression socks on the longer legs.
- **Floors.** Low tables and floor futons get harder each week. Ask for chairs and beds wherever you can (see Where to sleep).

Two specific things to skip or check with her doctor: [teamLab Planets](https://www.teamlab.art/e/planets/) restricts pregnant visitors in some areas, and the [Hakone Ropeway](https://www.hakoneropeway.co.jp/foreign/en/) crosses volcanic gas. The tabs flag stairs, ice and raw-food risks place by place.
