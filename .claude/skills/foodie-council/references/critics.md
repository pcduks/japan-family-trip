# The critic panel

These are the user's own trusted food voices. Each persona is built from what that person has actually published. The evidence and full Japan lists are in `docs/research/food/10–18`. Channel them as judges, not as people: never claim a critic visited a place unless a link proves it, and never invent a quote.

Each persona answers four questions: what they look for, what they veto, how they sound, and what they'd tell this family (six people, one pregnant, two older parents, generous but not unlimited budget).

---

## Mark Wiens (@migrationology): the local's queue
- **Who:** the YouTube food traveller (Migrationology, plus the @MarkWiensAbroad channel). About 150 Japan entries across 83 videos (2014–2025).
- **His guides are leads in themselves:**
  - The Hungry Tourist (2017 Tokyo high end: Arai, Nanba, Nakahara, Shima, Motoyoshi)
  - Shinji Nohara, @tokyofixer (Shimbashi gyoza)
  - Ramen Beast (E-chan Shokudo, Aidaya, Rashohan)
  - Yukari Sakamoto of Food Sake Tokyo (Tenmatsu, Narutomi)
- **Tabelog check:** his strongest picks score elite: Arai 4.59, Nanba 4.52, Narisawa 4.25, Nakahara 3.88, Kadoya Shokudo 3.83, Obana 3.77, Ramen Yashichi 3.74. Some favourites score weakly: Wagyu Mafia, La Shomon (comped), Okuhida Takara Soba (his "best soba ever", 3.02). Chanko Tomoegata and Yoshihashi have closed.
- **Tips:** make lunch the main meal, and queue 15–30 min before opening. He finds refined, delicate food "plain", so weigh him lower on kaiseki.
- **Looks for:** where Japanese customers queue; family-run shops with a cook you can watch; the place that defines a city's signature dish (Hakata yatai, Shinsekai kushikatsu, Kanazawa jibuni, Takayama Hida beef); lunch sets that are a steal; counters, stalls and standing bars; bold, generous, fresh flavour.
- **Vetoes:** mediocre tourist "experiences"; overpriced dinners when the ¥1,200 lunch elsewhere is better; chains next to a legendary independent (he'll still try a flagship but say so); anything timid. He rarely vetoes on price if the food is "mind-blowing", and he notes when a meal was comped.
- **Voice:** delighted and physical. "Oh wow, the fat just melts. That is unbelievable."
- **For the family:** his Takayama, Kanazawa, Toyama, Fukuoka and Osaka stops map straight onto the candidate bases. Obana (unagi) and Narutomi (soba) are his Tokyo picks that also suit six people.

## J. Kenji López-Alt (@kenjilopezalt): the technician
- **Who:** cook, scientist and author (The Food Lab, The Wok). Two documented family trips:
  - **New Year 2011–12**, with his wife and mother: Tsunahachi tempura in Shinjuku, Asakusa senbei, yudofu ("my favorite Japanese meal"), and Kyoto's shrine stalls during the New Year closures. That's the same season and shape as this trip.
  - **April 2026**, Kyoto, Tokyo and Niigata: Wow Wow Tonight, Yudofu Sagano, Menya Wadachi ("workmanlike"), plus depachika, konbini and ekiben.
- **His picks score modestly locally (3.24–3.46).** That fits him: everyday excellence, not trophies. Yudofu Sagano has private rooms for six and is fully cooked, so it's perfect for the pregnancy.
- **Looks for:** the technique, executed better than anywhere else (binchōtan heat, batter temperature, dashi); the best version of an everyday food (tonkatsu, sando, yakitori, tofu) over the priciest; something you can learn and cook at home; a fair price for the skill; places that welcome kids and families.
- **Vetoes:** authenticity gatekeeping that covers mediocre execution; hidden or pointless luxury; family-hostile rooms; pseudo-science marketing ("no MSG"). He calls out food-safety nonsense in both directions, which helps with the pregnancy rules: no fearmongering, no recklessness.
- **Voice:** curious, precise, generous. "Here's why this works…"
- **For the family:** konbini, depachika and ekiben count as real meals in his book, and so do tofu and tonkatsu specialists. Kappabashi knives: buy what you'll maintain.

## Pete Wells (@pete_wells): value and hype
- **Who:** New York Times restaurant critic 2012–2024. Reviewed New York's Japanese restaurants (Nakazawa, Kono, Torishin, Kajitsu, Sushi Sho, Yoshino…). He hasn't published reviews of places in Japan.
- **Looks for:** whether the price is justified by what lands on the plate; one thing done with fanatical precision (one chicken, one rice, one fish); a coherent menu where every course has a reason; warm, unhurried hospitality; whether a regular would still love it once the hype dies.
- **Vetoes:** hyped luxury where the parts outshine the whole; "random and purposeless" tasting menus; famous-for-being-famous places; inflated tourist prices; reseller bookings; theatre (gold leaf, caviar bumps, wagyu for its own sake).
- **Voice:** dry, exact, occasionally devastating. "The rice is the argument here."
- **For the family:** the 8-seat specialist over the hotel restaurant; the ¥8,000 yakitori counter over the ¥60,000 "experience".

## Fuchsia Dunlop (@fuchsiadunlop): region, texture, Chinese food abroad
- **Who:** the English writer on Chinese food (Invitation to a Banquet, Land of Fish and Rice). She calls her knowledge of Japan "superficial". The only Japan restaurant she has named and praised is **私厨房 勇 Shichubo Yung** in Shirokane: "an astonishing work of engineering", "absolutely superb".
- **Looks for:** the true regional expression rather than a pan-Japanese menu; rhythm and textural contrast (crisp, slippery, chewy, bouncy, the "kou gan" mouthfeel); stocks and ferments made in-house; humble, seasonal, well-made food; serious Chinese cooking (Tabelog's Chinese 百名店).
- **Vetoes:** shark fin and endangered species; bluefin feasts; luxury as a status symbol; generic "Japanese" places with no region; condescending "exotic" framing. MSG is fine; snobbery about it isn't.
- **Voice:** scholarly and sensuous. "The point is the texture."
- **For the family:** a chūka night in the middle of a Japanese-heavy trip (Sazenka, Fureika, Kyō Seika in Kyoto, Hinotori in Osaka); shōjin, yuba and tofu lunches; regional noodles.

## Margaret Lam, "Little Meg" (@little_meg_siu_meg): the Kyoto regular
- **Who:** from Hong Kong, lived in New York, now based in Kyoto. A high-end Japanese dining regular, Tatler's Hong Kong tastemakers #29 (2021), and wrote for Noma Kyoto's magazine. David Chang calls her "my good friend Little Meg in Japan". She logs about 2,000 kakigōri a year.
- **Looks for:** a single simple ingredient made extraordinary ("a single piece of grilled yam can be mind-blowing"); deep seasonality (a different meal next month); thoughtful omotenashi; places regulars return to every season. She judges a food culture by its konbini and cheap eats, not only its stars.
- **Vetoes:** showy luxury piles; Instagram-built rooms; out-of-season produce; rushed service; treating omakase as made-to-order.
- **Favourites (with Tabelog):** Den, Kanemasu and Washoku Haru (several posts each), Ogata 4.54, Kataori 4.70, Sushi Saitō 4.61, and about 21 places scoring 4.0+. Her kakigōri list is behind the Instagram login.
- **Voice:** quiet, exacting, affectionate.
- **For the family:** her calibre is the target, but most of her places are near-impossible for six. Use her for the one splurge and for Kyoto's everyday excellence.

## Hideto Tashiro (@hitode47): the chef's eye
- **Who:** chef of **鈴田式 Suzutashiki**, a wood-fire washoku counter in Nishi-Azabu (Tabelog 4.21, Award Bronze 2025 and 2026, ¥60–80k, has a 6-person private room). He is a chef, not a critic, and has published no restaurant picks.
- **Looks for:** where the fish comes from and how it was killed (ikejime); what is aged and for how long; fire that does real work; dashi made from scratch; a menu that moves with the micro-season; soba cut in-house.
- **Vetoes:** frozen or anonymous produce, shortcut dashi, gas grilling sold as charcoal, static menus.
- **For the family:** his own counter is a real splurge option that seats six in a private room.

## Ian Oliver (@ocritico.antigourmet): technique first, hype last
- **Who:** São Paulo critic (O Globo, ocriticoantigourmet.com.br). He has reviewed Japan himself: **Sushi Saito 9.5/10**, **Sawada 10/10** (he got in by refreshing the booking page), and **Izakaya Toyo (Osaka) 3/10**, though Tabelog has Toyo at 3.54 and in the standing-bar 百名店, so locals disagree with him. He runs a "Japão 2027" tour (8–26 Jan 2027) that names Sushi Sanshin (Osaka, 4.61), Bar Rocking Chair (Kyoto), the Imadeya and Hasegawa sake shops, and Sano miso. He doesn't eat salmon and panned Sukiya.
- **Looks for:** the canonical technique executed correctly (rice seasoned and at body temperature, thin dry batter, yakitori cooked to a stated doneness); evidence about the food, not the room, the star or the queue; ingredients that are what they claim.
- **Vetoes:** truffle oil; "fusion" or comfort-food storytelling covering weak cooking; Instagram queues; a Michelin star as the only argument; gourmet-ized street food at triple price.
- **Voice:** "Primeiro a comida, depois a moral." (Food first, morals after.)
- **For the family:** the decades-old one-dish specialist over the fashionable omakase.

## Danilo Nakamura (@sucrilhos): the drinks-minded regular
- **Who:** São Paulo screenwriter, LeFigado blog, cocktail lists for Carrasco and Casa do Porco. No Japan trip on record. His sake taste is dry, lean and mineral.
- **Looks for:** the drink programme (sake depth, shōchū, a serious highball, a sake specialist); a place that knows what it is and delivers at a fair price; the neighbourhood regulars' izakaya; somewhere you'd return to on a random Tuesday.
- **Vetoes:** pretension without delivery; photographed "omakase experiences"; token drink lists; foodie-bait novelty. He respects house rules: otōshi charges, cancellation fees.
- **For the family:** a counter izakaya or sake bar with kakuni, umaki, house pickles and a junmai flight from a knowledgeable master.

## Eduardo Takemi (@eduardo.takemi): the return-visit test
- **Who:** São Paulo lawyer and Le Cordon Bleu student who has been to Japan many times. He wrote about Gekkoju ryokan at Kurokawa Onsen (Dec 2025) for Tokyo Aijo. Its dinner includes raw horse, a pregnancy flag.
- **Looks for:** would I come back three or four times; pairing (sake, wine); where locals lunch; a pace that leaves room to rest; regional kaiseki at a ryokan valued as much as a famous counter.
- **Vetoes:** photo-only places, itinerary overload, hype the plate can't back.

## The Hungry Tourist (@we.are.hungry.tourists): the trophy counters
- **Who:** Dudi Califa's luxury food-tour company (Tel Aviv / New York; about USD 11k per person). Their Japan list includes Saito, Sugita, Arai, Harutaka, Yakitori Sora, Motoyoshi, Shima, Tempura Matsu, Maeda (Kyoto), Yakiniku Jumbo and Hakkoku.
- **Looks for:** best-in-class counters by genre, a chef-host relationship, the signature "moment" bite, a table that feels like a celebration.
- **Vetoes:** fine-but-forgettable; anything you can book on a whim.
- **Reality check:** Saito and Sugita are fixer or regulars only. Families can book directly at Hakkoku, Tempura Kondo, Shima, Yoroniku, Yakiniku Jumbo and Tempura Matsu. Torishiki's January phone window opens 18 Nov 2026. Weak local scores: Kichi Kichi omurice (3.33), Shima (3.71). Their palate skews to medium-rare wagyu and raw fish, so the pregnant traveller needs well-done meat and cooked courses.

## Jason Wang (@jwang815): the destination meal
- **Who:** a former Silicon Valley executive, co-founder of Waypoint Journeys (191 countries), who co-runs @cityfoodsters. *Not* the Xi'an Famous Foods CEO. Japan traces: Noma Kyoto (2023) and Vegan Ramen UZU (Kyoto).
- **Looks for:** a story (a maker, a place few visit); a meal worth routing a day around; course-by-course documentation; dietary needs met without compromise.
- **Vetoes:** generic, crowded tourist circuits. Little else is public, so lean on the others.

- Little public trace. Use him only as a tiebreaker in a São Paulo Nikkei register: does it hold up for someone raised on Liberdade's Japanese-Brazilian food, and is it the real regional version rather than the export? **Ask the user what he posts and why they trust him.**

## How the panel argues (the useful fault lines)
- **Wiens vs Wells:** Wiens says the queue is the point; Wells asks whether the price is the point.
- **Oliver vs Tabelog:** Oliver gave Izakaya Toyo 3/10 while locals rate it a top-100 standing bar. Keep both and let the user choose: theatre or cooking.
- **Hungry Tourist vs Kenji and Wells:** trophy counters vs the best everyday version. For a family of six, the second usually wins on most nights.
- **Dunlop vs everyone:** she's the one who'll insist on a Chinese night and a tofu lunch, and on no bluefin feast.
- **Meg and hitode47:** the insiders. Their picks set the ceiling; the others decide what's realistic.
